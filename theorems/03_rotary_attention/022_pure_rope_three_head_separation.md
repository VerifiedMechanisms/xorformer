# Pure RoPE Can Use Fewer Heads Than Vanilla Attention

> **Proof status.** The full separation theorem is Lean-checked without `sorry` or new axioms. The main theorem is `PureThreeReplicatedGrid.exists_complexity_separation` in [`PureThreeSeparation.lean`](../../formalization/HeadComplexity/Results/PureThreeSeparation.lean). Its axiom audit reports only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. The mathematical proof below has also passed three independent audits.

## Statement

For every positive integer $m$, the construction below defines a Boolean function $f_m$ on $18m$ bits and a pure RoPE representation using three heads. There is an integer $m_0$ such that, for every $m\geq m_0$,

$$ H^{\ast}_{\mathrm{pure\ RoPE}}(f_m)=3\qquad\text{and}\qquad4\leq H^{\ast}_{\mathrm{vanilla}}(f_m)\leq6. $$

The Lean proof establishes existence of $m_0$. The separate [effective estimate](../../artifacts/rope/research/effective_replication_threshold.md) proves that $m_0=10^{40000}$ suffices; that numerical strengthening is not Lean-formalized. The result refutes the proposed equality for every Boolean function. It concerns Boolean classifiers, not equality of their real scores.

In fact, there is an integer $N$ such that every input length $n\geq N$ admits a Boolean function with pure minimum three and vanilla minimum between four and six. This follows by adding unused input bits to any one separating function, as proved below.

## The explicit pure family

Index eighteen group coordinates by a block $b\in\lbrace1,2,3\rbrace$ and a position $j\in\lbrace1,\ldots,6\rbrace$. Each group has $m$ Boolean replicas $x_{bjr}$. Define its centered mean by

$$ q_{bj}(x)=\frac1m\sum_{r=1}^{m}(2x_{bjr}-1). $$

For each head $h\in\lbrace1,2,3\rbrace$, define the linear forms

$$ d_h(q)=\sum_{b=1}^{3}(q_{bh}-q_{b,h+3}),\qquad u_h(q)=2\sum_{b=1}^{3}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{6}q_{bj}+q_{bh}\right). $$

Write $d_{h,bj}$ and $u_{h,bj}$ for their coefficients. Thus $d_{h,bj}$ is $1$, $-1$, or $0$, and $u_{h,bj}$ is $4$ when $j=h$ and $2$ otherwise. Their coefficient sums are

$$ \sum_{b,j}d_{h,bj}=0,\qquad\sum_{b,j}u_{h,bj}=42. $$

Set

$$ D_h(q)=200+d_h(q),\qquad N_h(q)=u_h(q),\qquad S(q)=\sum_{h=1}^{3}\frac{N_h(q)}{D_h(q)},\qquad f_m(x)=\mathbf1\lbrace S(q(x))\gt0\rbrace. $$

Every $D_h$ lies in $[194,206]$ on the continuous cube $[-1,1]^{18}$. The pure atom parameters are

$$ a_{h,bjr}=\frac{2(u_{h,bj}-d_{h,bj})}{m},\qquad b_{h,bjr}=\frac{2u_{h,bj}}m,\qquad\gamma_h=116,\qquad\eta_h=-42,\qquad v_{h,0}=0,\qquad v_{h,1}=1. $$

All input masses and query denominator masses are strictly positive: the input masses are $4/m$, $6/m$, or $8/m$. The query numerator parameter may be negative in the unchanged pure model. Direct substitution gives

$$ \begin{aligned} \gamma_h+\sum_{b,j,r}\bigl(a_{h,bjr}(1-x_{bjr})+b_{h,bjr}x_{bjr}\bigr)&=D_h(q(x)),\cr \eta_h+\sum_{b,j,r}\bigl(v_{h,0}a_{h,bjr}(1-x_{bjr})+v_{h,1}b_{h,bjr}x_{bjr}\bigr)&=N_h(q(x)).\end{aligned} $$

Thus $f_m$ has three pure heads for every $m\geq1$. The all-positive and all-negative centered inputs give opposite strict labels, so the function is nonconstant.

Define the cleared polynomial

$$ P(q)=N_1(q)D_2(q)D_3(q)+N_2(q)D_1(q)D_3(q)+N_3(q)D_1(q)D_2(q). $$

The denominators are positive, so $P$ and $S$ have the same signs throughout the continuous cube. The homogeneous cubic part is

$$ P_3(q)=u_1(q)d_2(q)d_3(q)+u_2(q)d_1(q)d_3(q)+u_3(q)d_1(q)d_2(q). $$

## Three elementary structural facts

### Lemma 1. Each block supplies six independent linear coordinates

Restrict $d_1,d_2,d_3,u_1,u_2,u_3$ to any one block of six coordinates. These six linear forms are independent.

**Proof.** The common kernel of the three $d$ forms consists of vectors $(a,b,c,a,b,c)$. On this kernel, the three $u$ forms are

$$ 2(3a+2b+2c),\qquad2(2a+3b+2c),\qquad2(2a+2b+3c). $$

The coefficient matrix has eigenvalues $14$, $2$, and $2$, hence is invertible. Therefore the common kernel of all six forms is zero. In particular, the map from a block to the six coordinates $(d_1,d_2,d_3,u_1,u_2,u_3)$ is invertible. The common kernel of the three $d$ forms has dimension three and contains the all-ones vector. $\blacksquare$

### Lemma 2. The cleared polynomial is irreducible

The polynomial $P$ is irreducible over $\mathbb R$, has degree three, and has a smooth real zero hypersurface through the origin.

**Proof.** Lemma 1 lets us extend the six affine coordinates $(D_1,D_2,D_3,N_1,N_2,N_3)$ to an affine coordinate system on $\mathbb R^{18}$. In these coordinates, $P$ becomes

$$ W(y,z)=z_1y_2y_3+z_2y_1y_3+z_3y_1y_2. $$

View this polynomial as a polynomial in $z_3$ over the unique factorization domain generated by all other coordinates. Its coefficient $y_1y_2$ and its constant term $y_3(z_1y_2+z_2y_1)$ are relatively prime. Indeed, neither $y_1$ nor $y_2$ divides the constant term. The polynomial is primitive and linear over the fraction field, so Gauss's lemma proves irreducibility. Adding the twelve unused coordinates preserves irreducibility.

At the origin in the original coordinates, every $N_h$ is zero and every $D_h$ is $200$. The gradient is $200^2(u_1+u_2+u_3)$, which is nonzero. Thus $P$ changes sign across a smooth zero hypersurface inside the open cube. $\blacksquare$

### Lemma 3. A cubic tensor identifies the denominator kernel

For the homogeneous cubic in six coordinates

$$ W_3(d,u)=u_1d_2d_3+u_2d_1d_3+u_3d_1d_2, $$

the vectors at which its Hessian matrix has rank at most three are exactly those satisfying $d_1=d_2=d_3=0$.

**Proof.** In the coordinate order $(d_1,d_2,d_3,u_1,u_2,u_3)$, the Hessian has block form

$$ M(d,u)=\begin{pmatrix}A(u)&B(d)\cr B(d)&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad A(u)=\begin{pmatrix}0&u_3&u_2\cr u_3&0&u_1\cr u_2&u_1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad B(d)=\begin{pmatrix}0&d_3&d_2\cr d_3&0&d_1\cr d_2&d_1&0\end{pmatrix}. $$

If $d=0$, only the upper-left three-by-three block can be nonzero, so the rank is at most three. If $d_i\neq0$, select the two complementary $d$ coordinates and the matching two $u$ coordinates. The corresponding principal four-by-four minor has determinant $d_i^4\neq0$, so the rank is at least four. $\blacksquare$

The third derivative tensor of $P_3$, restricted to one direction in each of its three blocks, is obtained from the polarization of $W_3$ by invertible changes of coordinates in the three modes, by Lemma 1. Consequently, contracting that tensor against a vector in the first block gives a matrix of rank at most three exactly when the vector annihilates the three first-block denominator forms $d_h$.

## From infinitely many vanilla representations to a limiting polynomial

Suppose, for a contradiction, that $f_m$ has a vanilla representation with at most three heads for infinitely many positive integers $m$. Padding with zero heads gives exactly three. This infinite set of integers is unbounded; restrict to $m\geq3$.

The exact vanilla affine-fraction normal form gives a score

$$ c_m+\sum_{h=1}^{3}\frac{A_{m,h}(x)}{E_{m,h}(x)}, $$

where every numerator and denominator is affine, every denominator is positive on the Boolean cube, and the coefficients of each nonconstant denominator are either all strictly positive or all strictly negative. A constant denominator has all slopes zero.

Apply a sufficiently small downward shift to the free score constant. Because the input cube is finite, all labels are preserved and every score becomes nonzero. Absorb the shifted constant into the first numerator. Clearing the three positive denominators then gives a degree-at-most-three polynomial $R_m$ with strictly positive values where $f_m=1$ and strictly negative values where $f_m=0$.

Replace the Boolean variables by centered variables $z_{bjr}=2x_{bjr}-1$. Multilinearize $R_m$ using $z_{bjr}^2=1$, which preserves all cube values and does not increase the degree. Average the resulting polynomial over all permutations of replicas within every group. The classifier $f_m$ is invariant under these permutations, so the averaged polynomial retains the same strict signs.

A separately symmetric multilinear polynomial of total degree at most three is a polynomial of total degree at most three in the group means $q_{bj}$ on the Boolean cube. To see this directly, in a group of $m$ centered bits the first three elementary symmetric polynomials are

$$ e_1=mq,\qquad e_2=\frac{m^2q^2-m}{2},\qquad e_3=\frac{m^3q^3-(3m-2)mq}{6}. $$

Products of these expressions across groups preserve the total degree bound. Denote the resulting polynomial in eighteen real variables by $Q_m(q)$. It has the prescribed strict signs on the grid

$$ \mathcal G_m=\lbrace-1,-1+2/m,\ldots,1\rbrace^{18}. $$

The polynomial $Q_m$ is nonzero. Divide it by the maximum absolute value of its coefficients; this is a positive normalization and preserves its signs. The space of polynomials in eighteen variables of degree at most three is finite dimensional. Passing to a subsequence, the normalized coefficient vectors converge to a vector of maximum norm one. Let $Q$ be the corresponding nonzero polynomial. Convergence is uniform on the continuous cube.

For every point $q$ of the open cube with $P(q)\neq0$, choose grid points converging to $q$ along the subsequence. Eventually their $P$ values have the same sign as $P(q)$. The signs of $Q_m$ on those grid points agree with $P$, and uniform convergence gives

$$ P(q)\gt0\Longrightarrow Q(q)\geq0,\qquad P(q)\lt0\Longrightarrow Q(q)\leq0. $$

At every point of the smooth zero hypersurface of $P$ sufficiently near the origin, both signs occur arbitrarily nearby. Continuity therefore gives $Q=0$ on that whole hypersurface patch.

This implies $P$ divides $Q$. For completeness, use the affine coordinates from Lemma 2 and solve the equation $P=0$ near the origin for

$$ z_3=-\frac{y_3(z_1y_2+z_2y_1)}{y_1y_2}. $$

The denominator is nonzero near $y_1=y_2=200$. Substitute this expression into $Q$ and clear a sufficiently large power of $y_1y_2$. The resulting polynomial in the other seventeen coordinates vanishes on a nonempty open set, hence is identically zero. Division in the fraction field shows that $W$ divides the transformed $Q$ there. Since $W$ is primitive and irreducible, Gauss's lemma gives divisibility in the polynomial ring. Returning to the original coordinates gives $P\mid Q$.

Both polynomials have degree at most three, $P$ has degree exactly three, and $Q$ is nonzero. Therefore

$$ Q=\lambda P\qquad\text{for some }\lambda\gt0. $$

The strict positivity of $\lambda$ follows from the weak sign agreement at a point where $P\gt0$.

## The cubic coefficients that survive averaging

Separate the eighteen group coordinates into their three blocks of six. For each $Q_m$, retain the coefficients of monomials with one group coordinate from each block. These coefficients form a six-by-six-by-six tensor $T_m$. Coefficient convergence gives $T_m\to\lambda T$, where $T$ is the corresponding tensor of $P_3$.

These particular coefficients retain the exact numerator-denominator structure of the original vanilla representation. This fact does not require the full averaged polynomial to retain that structure.

To verify it, write the affine numerator and denominator slopes in centered replica coordinates. Let $a_{m,h,bj}$ and $e_{m,h,bj}$ be the sums of those slopes over the $m$ replicas of group $(b,j)$. A cubic monomial using three different groups always uses three different replica variables. Multilinearization therefore does not change its coefficient. Averaging replaces each replica variable by the corresponding group mean, and summing the coefficients factors into products of the aggregated slopes. Terms supported on fewer than three groups cannot contribute to these coefficients.

There is also a direct coefficient argument, used in Lean. On group-constant centered Boolean inputs, every within-group permutation fixes the input. The averaged polynomial and the polynomial obtained by collapsing every replica to its group variable therefore agree on the eighteen-dimensional centered Boolean cube. Centered Boolean reduction only deletes pairs of occurrences of a variable. For polynomials of degree at most three, it consequently preserves every squarefree coefficient of degree three. Multilinear polynomials are determined by their Boolean cube values, so those coefficients agree exactly for the two polynomials.

Consequently, up to the common positive coefficient normalization, $T_m$ is the polarization across the three blocks of

$$ \sum_{h=1}^{3}a_{m,h}(q)e_{m,j}(q)e_{m,k}(q),\qquad\lbrace j,k\rbrace=\lbrace1,2,3\rbrace\setminus\lbrace h\rbrace. $$

Here the displayed forms mean the aggregated slopes only; affine constants do not contribute to the homogeneous cubic. The coefficients $e_{m,h,bj}$ of each nonconstant denominator are all strictly positive or all strictly negative, since they are sums of slopes with that same strict sign.

Let $K_m$ be the common kernel of the three first-block denominator covectors. Its dimension is at least three. If $v\in K_m$, then contraction of $T_m$ against $v$ in the first block has matrix rank at most three: every term in which a denominator covector is evaluated at $v$ vanishes, and the remaining matrix has its row and column spaces contained in the spans of the three denominator covectors in the other blocks.

## The cone contradiction

For all sufficiently large indices of the convergent subsequence, $T_m$ is nonzero because its limit $\lambda T$ is nonzero. Hence at least one vanilla denominator is nonconstant. Its aggregated first-block coefficient vector is strictly positive or strictly negative. Change its sign if necessary and divide by the sum of its entries to obtain a vector $v_m$ satisfying

$$ v_m\geq0\quad\text{coordinatewise},\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{6}(v_m)_j=1,\qquad v_m\perp K_m. $$

Choose an orthonormal triple $w_{m,1},w_{m,2},w_{m,3}$ in $K_m$. The probability simplex and the space of orthonormal triples in $\mathbb R^6$ are compact. Pass to a further subsequence on which $v_m\to v$ and $w_{m,i}\to w_i$ for all three indices. The limiting vectors $w_1,w_2,w_3$ remain orthonormal. Also,

$$ v\geq0,\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{6}v_j=1,\qquad v\perp\mathrm{span}\lbrace w_1,w_2,w_3\rbrace. $$

For every $i$, contraction of $T_m$ against $w_{m,i}$ has rank at most three. All four-by-four minors vanish, and this property is closed under entrywise limits. Hence contraction of $\lambda T$ against $w_i$ has rank at most three. Lemma 3 and the invertible block coordinate changes imply

$$ w_i\in K:=\ker d_1\cap\ker d_2\cap\ker d_3 $$

inside the first block. The three limiting vectors are independent and $K$ has dimension three, so they span $K$. The all-ones vector belongs to $K$ by Lemma 1. Orthogonality therefore gives $\sum_jv_j=0$, contradicting $\sum_jv_j=1$.

This contradiction rules out infinitely many $m$ with a three-head vanilla representation. Thus all sufficiently large $m$ fail to have one.

## Consequences and architectural scope

Every $f_m$ has a three-head pure scalar representation by the explicit positive masses above. For all sufficiently large $m$, no vanilla representation with at most three heads exists. Theorem 16 already proves pure-to-vanilla conversion at zero, one, and two heads. Therefore the pure minimum is exactly three for these functions, while the vanilla minimum is at least four. The endpoint refinement of the finite-difference construction gives a vanilla upper bound of $2^H-2$ for pure families with $H\geq2$, hence an upper bound of six here.

The separation extends to every sufficiently large input length. Fix one separating function on $N$ bits and add any number of unused bits. To preserve its pure head count, first make its finite set of score signs strict by a constant shift. Give each new position equal positive masses $t$ for its two tokens in each head. As $t\to0$, the finitely many extended scores converge to the original strict scores, so a sufficiently small positive $t$ preserves every label. Conversely, fixing all added bits to zero in any vanilla representation absorbs their positive denominator masses and their numerator contributions into the query constants, preserving the head count exactly. Thus a vanilla representation with three heads of the extended classifier would contradict the original lower bound. The two-head equivalence and the six-head upper bound then give the same minimum-head bounds at every length at least $N$.

The pure representation is in the unchanged model: the query token is fixed, rotary maps act only on queries and keys, and values depend only on the token and are unrotated. The scalar-to-rotary realization from Theorem 18 realizes every displayed finite collection of positive masses with finite real parameters and common finite width $2(18m+2)$, using the standard frequencies. The Lean theorems `standardPureRotaryComputable_iff_scalar` and `freePureRotaryComputable_iff_scalar` give the square architecture equivalences. The corresponding rectangular architecture equivalences are `rectangularStandardPureRotaryComputable_iff_scalar` and `rectangularFreePureRotaryComputable_iff_scalar`. These quantify over all finite input lengths and head counts. Conversely, the vanilla affine-fraction normal form is the exact one for the original shared-embedding architecture. Thus the scalar separation transfers to both standard-frequency and free-frequency pure rotary architectures with unrestricted finite width.

The compactness proof above gives an existential finite-dimensional Boolean separation. The [effective estimate](../../artifacts/rope/research/effective_replication_threshold.md) replaces its limit argument with quantitative polynomial interpolation and an integer-minor bound, proving separation for every $m\geq10^{40000}$. It combines with the padding argument to give a separating function at every input length at least $18\cdot10^{40000}$. These constants are deliberately loose. Neither argument uses approximate real-score equivalence or infers a lower bound from a failed optimization search.
