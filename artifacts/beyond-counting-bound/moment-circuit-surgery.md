# Minimal Boolean Moment Circuits with Near-Maximal Contact

## Statement

For a finite set of velocity vectors $V\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ and an integer $k\geq0$, define its Boolean moment column at $v$ by

$$ C_k(v)=\bigl((v\cdot x)^j\bigr) _{x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n,\ 0\leq j\leq k}. $$

A circuit means that these columns have a one-dimensional space of linear relations, generated by a vector with every coordinate nonzero. Equivalently, the columns are dependent and every proper subfamily is independent.

**Theorem.** For all integers $n\geq3$ and $m\geq2n$, there is an explicit set of

$$ H=m+4n-7 $$

distinct positive integer velocity vectors with full affine span $\mathbb R^n$, and nonzero integer residues $c_v$, with all of the following properties:

1. The columns $C_{m-1}(v)$ form a circuit.

2. With $D(x)=1+\sum_i x_i$, the reciprocal score $R_x(\varepsilon)=\sum_v c_v/(D(x)+\varepsilon v\cdot x)$ has zero Taylor coefficients below degree $m$ on the entire cube, and a nonzero coefficient of degree $m$ at the anchor vertex.

3. The reciprocal families, as functions of both $x$ and $\varepsilon$, are linearly independent. In particular, there is no persistent subidentity.

4. Every denominator has strictly positive coherent slopes for every $\varepsilon\geq0$.

Thus full affine span, minimal dependence of truncated Boolean moments, and exclusion of persistent identities do not give a contact bound proportional to the head count divided by the input dimension. The contact can be the head count minus $4n-7$.

This is an exact construction within the admissible reciprocal-head family, not a lower bound on Boolean head complexity. Its labels after a harmless bias are constant, as quantified below.

## The local substitution

Use coordinates numbered from zero, with coordinate zero reserved as an anchor. Suppose a velocity $p$ lies in a space with an additional coordinate $a$, and choose a different nonanchor coordinate $b$. For a positive integer $T$, replace $p$ by

$$ \begin{aligned} A&=p+T(e_b+e_a),& B&=p+2Te_a,& C&=p+Te_b,\cr E&=p+T(-e_b+2e_a),& F&=p+T(-e_b+e_a). \end{aligned} $$

The replacement coefficients are $(-1,1,1,-1,1)$ in the order $A,B,C,E,F$. The six-point Boolean switch proves

$$ \frac1{D+\varepsilon p\cdot x}=-\frac1{D+\varepsilon A\cdot x}+\frac1{D+\varepsilon B\cdot x}+\frac1{D+\varepsilon C\cdot x}-\frac1{D+\varepsilon E\cdot x}+\frac1{D+\varepsilon F\cdot x}. $$

This holds for every Boolean $x$, wherever the displayed denominators are nonzero. It also holds with an arbitrary common base in place of $D$. One can verify the four choices of $(x_b,x_a)$ directly: the equal reciprocal arguments cancel in pairs. In particular, it is an identity of every Taylor moment. The five coefficients sum to one and their weighted sum of velocity vectors is $p$.

## Construction and maintained invariants

Begin in two coordinates with the line points

$$ v_h=(L+4h)(1,1),\quad 0\leq h\leq m,\qquad L=2,\qquad c_h=(-1)^{m-h}\binom mh. $$

The point $v_0$ is the initial cluster. The other $m$ points will remain on the common diagonal throughout the construction. After $r$ substitutions the cluster has $1+4r$ points, every cluster point has anchor coordinate $L$, and the ambient dimension is $r+2$. The cluster, with its inherited residues, exactly replaces the original head at $L\mathbf1$ in all reciprocal evaluations. The line point residues remain $c_1,\ldots,c_m$, and the cluster residues sum to $c_0$.

At each step choose any cluster point $p$. Always use coordinate $b=1$. Form the finite integer set

$$ Q=\lbrace L\rbrace\cup\lbrace v_1:v\text{ belongs to the cluster}\rbrace. $$

Choose

$$ T=1+\max_{q\in Q}|q-p_1|. $$

Then $p_1+T\notin Q$. Translate every current velocity by $(T+1)\mathbf1$, increasing $L$ by $T+1$. This preserves the condition $p_1+T\notin Q$, with both sides translated, and makes the later negative offset $-T$ harmless. Translation preserves all truncated-moment kernels: at each fixed input, the binomial expansion is an invertible triangular change among its moment rows.

Add a new coordinate $a$, setting it equal to the anchor coordinate at every old velocity. Then perform the local substitution at $p$, multiplying the five replacement coefficients by the former residue at $p$.

The five new points are distinct. The four with a positive offset in coordinate $a$ cannot equal any old point, since old points satisfy $v_a=v_0$. The remaining point $C$ cannot equal an old cluster point because its coordinate one is outside $Q$, and cannot equal a retained line point because its anchor coordinate is $L$. Every new velocity is positive. The new cluster has four more points and remains in the hyperplane $v_0=L$.

The set $Q$ gains at most two values at each substitution, since only the offsets $-T,0,T$ appear in coordinate one. Thus after $r$ substitutions, $|Q|\leq2r+1$.

The cluster replacement identity survives the added coordinate: all cluster points have anchor coordinate $L$, so the added bit contributes the same $\varepsilon Lx_a$ to every cluster denominator and to the denominator of the original cluster point. It is absorbed into the arbitrary common base in the identity. The retained line points acquire the same coordinate $L+4h$ as their other coordinates. Hence every step preserves the stated exact score representation by the original diagonal family.

## Preservation of the moment circuit

Put $k=m-1$. Initially the test $x=e_0$ gives the powers of the $m+1$ distinct numbers $L,L+4,\ldots,L+4m$. Their Vandermonde matrix through degree $m-1$ has rank $m$. The finite-difference relation $c$ annihilates every Boolean moment of those degrees, so the full moment kernel has dimension one and its generator has no zero coordinate.

Suppose the circuit property holds before a substitution. It survives the preliminary translation. It also survives the coordinate extension: the old rows are still present by taking the added bit zero, so the kernel can only shrink; the known relation remains valid by the cluster identity and the diagonal finite-difference identity, so the kernel is unchanged.

Consider any relation among the new moment columns. Write the new coefficients as $s_A,s_B,s_C,s_E,s_F$. Compare the moments at $e_a$ and $e_0$ in degrees one and two. Every old retained point contributes zero to the difference. The new anchor offsets, divided by $T$, are $1,2,0,2,1$. The two equations therefore give

$$ s_A+s_F=0,\qquad s_B+s_E=0. $$

Next compare degree-two moments at $e_1+e_a$ and $e_1+e_0$. Write the offsets in coordinates one and $a$ as $T\alpha$ and $T\beta$. After the preceding first and second offset moments cancel, the remaining equation is $\sum s\alpha\beta=0$. In the displayed order, $\alpha\beta$ has values $1,0,0,-2,-1$. Consequently

$$ s_A=s_E=t,\qquad s_B=s_F=-t $$

for some real $t$. The coefficient $s_C$ is not yet determined.

The moment version of the local switch now rewrites the putative relation as a combination of all old columns, with coefficient $-t$ at $p$, plus $(s_C+t)C_k(C)$. We show that $C_k(C)$ is outside the span of the old columns. Use the polynomial

$$ q(z)=\prod_{u\in Q}(z-u). $$

The linear functional on moment columns defined by $q(v_1)-q(v_0)$ vanishes at every old point. For a line point, its two arguments agree. For a cluster point, both arguments are roots of $q$. At $C$, its anchor coordinate is $L$, while its coordinate one is $p_1+T\notin Q$, so the value is nonzero.

This functional uses only available moment rows: $\deg q=|Q|\leq2r+1\leq m-1$. There are $n-2$ substitutions in total, so the largest preceding $r$ is $n-3$, and the assumed $m\geq2n$ suffices. Degree two is also available.

It follows that $s_C=-t$. The remaining relation is among the old columns and hence is a scalar multiple $\lambda c$ of the old circuit. Therefore $t=-\lambda c_p$, and all new coefficients are exactly $\lambda c_p(-1,1,1,-1,1)$. Every old retained coefficient is $\lambda$ times its former value. The kernel is one-dimensional and its generator has no zero coordinate. This proves the induction.

## Affine span, contact, and absence of identities

Every removed point is an affine combination of its replacements, so the old affine span is retained. The new differences include $A-C=Te_a$ and $B-E=Te_1$. At the first substitution these two coordinate directions together with the old diagonal direction span three dimensions. At each later substitution, the old full affine span lies in the hyperplane $v_a=v_0$, and the direction $e_a$ leaves that hyperplane. Thus each step increases the affine dimension by one. After $n-2$ substitutions, the affine span is all of $\mathbb R^n$ and the number of points is $m+1+4(n-2)$.

The complete score is still the original finite-difference score. Its moments below order $m$ vanish at every Boolean input because the projected diagonal velocities are $(L+4h)|x|$, polynomial in $h$. At the anchor input the degree $m$ moment is

$$ \sum_{h=0}^m(-1)^{m-h}\binom mh(L+4h)^m=4^m m!. $$

This follows by expanding $\sum_h(-1)^{m-h}\binom mh e^{ht}=(e^t-1)^m$. Since $D(e_0)=2$, the actual degree $m$ Taylor coefficient is $(-1)^m4^m m!/2^{m+1}$, which is nonzero. Hence the contact order is exactly $m$.

Any persistent reciprocal identity would annihilate every Taylor moment. By the circuit result it would be a scalar multiple of the constructed residue vector, and its degree $m$ moment then forces that scalar to vanish. All velocities are positive, so the denominators $1+\sum_i(1+\varepsilon v_i)x_i$ have strictly positive coherent slopes. This proves every part of the theorem. $\blacksquare$

## What the theorem excludes and what it does not prove

The construction excludes a contact estimate proportional to $H/n$ based only on full affine span, minimal truncated-moment dependence, and absence of persistent identities. For example, choose $m=n^2$. Then $m/H$ tends to one while $n$ tends to infinity. Such an estimate would need an additional invariant or a genuinely different normal form.

It is not a Boolean head-count lower bound. Indeed the exact score has the closed form

$$ R_x(\varepsilon)=\frac{(-1)^m m!(4\varepsilon|x|)^m}{\prod_{h=0}^m\bigl(D(x)+\varepsilon(L+4h)|x|\bigr)}. $$

To verify the identity, multiply by the denominator and evaluate at its $m+1$ simple poles as a rational function of $D$, or apply finite differences to $1/(a+hq)$. At $|x|=0$ both sides are zero. For positive $\varepsilon$ its sign at every nonzero Boolean input is $(-1)^m$. Adding bias one to $(-1)^mR$ gives the constant positive classifier. The original $m+1$ diagonal heads are also an explicit shorter representation of the real table. At each fixed deformation parameter, at most $n+1$ heads suffice: interpolate its $n+1$ Hamming-weight values using $1/(t+j+1)$ for $0\leq j\leq n$. The interpolation matrix is invertible, since a linear combination vanishing at $t=0,\ldots,n$ has a cleared numerator of degree at most $n$ with $n+1$ roots, and its distinct pole residues then force every coefficient to vanish. All these interpolation heads have positive coherent slopes.

The main lower-bound target, including any denominator exponent between one and two, remains unproved. This result closes a proposed contact-based transfer under the exact hypotheses stated above; it is not an obstruction to every possible analysis of minimal head representations.

## Verification scope

The arbitrary-dimension construction and circuit induction are proved in writing above. The finite exact replay checks the full Boolean moment matrices, replacement identities, affine ranks, and first nonzero moments in explicitly generated instances.
