\paragraph{Scope and status.}
This note audits two attempted assembly mechanisms for the requested
reverse-orientation, axis-zero local compiler. Neither result is an
obstruction to that compiler itself. In particular, freely and jointly
programmed row and column coefficients in different denominators are
not excluded. No superconstant universal head saving is proved here.
The propositions have complete written proofs; they are not Lean
formalizations. The companion exact-arithmetic script
\texttt{reverse-joint-transfer-check.py} checks the displayed identities
and the smallest nontrivial row-order example.

\section{Common positive factors do not repair independent row orders}

On a two-one-hot grid an affine denominator is an additive matrix.
Multiplication of the complete score by positive row and column factors
would preserve every strict sign. This suggests using a Cauchy-kernel
change of variables to change all the denominator orientations together.
There is a restrictive compatibility condition.

\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous additive rescaling]
Let $r,s\geq 2$, let $h$ range over a finite nonempty family, and put
$D_h(i,j)=b_h(i)+t_h(j)$. Suppose two of the row vectors $b_h$ are
nonconstant and are not affinely related. Suppose at least one column
vector $t_h$ is nonconstant. If positive numbers $\alpha_i,\beta_j$
make every matrix
\[
 E_h(i,j)=\alpha_i\beta_j\bigl(b_h(i)+t_h(j)\bigr)
\]
additive, then $\alpha_i$ is independent of $i$ and $\beta_j$ is
independent of $j$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume first that $\beta_j\neq\beta_\ell$ for two columns. An additive
matrix has a column difference independent of the row. Consequently,
for every $h$ there is a constant $K_h$ such that
\[
 \alpha_i\bigl[(\beta_j-\beta_\ell)b_h(i)
      +\beta_jt_h(j)-\beta_\ell t_h(\ell)\bigr]=K_h
 \quad\text{for every }i.
\]
If $b_h$ is nonconstant, then $K_h\neq0$: otherwise positivity of
$\alpha_i$ and $\beta_j-\beta_\ell\neq0$ would make $b_h$ constant.
Thus $1/\alpha_i$ is an affine function of $b_h(i)$ with nonzero
linear coefficient. Applying this to the two designated row vectors
makes them affinely related, contrary to the hypothesis.
Hence $\beta_j=\beta$ is constant.

Choose $h$ and columns $j,\ell$ with $t_h(j)\neq t_h(\ell)$.
The column difference of $E_h$ is
$\beta\alpha_i(t_h(j)-t_h(\ell))$. Its independence of $i$, together
with $\beta>0$, proves that $\alpha_i$ is constant.
\end{proof}

For example, $b_1=(1,2,4)$ and $b_2=(1,4,2)$ are not affinely related.
Indeed, the first two coordinates would force slope $3$ and intercept
$-2$, incorrectly predicting the last coordinate to be $10$.
Thus even two such row orders already prevent any nonconstant common
separable rescaling when there is a varying column parameter.

For clarity, the exact Cauchy covariance identity is as follows. For
$f(x)=(ax+b)/(cx+d)$ and $ad-bc\neq0$, wherever the expressions are
defined,
\[
 f(u)-f(-v)=\frac{(ad-bc)(u+v)}{(cu+d)(d-cv)}.
\]
It supplies a separable row and column multiplier for one kernel.
For independent row orders those multipliers generally depend on the
head. Such different multipliers cannot simply be pulled outside a
signed sum. The proposition excludes a common separable multiplier
that keeps every individual transformed denominator additive; it does
not exclude changing the head decomposition, adding heads, or a
nonseparable multiplier.

\section{Exact common-selector gates need enough poles}

The positive-orientation interval extraction compiler uses saturation to
make a selected interval contribute little on other row groups. An
attempt to replace this with exact cancellation on those groups has the
following elementary cost when all selector choices lie on one affine
parameter line.

\begin{proposition}[Exact selector cost]
Let $t_1,\ldots,t_c$ be distinct reals and fix a group $g$. Let
\[
 Q_g(t)=q_g+\sum_{h=1}^H\frac{a_{gh}+b_{gh}t}{d_{gh}+e_{gh}t},
\]
where all displayed denominators are nonzero at every $t_k$. If
$Q_g(t_g)\neq0$ and $Q_g(t_k)=0$ for all $k\neq g$, then $H\geq c-1$.
The conclusion permits signed numerators and arbitrary cancellation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Multiply by $D_g(t)=\prod_{h=1}^H(d_{gh}+e_{gh}t)$. The resulting
polynomial $P_g$ has degree at most $H$, including its bias term.
It is nonzero because $P_g(t_g)=D_g(t_g)Q_g(t_g)\neq0$.
Each of the $c-1$ other distinct selectors is a root. The elementary
root bound therefore gives $c-1\leq\deg P_g\leq H$.
The argument also covers $H=0$ by the empty-product convention.
\end{proof}

This is a statement about exact off-group zeros on one parameter line.
It does not apply to approximate gates with controlled accumulated error,
selectors with several independent parameters, sign-only majority
assembly, or a fully joint compiler that never isolates the groups.

\paragraph{Remaining construction problem.}
The requested $O((r+s)/\log\log(r+s))$ compiler remains open after these
tests. A successful joint construction must avoid the particular
common-factor or common-selector restrictions above, or quantitatively
account for the cost of abandoning exact cancellation. These tests do
not replace the main universal construction requirement.
