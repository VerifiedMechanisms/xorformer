\paragraph{Outcome.}
The requested unconditional bound
\[
 W(n)=O\left(\frac{2^n}{n\log\log n}\right)
\]
was not proved. Nor was the stronger logarithmic bound proved.
The reverse-orientation, axis-zero compiler with a sublinear head budget
remains unresolved. This run does not prove that compiler false.
The best unconditional global bound available in the repository remains
the existing $W(n)\leq 11\cdot 2^n/n$ for $n>0$.

Work continues on the supplied branch \texttt{ar/1c1556983291}.
The original definitions of \texttt{FracAtom}, \texttt{HStar}, and
\texttt{worstCaseHeadComplexity} are unchanged.
No input coordinates, additional layers, altered denominator conditions,
proof placeholders, or new axioms are introduced. The new results are
an independently audited and substantially formalized gluing reduction,
and an exact universal local compiler at linear cost. They do not satisfy
the requested asymptotic improvement.

\section{Exact universal local construction at linear cost}
For an arbitrary real $r$ by $s$ matrix $M$, let $m=r+s$ and define
\[
 D=m+2-\sum_i z_i-\sum_jw_j,\qquad
 L_i(w)=m(m-1)\sum_jM_{ij}w_j,
\]
\[
 F(z,w)=\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{L_i(w)}{D-z_i}
               -\frac{\sum_iL_i(w)}D.
\]
On the entire Boolean cube, $D\geq2$ and $D-z_i\geq1$.
Every denominator slope is $-1$, except for one slope $-2$ in each row
head. Both axes vanish exactly. At $(e_a,e_b)$ all but the $a$-th row
contribution cancel, leaving
\[
 F(e_a,e_b)=m(m-1)M_{ab}
       \left(\frac1{m-1}-\frac1m\right)=M_{ab}.
\]
Thus $r+1$ actual admissible atoms suffice, with zero output bias.
Transposition gives $\min(r,s)+1$ heads. The denominator conditions
hold strictly before any limiting argument.

The file \texttt{formalization/HeadComplexity/Results/ReverseAxisCompiler.lean}
proves \texttt{exists\_reverseAxis\_atoms} and
\texttt{exists\_reverseAxis\_exact\_compiler}. The latter packages exact
arbitrary real grid interpolation and both Boolean-axis identities into
one theorem about a family of $r+1$ original \texttt{FracAtom}s.
The public algebraic lemmas include
\texttt{reverseAxisDenom\_ge\_two},
\texttt{reverseAxisRowDenom\_ge\_one},
\texttt{reverseAxisScore\_zero\_left},
\texttt{reverseAxisScore\_zero\_right}, and
\texttt{reverseAxisScore\_oneHot}.
The transposed minimum formulation is written in
\texttt{reverse-alternative-notes.tex}; Lean states the $r+1$ orientation.
This is a joint exact construction for all tables, but its cost is linear.

\section{Independent gluing audit and complete budget}
The complete written audit is \texttt{reverse-gluing-audit.tex}.
The finite induction survives independent checking. Bias must be absorbed
into a numerator before adding the outside-coordinate denominator penalty.
Centers must be ordered by nonincreasing total cardinality. An earlier
protected point inside the current downset then has at most two deletions.
It is either on an exact-zero axis or in the current cell, where its new
contribution has the correct strict sign. Outside the downset, the fixed
numerators divided by penalized denominators tend to zero. Finiteness and
the earlier strict margin supply one penalty preserving all protected signs.

In \texttt{Results/AxisZeroGluing.lean}, namespace
\texttt{HeadComplexity.AxisZeroGluing}, the public declarations
\texttt{absorb\_bias}, \texttt{exists\_positive\_scale},
\texttt{glue\_step}, \texttt{glue\_finite},
\texttt{deletion\_counts}, and \texttt{ratios\_tendsto\_zero}
check these analytic and arithmetic steps.
\texttt{glue\_positive\_affine\_ratios} constructs actual atoms from
compatible local affine-ratio modules, and \texttt{glued\_head\_count}
proves that flattening its family uses exactly the sum of local head counts.
\texttt{weak\_affine\_ratios\_admissible} checks the final perturbation
from nonnegative slopes to strict positive slopes with unchanged head count.
Its strict-sign hypothesis is imposed after gluing, so axis identities
are not incorrectly required to survive that perturbation.

\texttt{Results/AxisZeroCover.lean} proves
\texttt{openDownwardCover\_of\_upward},
\texttt{exists\_openDownwardCover},
\texttt{product\_openDownwardCover}, and
\texttt{exists\_product\_openDownwardCover}.
Using the existing checked constant eleven, a block of size $a>0$ has an
open downward cover of its proper vertices with at most $22\cdot2^a/a$
centers. Therefore the product of blocks of sizes $a,b>0$ requires at most
\[
 \frac{484\cdot2^{a+b}}{ab}
\]
modules and excludes precisely the two faces where one block is all ones.

\texttt{Results/AxisZeroFaces.lean} proves arbitrary real face corrections
via \texttt{positive\_face\_uniformlyAtomsApproximable}.
The theorem \texttt{repair\_two\_positive\_faces} takes an actual $H$-head
score strictly correct off two positive faces with free dimensions $a,b$
and produces \texttt{computableWithHeadsN} with $H+2^a+2^b$ heads.
The first formal correction sets its face exactly to $\pm1$; the second
has zero residual at the intersection and preserves the first face.
Uniform monomial approximation then preserves the finite positive margin.
These statements are about the original atom and attention interfaces.
The companion \texttt{HStar\_le\_repair\_two\_positive\_faces}
states the same head bound directly for \texttt{HStar}.

Consequently the complete written local-to-global implication has budget
\[
 H\leq \frac{484\cdot2^n}{ab}\,M_n+2^a+2^b,
 \qquad a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\quad b=\lceil n/2\rceil,
\]
where $M_n$ is a uniform valid local cost for all $2\leq r+s\leq n$.
The hoped-for local rate gives $M_n=O(n/\log\log n)$, while
$2^a+2^b=O(2^{\lceil n/2\rceil})$ is negligible at that scale.
The exact compiler proved here instead supplies only $M_n=O(n)$.
The geometric local-to-ambient substitution and the final asymptotic
combination are written arguments. No Lean theorem asserting the requested
new bound against \texttt{worstCaseHeadComplexity} has been added.

\section{A finite joint decoder with two heads}
The complete written proof in \texttt{reverse-joint-five-row.tex}
constructs, for every $s\geq1$, two strictly admissible negative-slope
heads realizing every sign table on a $5$ by $s$ grid.
Its row coefficients are fixed independently of the labels; each column
has four independent numerator and denominator parameters.
Four fixed row queries have two distinct regular common zeros. A fifth
query is exactly $+1$ at one and $-1$ at the other. The ordinary real
inverse function theorem then realizes all $32$ strict row-sign patterns
in two small parameter neighborhoods.

With $\varepsilon=1/(100(s+1))$, every chosen column denominator
coefficient lies in $(\varepsilon/2,5\varepsilon/2)$. The entire Boolean
cube has denominator margin strictly greater than
$1-37\varepsilon-(5/2)s\varepsilon>0$. Thus this result does not
confuse positivity on the grid with full-cube admissibility.
The sheet values and both Jacobian determinant identities have exact
algebraic certificates. The inverse-function step is a written proof,
not a Lean theorem in this run.

This construction lacks exact axis zeros. It also gives only a fixed
ratio of five rows per two heads. Disjoint repetition does not provide
an unbounded improvement, and no interference-free repetition has been
proved here. Multiplying or recursively composing such scores introduces
products of unrelated affine denominators. The same note solves the row
coefficients again after axis subtraction. This does yield a finite
five-row decoder with both exact axes zero, but costs six ratios and
has weakly negative slopes in four of them. Final global perturbation
can restore admissibility after gluing, with no increase in this count.
Six ratios for five rows gives no improvement over the strictly admissible
linear compiler already proved in Lean.
Consequently this finite positive result does not establish either the
requested local compiler or the global asymptotic target.

\section{Exact tests and failed compression attempts}
The script \texttt{reverse\_exact\_audit.py} uses rational arithmetic.
It exhaustively checks 602 sign tables of sizes $1\times1$, $1\times3$,
$2\times2$, $2\times3$, and $3\times3$, including every cube denominator,
both complete Boolean axes, and every exact grid value.
Nine complete gluing examples in dimensions two through four check bias
absorption, arbitrary tie order, preservation after each insertion, and
both exceptional-face repairs followed by actual monomial-head approximation.
The output is saved as \texttt{reverse-exact-validation.json}.

An exact wrong-order example has four coordinates. The earlier singleton
center module has score $-1$ at zero and $-1/6$ at $(0,1,0,1)$.
The later full-center module has scores $24$ and $1$ respectively.
Repairing the second point requires $\lambda>1/6$, whereas retaining the
negative sign at zero requires $\lambda<1/24$. Thus reversing the audited
order really can prevent a scalar repair. This example does not obstruct
the correct descending order.

Direct joint compression was investigated rather than separately reversing
the known numerical ranking votes. The following failures are scoped:
\begin{itemize}
\item Grouping row shifts with one common grid-constant baseline yields
  a matrix of rank at most the number of groups. This prevents arbitrary
  real interpolation with fewer groups, but is not a general lower bound
  for sign-only reverse compilers.
\item A common positive separable multiplier cannot change independent
  Cauchy row orders while keeping each denominator additive. The exact
  compatibility theorem is in \texttt{reverse-joint-transfer-audit.tex}.
  Head-dependent multipliers cannot be pulled out of a signed sum.
\item Exact off-group zeros along one affine selector line require at
  least one pole per other selector. This does not cover approximate
  selectors or independently programmed multiple parameters.
\item Arbitrary numerator memory, including high-degree polynomial divided
  differences, was tested as a permutation-sheet decoder. A query changing
  one slot against one symmetric baseline clears to an affine form in
  $(\psi(t),t,t^2)$. Four such fixed queries have at most fifteen strict
  patterns, so this particular high-degree proposal does not provide four
  independently readable bits. This does not address joint multi-slot
  resynthesis. Its identity is checked exactly in
  \texttt{reverse-joint-transfer-check.py}.
\item Changing upward versus downward covers still leaves the known
  positive-orientation decoder incompatible with the outside suppression
  slopes. Global amortization of arithmetic-chain reversal was considered,
  but the existing target-dependent denominators do not satisfy its
  common-chain and periodic-residue hypotheses. No construction enforcing
  those hypotheses at the required cost was found.
\item Near-common-denominator finite differences give higher-degree
  polynomial limits, but a separate degree-$d$ feature costs $d$ poles.
  No shared cancellation construction producing an unbounded head saving
  was obtained. Analytic deformation of the known majority compiler
  supplies no transfer theorem: its open sign regions need not continue
  through the constant-denominator midpoint, and a regular common zero
  for all $rs$ outputs cannot have a surjective Jacobian with only
  $O(Hm)<rs$ parameters.
\item Removing all center cylinders would remove the intermediate
  axis-preservation requirement, but repairs on those general mixed faces
  are not covered by the cheap positive-face correction theorem above.
  Their required head budget remains unproved.
\item Partitioning centers into four classes according to the two
  cardinality parities removes the need for axis zeros within each class:
  both deletion counts are odd and their sum is at most two. Combining
  the four resulting scores would require a parity-dependent selection
  operation. No such operation with a sufficient head budget was found.
\end{itemize}
The written transfer tests are not advertised as refutations of the
desired local theorem. No successful alternative global construction was
obtained.

\section{Verification and remaining problem}
All new Lean theorems are proved without placeholders. The audit checks
60 theorem declarations across the four added modules. Their axiom audits
permit only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and
\texttt{Quot.sound}. The main \texttt{HeadComplexity} build and the exact
scripts are run as part of this checkpoint; the machine-readable
\texttt{reverse-validation.json} records final commands, source hashes,
and verification status. The aggregate result module imports the new
files. No original theorem statement or resource definition was weakened.

No external mathematical hypothesis is needed for the newly proved finite
results. The sublinear local compiler is an unproved hypothesis only in
the written conditional asymptotic implication. It is neither assumed as
an axiom nor discharged by the linear compiler. A universal
$O(m/\log\log m)$ joint reverse compiler, or a different complete
global construction with the same saving, remains the required next result.
