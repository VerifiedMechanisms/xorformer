\section{The exact finite compiler}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:finite}
Let $k\ge1$, $q=2^k$, $r=q^2k$, and $s\ge1$. Every sign matrix
$Y\in\{-1,1\}^{r\times s}$ has an exact realization on the one-hot
row by one-hot column grid by $2q^2+1$ affine-ratio heads. Every
denominator is positive on the whole $(r+s)$-dimensional Boolean cube,
and every slope can be made strictly negative without changing any grid
value. The realized values equal $Y$, not merely its signs.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{A common Cauchy decoder}
Fix a field $F$ of $q$ elements, and identify its elements with the
$q$ binary words of length $k$. Write $\beta_b(z)\in\{-1,1\}$
for bit $b$ of word $z$. Choose positive numbers $\delta,\tau$ with
\[
 r\delta+s\tau<1,
 \qquad \delta+\tau<1.
 \tag{1}
\]
Choose pairwise distinct real poles $d_a\in(1-\delta,1)$ for
$a\in F$, and pairwise distinct tokens $t_z\in(0,\tau)$ for
$z\in F$. Thus every $d_a-t_z$ is strictly positive.

The square matrix $C_{z,a}=1/(d_a-t_z)$ is invertible. Indeed, a
linear combination $\sum_a c_a/(d_a-t)$ vanishing at all $q$ tokens
has, after multiplication by $\prod_a(d_a-t)$, a polynomial numerator
of degree at most $q-1$ with $q$ distinct roots. That numerator is
zero. Evaluation at $t=d_a$ gives
$c_a\prod_{a'\ne a}(d_{a'}-d_a)=0$, hence $c_a=0$ for every $a$.
Therefore, for each bit $b$, there are real numbers $\eta_{b,a}$ with
\[
 \sum_{a\in F}\frac{\eta_{b,a}}{d_a-t_z}=\beta_b(z)
 \quad\text{for every }z\in F.
 \tag{2}
\]
These coefficients can be arbitrarily large. The unchanged model
places no bound on numerator size or parameter precision.

\subsection{Point states and shared line values}
Use points $P=F^2$ and only nonvertical lines, indexed by
$\mathcal L=F^2$:
\[
 L_{a,c}=\{(x,y):y=ax+c\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
There are $q^2$ points and $q^2$ lines. Each point lies on one line
of each slope $a$. Distinct points with different first coordinates
share exactly one line, of slope
\[
 a(h,g)=\frac{g_2-h_2}{g_1-h_1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Distinct points with the same first coordinate share no nonvertical
line. All arithmetic in (3)--(4) is in $F$; the poles, tokens, and
head coefficients are real numbers.

Index rows by $(h,b)\in P\times\{1,\ldots,k\}$. For every point
$g$ and table column $j$, select $z_{g,j}\in F$ whose $k$ bits are
the prescribed labels $Y_{(g,b),j}$. This is possible for every
label vector because the identification $F\cong\{0,1\}^k$ is a
bijection. Put $T_{g,j}=t_{z_{g,j}}$ and define real constants
\[
 Z_{(a,c),j}=\sum_{g\in L_{a,c}}\frac1{d_a-T_{g,j}}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The word ``constants'' is important: the complete table is fixed before
the heads are built. The right side of (5) computes parameters; it is
not an operation performed on the Boolean input.

Define the selector matrix
\[
 R_{(h,b),(a,c)}=
 \begin{cases}\eta_{b,a},&c=h_2-ah_1,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
 \tag{6}
\]
The rank-factorized table
\[
 A_{(h,b),j}=\sum_{(a,c)\in\mathcal L}
               R_{(h,b),(a,c)}Z_{(a,c),j}
 \tag{7}
\]
has $q^2$ displayed factors. Expanding (5) and exchanging the two
finite sums gives the exact identity
\[
 A_{(h,b),j}=Y_{(h,b),j}
 +\sum_{\substack{g\in P\\g_1\ne h_1}}
 \frac{\eta_{b,a(h,g)}}{d_{a(h,g)}-T_{g,j}}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The diagonal point $g=h$ contributes (2). Every other point occurs
once or not at all, by (4). This is the essential incidence saving.
The checked declaration
\texttt{affinePlane\_weighted\_pencil\_identity} is precisely this
finite-sum identity for an arbitrary real kernel. The checked
\texttt{affinePlane\_line\_rank\_identity} gives (6)--(7).

\subsection{One correction head per point}
Let $u_{h,b}$ and $v_j$ be the row and column Boolean input bits.
For each $g\in P$, define real row constants
\[
 e_{(h,b),g}=
 \begin{cases}\eta_{b,a(h,g)},&h_1\ne g_1,\\0,&h_1=g_1,\end{cases}
\]
and choose $p_{(h,b),g}=d_{a(h,g)}$ when $h_1\ne g_1$.
When $h_1=g_1$, choose any one of the same poles for $p_{(h,b),g}$.
The correction head is the single admissible affine ratio
\[
 -\frac{\sum_{h,b}e_{(h,b),g}u_{h,b}}
 {1-\sum_{h,b}(1-p_{(h,b),g})u_{h,b}-\sum_jT_{g,j}v_j}.
 \tag{9}
\]
On the grid input $u=e_{(h,b)},v=e_j$, it equals the negative
summand for $g$ in (8), or zero if $h_1=g_1$. The numerator of (9)
is affine: in particular there is no product of a row bit with a
column bit or with another input bit. Every denominator slope is
strictly negative, and on the entire cube its denominator exceeds
\[
 1-r\delta-s\tau>0.
 \tag{10}
\]
The zero numerator in some rows imposes no exception to this global
admissibility assertion. The $q^2$ heads (9) cancel all unwanted
terms of (8) exactly.

\subsection{The exact rank compiler, expanded}
For completeness, consider one arbitrary factor $f_i g_j$ on an
$r\times s$ grid. Choose
\[
 C=1+\sum_i|f_i|,\quad u_i=C+f_i>0,
 \quad M=4(r+1)C.
\]
The head
\[
 \frac{M\sum_jg_jv_j}
 {1-\sum_i\frac{u_i}{M+u_i}u^{\rm row}_i}
 \tag{11}
\]
has grid value $(M+C+f_i)g_j$. Since $0<u_i<2C$,
\[
 \sum_i\frac{u_i}{M+u_i}<\frac{2rC}{M}<\frac12,
\]
so its denominator is positive on the whole cube. Its row slopes are
negative and its column slopes zero. Applying (11) to each of the
$q^2$ factors in (7), one additional denominator-one affine head
subtracts the column-only sum $\sum_L(M_L+C_L)Z_{L,j}$.
Thus (7) costs exactly $q^2+1$ weak reverse heads. There is no
restriction on the sizes or signs of the matrix entries.

To restore strict slopes, consider any weak denominator
$D(w)=b_0+\sum_i b_iw_i$ with $b_i\le0$ and positive full-cube
minimum $m=D(1,\ldots,1)$. With $N=r+s$ and
$\epsilon=m/(N+1)$, replace it by
\[
 D'(w)=D(w)+\epsilon\left(2-\sum_iw_i\right).
 \tag{12}
\]
Every slope is now strictly negative. On the whole cube,
$D'\ge m+2\epsilon-N\epsilon=3\epsilon>0$.
Every grid input has weight two, so its denominator is unchanged.
The numerator is unchanged as well. This is the checked
\texttt{weak\_reverse\_ratio\_strictify} construction.

Combining the $q^2+1$ rank heads with the $q^2$ correction heads
proves Theorem~\ref{thm:finite}. No limit, approximate sign, product
gate, or implicit determinant evaluator is used.

\section{Arbitrary row counts and the global bound}
For any integer $a\ge5$, take the least $k\ge1$ with
$a\le k4^k$, and put $q=2^k$. The finite compiler may be restricted
to any $a$ of its $r=k4^k$ rows by setting all unused row input bits
to zero. This retains strict negativity for every remaining slope
and full-cube positivity. Extend the target table arbitrarily on
the unused rows before constructing the heads. Thus it gives every
$a\times b$ table, for every $b\ge1$, with
\[
 H=2\cdot4^k+1\le17a/k.
 \tag{13}
\]
Indeed minimality gives $(k-1)4^{k-1}<a$, $k\ge2$, and hence
$4^k<4a/(k-1)\le8a/k$; also $k\le a$.
Since $k\le2^k$, the inequality $a\le k4^k\le8^k$ gives
\[
 k\ge\frac{\log a}{3\log2}.
 \tag{14}
\]

The already checked axis repair converts $H$ strict reverse grid
heads into $3H+3$ reverse heads that vanish identically on both
Boolean axes, with exactly the same grid values. The already checked
global transfer then gives, for positive $a,b$,
\[
 W(a+b)\le
 \frac{484\,2^{a+b}(3H+3)}{ab}+2^a+2^b.
 \tag{15}
\]
This is
\texttt{worstCaseHeadComplexity\_real\_le\_of\_strictReverseGridCompiler}
against the original \texttt{worstCaseHeadComplexity} definition.

Set $a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, $b=n-a$. For sufficiently large $n$,
$b\ge n/2$, $a\ge\sqrt n$, and (13) implies $3H+3\le54a/k$.
Combining (14)--(15) yields
\[
 W(n)\le
 (313632\log2)\frac{2^n}{n\log n}+2^a+2^b.
 \tag{16}
\]
The last two terms are negligible on this scale. Here is an explicit
elementary justification, avoiding an unproved comparison of rates.
For $a\ge6$, the binomial theorem gives
$2^a\ge\binom a4\ge a^4/384$. Since $a\ge n/3$ and
$\log n\le n$ for large $n$,
\[
 \frac{(2^a+2^b)n\log n}{2^n}
 \le\frac{2n\log n}{2^a}
 \le\frac{768n^2}{a^4}
 \le\frac{62208}{n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore (16) proves the unconditional eventual bound
\[
 \boxed{W(n)=O\!\left(\frac{2^n}{n\log n}\right).}
 \tag{17}
\]
The construction depends on the full truth table through the tokens
$T_{g,j}$ and the precomputed line constants $Z_{L,j}$. It therefore
does not fall under the fixed-denominator obstruction. Arbitrarily
high parameter precision is allowed and is used by the compact
Cauchy interpolation. No claim of a positive fixed exponent
improvement follows from (17).

\section{Independent finite and formal checks}

$F_8=\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^3+X+1)$, computes all Cauchy coefficients
with rational arithmetic, and checks (8) for a $192\times8$ table

$\delta=1/(8r)$ and $\tau=1/(8s)$, giving the common correction
denominator lower bound $3/4$. This test supplements the symbolic
identity; it is not the proof of universality.

The independent Lean incidence file proves
