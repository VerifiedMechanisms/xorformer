\paragraph{Status.}
The implementation statements below are Lean checked in
\texttt{Results/ReverseSignedCauchy.lean} and
\texttt{Results/ReverseCauchyBiasAssembly.lean}. They preserve the
original input dimension and strict atom admissibility. They do not
assert that a growing number of independent signs can be decoded.

\begin{theorem}
Fix positive query times $t_j$, positive row poles $p_{hi}<t_j$,
arbitrary real residues $a_{hi}$, and two fixed Boolean query masks
$M^+_{hj},M^-_{hj}$. The masks may overlap. Suppose $\epsilon>0$ and
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^r\frac{p_{hi}}{p_{hi}+\epsilon}
 +\sum_{j=1}^s\frac{\epsilon}{t_j+\epsilon}<1
 \qquad\text{for every }h.
 \tag{1}
\]
Then $2H$ affine-fraction heads, with denominators positive on the
whole Boolean $(r+s)$-cube and all slopes strictly negative, have
exact zero scores on both Boolean axes and grid score
\[
 \epsilon\sum_{h=1}^H
 (M^+_{hj}-M^-_{hj})\frac{a_{hi}}{t_j-p_{hi}}.
 \tag{2}
\]
If $G$ additional fixed Boolean query masks $N_{gj}$ carry arbitrary
row coefficients $b_{gi}$, then $2H+2G$ heads realize instead
\[
 \epsilon\left(\sum_{h=1}^H
 (M^+_{hj}-M^-_{hj})\frac{a_{hi}}{t_j-p_{hi}}
 +\sum_{g=1}^G N_{gj}b_{gi}\right)
 \tag{3}
\]
with the same strict admissibility and axis identities.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $q_{hi}=p_{hi}/(p_{hi}+\epsilon)$,
$\tau_j=t_j/(t_j+\epsilon)$, and
$\alpha_{hi}=\epsilon a_{hi}/(p_{hi}+\epsilon)$.
Choose a sufficiently small $\eta>0$ using the strict margin in (1).
For each $h$, use opposite numerators
$\pm\sum_i(1-q_{hi})\alpha_{hi}z_i$ and denominators
\[
 B_h^\pm=1+\eta-\sum_iq_{hi}z_i
 -\sum_j\left(\eta+M^\pm_{hj}(1-\tau_j)\right)w_j.
\]
All slopes are strictly negative. Each mask sum is bounded by the
unmasked sum in (1), so these denominators are positive on the
whole cube for sufficiently small $\eta$. On the left axis the
numerators vanish; on the right axis each pair cancels.
At a grid point, subtract the inactive denominator from each active
one. The exact paired identity gives
\[
 (M^+_{hj}-M^-_{hj})(1-\tau_j)
 \frac{\alpha_{hi}}{\tau_j-q_{hi}}
 =\epsilon(M^+_{hj}-M^-_{hj})
 \frac{a_{hi}}{t_j-p_{hi}}.
\]
This proves (2). The checked masked-row-bias construction uses two
heads per mask, with row coefficient $\epsilon b_{gi}$, to produce
the remaining term in (3). Concatenation preserves every denominator
condition and both axis identities.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why the uniform factor matters.}
The uncompactified paired identity has factor $1-t_j$, which depends
on the query. Adding an unscaled row bias to that score would not
implement a bias inside the original Cauchy expression. Formula (2)
has one uniform positive factor, and therefore closes this assembly
gap. A Haar system with $H-1$ independent block biases has total cost
$4H-2$ whenever (1) holds.

\paragraph{A sufficient small-pole condition.}
Condition (1) follows from $0<p_{hi}\leq\epsilon^2C$, $t_j\geq b>0$,
$\epsilon^2C<b$, and $\epsilon(rC+s/b)<1$. Indeed its two sums are
bounded by $r\epsilon C$ and $s\epsilon/b$ respectively. Thus a decoder
valid for arbitrarily small positive poles and queries bounded away
from zero can meet the whole-cube budget for any finite row count.
This estimate does not establish such a decoder.
