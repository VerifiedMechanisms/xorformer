\begin{theorem}[Exact ternary row multipliers]
Let $F$ be a reverse grid score with $H$ affine ratios and an arbitrary
bias. Every denominator is positive on the entire Boolean $(r+s)$-cube
and has strictly negative slopes. For any
$\epsilon:[r]\to\{-1,0,1\}$ there is a reverse grid score with
$2H+2$ ratios having grid values $\epsilon(i)F(e_i,e_j)$.
Its denominators retain full-cube positivity and strictly negative slopes.
The same statement holds for column multipliers.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write one input ratio on the grid as
\[
 \frac{A_i+a_j}{b_0-p_i-u_j},\qquad p_i,u_j>0,
 \qquad b_0-\sum_i p_i-\sum_j u_j>0.
\]
Here the numerator intercept is included in $A_i$. Introduce two weak
reverse denominators and their affine numerators:
\[
 B_\pm=b_0-\sum_{\epsilon(i)=\pm1}p_i z_i-\sum_j u_jw_j,
\]
\[
 A_+=\sum_{\epsilon(i)=1}A_i z_i+\sum_j a_jw_j,
 \qquad
 A_-=-\sum_{\epsilon(i)=-1}A_i z_i-\sum_j a_jw_j.
\]
Removing negative slopes only increases the cube minimum. Directly in
all three cases $\epsilon(i)\in\{-1,0,1\}$,
\[
 \frac{A_+(e_i,e_j)}{B_+(e_i,e_j)}+
 \frac{A_-(e_i,e_j)}{B_-(e_i,e_j)}
 =\epsilon(i)\left(\frac{A_i+a_j}{b_0-p_i-u_j}
                         -\frac{a_j}{b_0-u_j}\right).
\]
Summing over the input heads leaves one column baseline $q(j)$.
The missing matrix $\epsilon(i)q(j)$, together with the row term
$\epsilon(i)c$ from the original bias $c$, is exactly one rank-one
factor plus a row addition. The checked rank-correction theorem
represents it with two further heads. Strictify each weak denominator
$B$ by adding
$\delta(2-|z|-|w|)/(r+s+1)$, where
$\delta=B(\boldsymbol1)>0$. This leaves every grid value unchanged and
gives strictly negative slopes and cube minimum $3\delta/(r+s+1)$.
Swapping coordinate blocks proves the column statement.
\end{proof}

Thus a row sign followed by a Boolean column mask uses $4H+6$ heads.
Every inactive column is exactly zero on the grid. This is an exact
mask, so stacking masked scores incurs no accumulated numerical error.

\begin{theorem}[One common row translation]
Let $\mathcal G\subseteq\{-1,1\}^r$, and write
$N=2^r$ and $b=N-|\mathcal G|$. Suppose every $r\times s$ sign table
whose columns lie in $\mathcal G$ has a jointly admissible reverse grid
compiler with at most $H$ heads. If $sb<N$, then every $r\times s$
sign table has such a compiler with at most $2H+2$ heads. Exact axis
repair gives at most $6H+9$ heads.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a common row sign uniformly from $\{-1,1\}^r$. For each fixed
target column the translated column is uniform, so exactly $b$ row
signs are bad. The union of the bad sets for the $s$ columns has
cardinality at most $sb<N$. Choose a row sign outside that union,
compile the resulting good columns, and apply the exact row multiplier
above to recover the original table. The separately checked axis repair
costs $3K+3$ heads for a $K$-head grid compiler.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Several row translations]
Under the same joint family promise, suppose $r,s,k\geq1$ and
\[
 s b^k<N^k.
\]
Then every $r\times s$ sign table has a reverse grid compiler with
at most $k(4H+6)$ heads. Exact axis repair gives at most
$12kH+18k+3$ heads.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose $k$ independent row signs. A fixed column fails all of them for
exactly $b^k$ of the $N^k$ choices, so the union bound supplies a tuple
under which every target column succeeds at least once. Assign each
column to its first successful translation. The density assumption
implies $\mathcal G\neq\varnothing$. In each batch fill its unassigned
columns with any fixed good pattern, and apply the promised compiler
to the full width-$s$ table. Apply the row sign and the exact column
mask for the assigned columns, at cost $4H+6$. Summing the $k$ scores
leaves exactly one strict signed score at each grid point.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Quantitative sufficient promise.}
For balanced $r,s$ of comparable size, it would suffice to construct a
jointly admissible good family with $H=O(r/\log r)$ and bad fraction
$b/N\leq(\log r)^{-a}$ for any fixed $a>0$. Taking
\[
 k=\left\lceil\frac{\log(2s)}{a\log\log r}\right\rceil
\]
gives $s(b/N)^k\leq1/2$ and hence an
$O(r/\log\log r)$ universal compiler, including exact axes.
A bad fraction at most $r^{-a}$ instead allows bounded $k$ and
preserves the stronger $O(r/\log r)$ head bound.

\paragraph{Verification and unresolved hypothesis.}
The ternary row multiplier, block transposition, column multiplier,
$4H+6$ masked score, exact finite one-translation union bound, and
$6H+9$ conditional axis compiler are formalized in
\texttt{formalization/HeadComplexity/Results/ReverseRowFlip.lean}.
The multiple-translation argument and its asymptotic specialization
are written proofs. The exact rational checker
\texttt{reverse-row-flip-check.py} tests all 27 ternary row masks of
one three-head $3\times2$ example, including every full-cube
vertex and the strict gauge.

No sufficiently efficient high-density family has been established.
The family promise requires admissibility jointly for every choice of
$s$ good columns. Separate realizations of each column, or success on
most random complete tables with unrelated row designs, do not by
themselves establish this promise. The new reduction is conditional
and does not complete the requested unconditional global bound.
