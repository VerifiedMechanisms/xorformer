\title{Constant-density local decoding and exact residual repair}

\paragraph{Status.}
The finite half-density assembly and its exact head budget are Lean
checked in \texttt{Results/ReverseDensityBatching.lean}. The asymptotic
consequences below are elementary written deductions from that theorem
and the checked balanced gluing theorem. The required growing dense
decoder is not known. This note is a reduction, not a proof of either
accepted global target. The original atom model, input dimension, and
definitions of head complexity are unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Half-density batching]
Let $r,s,M$ be nonnegative integers. Suppose there is a set
$G\subseteq\{-1,1\}^r$, with $|G|\geq2^{r-1}$, such that every
$r$ by $s$ sign table whose columns belong to $G$ has a joint reverse
grid realization using at most $M$ heads. Every denominator in this
realization must be positive on the entire Boolean $(r+s)$-cube and
must have strictly negative slopes. Then for every pair of nonnegative
integers $t,R$ satisfying $s\leq2^tR$, every $r$ by $s$ sign table has
a strict reverse axis-zero realization with at most
\[
 t(12M+21)+3R+6                                           \tag{1}
\]
heads. There are no hypotheses on the represented table.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a target table $\sigma$ and a set $U$ of uncovered columns. For a
uniformly chosen common row-sign vector $v\in\{-1,1\}^r$, each
column $v\sigma_{\cdot j}$ is uniform on the pattern cube. Therefore
the average number of columns in $U$ whose translated pattern lies
outside $G$ is at most $|U|/2$. Some $v$ leaves at most this many
columns uncovered. This exact finite double counting is
\texttt{exists\_row\_sign\_half\_remainder}.

Let $C\subseteq U$ be the columns covered at this step. On $C$, use
the translated patterns. Fill every other column with a fixed member
of $G$. The assumed joint compiler gives at most $M$ strict reverse
grid heads. Exact common row-sign multiplication and exact masking to
$C$ cost at most $4M+6$ heads, by
\texttt{exists\_strict\_reverse\_row\_flip\_column\_mask}.
Repairing both axes costs three times this number plus three, hence
at most $12M+21$ heads. The score has the desired strict signs on $C$
and is exactly zero at every grid column outside $C$. Both Boolean
axes are exactly zero, and all denominators are strictly admissible
on the whole cube.

Repeat this procedure on the residual set. The sets $C$ are disjoint.
Concatenating the modules adds their scores, so no previously obtained
strict sign is changed. After $t$ steps the remainder $U_t$ satisfies
$2^t|U_t|\leq s$, and hence $|U_t|\leq R$. This is
\texttt{exists\_half\_dense\_reverse\_batches}.

The matrix equal to $\sigma$ on $U_t$ and zero elsewhere has an
explicit factorization with $|U_t|$ rank-one terms, one for each
remaining column. The exact rank compiler realizes this matrix with
$3(|U_t|+1)+3=3|U_t|+6$ strict axis-zero heads. This fact is
\texttt{exists\_reverse\_axis\_column\_remainder}. The earlier
modules are zero on $U_t$, and the correction is zero off $U_t$.
Their sum therefore realizes every target sign and has budget (1).
The complete theorem is
\texttt{hasReverseAxisCompiler\_of\_half\_dense\_family}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why a constant success fraction would suffice.}
Suppose for every sufficiently large $q$ there were a joint decoder
for at least half of all $q$-bit column patterns, with
$M\leq Cq/\log q$ heads. Take $R=\lceil Cq/\log q\rceil$ and
$t=\lceil\log_2(q/R)\rceil$ when $R<q$, taking $t=0$ otherwise.
Then $q\leq2^tR$, $R=O(q/\log q)$, and
$t=O(\log\log q)$. The theorem yields a universal local compiler of
cost
\[
 O\!\left(\frac{q\log\log q}{\log q}\right).       \tag{2}
\]
The same conclusion holds for balanced rectangular cells, with their
two side lengths differing by at most one, provided the joint dense
family hypothesis holds for those cells. The checked gluing bound is
\[
 W(a+b)\leq\frac{484\,2^{a+b}M_{\mathrm{local}}}{ab}
             +2^a+2^b .                              \tag{3}
\]
Thus (2), for $a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and
$b=\lceil n/2\rceil$, would give
\[
 W(n)=O\!\left(\frac{2^n\log\log n}{n\log n}\right)
      =O\!\left(\frac{2^n}{n\log\log n}\right),       \tag{4}
\]
since $(\log\log n)^2/\log n\longrightarrow0$ and the exceptional
face cost in (3) is exponentially smaller. Here all unmarked logarithms
are natural. Half-density may be replaced by any fixed positive
density: the same averaging leaves at most a fraction $1-p$ of the
columns at each step, and $O_p(\log(q/R))$ steps suffice. That
general-$p$ variant is written only; the half-density version and
all exact head operations in (1) are Lean checked.

\paragraph{The remaining obligation.}
Separate successful realizations of individual patterns do not imply
the joint full-cube hypothesis. Fixed query masks, simultaneous slope
budgets, and a scale valid for every selected pattern must be supplied.
The compactified signed-Cauchy assembly can discharge these obligations
for a decoder which works at every sufficiently small pole scale.
No proof currently establishes a positive success fraction uniformly
in the number of poles. Finite samples and finite universal certificates
do not establish this premise.
