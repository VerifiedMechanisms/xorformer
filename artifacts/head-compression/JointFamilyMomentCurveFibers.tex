\section{A polynomial-sized common signature}
Number the heads by $h=1,\ldots,H$, and let $\pi$ be a permutation of
these integers. Define
\[
 M_{a,b}(\pi)=\sum_{h=1}^H h^a\pi(h)^b,
 \qquad 0\leq a\leq kd,\quad 0\leq b\leq d.
\]
Every component is a natural number at most $H^E$, where
$E=(k+1)d+1$. There are $S=(kd+1)(d+1)$ components.

\begin{theorem}
If $(H^E+1)^S L\leq H!$, there is a set $\mathcal C$ of at least
$L$ permutations on which every displayed mixed moment is constant.
For every real polynomial $P$ of degree at most $k$, every
$0\leq r\leq d$, and every $\pi,\sigma\in\mathcal C$,
\[
 \sum_h(P(h)+\pi(h))^r=\sum_h(P(h)+\sigma(h))^r.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The fiber assertion is the pigeonhole principle applied to at most
$(H^E+1)^S$ signatures. Expand the power with the binomial theorem.
Each summand is a polynomial in $h$ of degree at most $kr\leq kd$
times a memory power at most $d$, and is therefore determined by the
signature. The Lean declarations are
\texttt{jointFamily\_large\_moment\_fiber} and
\texttt{jointFamily\_same\_signature\_power\_sums}.
\end{proof}

The logarithm of the number of signatures is at most
\[
 S(E\log H+\log 2)=O(k^2d^3\log H)
\]
when $k,d\geq1$. In particular, for $H$ exponential in $k$ and $d$
bounded by any fixed power of $k$, a fiber has
$\exp((1-o(1))H\log H)$ elements. This removes the exponential-in-$k$
signature cost of recording a separate jet at each Boolean input.
It is an entropy statement, not a sign-decoding statement.

\section{Exact remainders and full-cube admissibility}
For Boolean row addresses $x\in\{0,1\}^k$, put
\[
 P_x(h)=\sum_{i=1}^k x_i h^i.
\]
The polynomial $P_x$ has degree at most $k$. For any $B\neq0$ and
nonzero denominators, equality of the moments through order $d$ gives
the exact identity
\[
\begin{split}
 &\sum_h\frac1{B-\varepsilon(P_x(h)+\pi(h))}
 -\sum_h\frac1{B-\varepsilon(P_x(h)+\sigma(h))}\\
 &=\left(\frac\varepsilon B\right)^{d+1}
 \left(\sum_h\frac{(P_x(h)+\pi(h))^{d+1}}
 {B-\varepsilon(P_x(h)+\pi(h))}
 -\sum_h\frac{(P_x(h)+\sigma(h))^{d+1}}
 {B-\varepsilon(P_x(h)+\sigma(h))}\right).
\end{split}
\]
This is \texttt{jointFamily\_same\_signature\_remainder}; it uses the
inherited exact geometric-remainder identity, not a formal power-series
argument.

For $s$ independently labelled columns, choose a permutation $\pi_j$
independently for every column $j$, and one shared reference $\sigma$.
More generally let $d_{hi}>0$ be arbitrary fixed row weights, and set
\[
 R=\sum_{h,i}d_{hi}+sH,\qquad B=1+\varepsilon R,
 \qquad \varepsilon>0.
\]
Use two banks of $H$ heads, with numerators $\sum_jz_j$ and
$-\sum_jz_j$ respectively, and denominators
\[
 B-\varepsilon\sum_i d_{hi}x_i
   -\varepsilon\sum_j\pi_j(h)z_j,
 \qquad
 B-\varepsilon\sum_i d_{hi}x_i
   -\varepsilon\sum_j\sigma(h)z_j.
\]
All slopes are strictly negative. Every denominator is at least one
on the entire ambient Boolean cube, including multihot column inputs,
since each row sum is at most $\sum_{h,i}d_{hi}$ and each column sum
is at most $sH$. Numerators are affine. On the zero-column axis they
vanish exactly. At column $j$ the score is exactly the displayed
reciprocal difference, with $P_x(h)=\sum_i d_{hi}x_i$.

These assertions, with the exact head count $H+H$, are the declarations
\texttt{jointFamilyPermutationDifferenceData} and
\texttt{jointFamilyPermutationDifferenceData\_score}. The permutations
in distinct columns have no consistency condition. At the all-zero row,
every difference is zero because both banks have the same reciprocal
histogram; this additional identity is
\texttt{jointFamilyPermutationDifferenceData\_zero\_row}.

If strict signs on all nonzero rows were obtained, one additional head
would repair the zero row: use an independently signed, sufficiently
small column numerator and any everywhere-positive denominator with
strictly negative slopes. Finiteness gives a positive margin, so this
head preserves all previously strict local signs. This last repair
statement is a written argument here; it is not part of the new formal
declarations.

\section{The exact missing lemma}
For $M=2^k-1$, a sufficient next statement is that a fiber
$\mathcal C$, a reference $\sigma$, and a sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$ can be chosen such that the vectors
\[
 \left(\sum_h\frac1{B-\varepsilon(P_x(h)+\pi(h))}
 -\sum_h\frac1{B-\varepsilon(P_x(h)+\sigma(h))}\right)_{x\neq0},
 \qquad \pi\in\mathcal C,
\]
meet every strict orthant, with $H=o(2^k/\log k)$. For the fixed-power
goal, $H=O(2^k/\sqrt{k})$ would suffice in the existing conditional
full-cube transfer, with its documented covering costs. No version of
this assertion has been proved.

The fiber cardinality is compatible with that width, but cardinality
alone does not imply orthant coverage. Nor does an interior point in the
convex hull of these vectors: averaging permutations is not one
permutation. Existing exact counterexamples to other prescribed
permutation dictionaries remain relevant warnings. The present
construction only makes the shared cancellation and full admissibility
costs concrete; cell selection, prior-sign preservation, overlaps and
exceptions still require the original global transfer once a universal
local strict-sign theorem is available.
