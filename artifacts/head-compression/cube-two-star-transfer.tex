\title{Joint full-cube and two-star transfer}
\maketitle
The original model and worst-case quantity are unchanged.  The kernel-checked
interface \texttt{HasReverseCubeTwoStarCompiler k a b M} requires every table
on $\{0,1\}^k\times[a]\times[b]$, with arbitrary independent labels, to have a
single score using at most $M$ affine fractions.  Every denominator is positive
on the entire $(k+a+b)$-cube and has strictly negative slopes.  The complete
score vanishes on either star's zero axis for every row input and every input
of the other star, including multihot inputs.

\texttt{worstCaseHeadComplexity\_real\_le\_of\_cube\_two\_star\_compiler}
proves, for $a,b>0$,
\[
 W(k+a+b)\leq \frac{484\,2^{a+b}M}{ab}+2^{k+a}+2^{k+b}.
\]
The exact finite cover bound is $|D\times E|M+2^{k+a}+2^{k+b}$.
The cover centers are ordered by decreasing rank.  Previously treated inputs
inside a new downset lie in its current cell or one of its two axes.  Exact
axis zeros preserve their signs; inputs outside the downset are protected by
a common affine suppression penalty and a finite strict margin.  Complementing
the local coordinates gives strictly positive global slopes.  The two positive
faces omitted by the open covers are repaired at the displayed costs.

This theorem supplies the outer stage of the filtered weight-layer proposal.
Its inner stage must first combine all row cells for one fixed pair of star
centers.  Lower-face cancellation on one-hot bit and column inputs alone does
not justify simultaneous gluing over all three sets of centers.  The full-cube
row interface avoids that invalid inference.  No improved local compiler is
asserted by this transfer theorem.
