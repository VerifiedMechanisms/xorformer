\paragraph{Status and scope.}
This note does not prove an improvement in the worst-case function
$W(n)=\texttt{HeadComplexity.worstCaseHeadComplexity}\ n$.
In particular, it proves neither a positive exponent improvement,
a superconstant saving, nor a matching lower bound for $W$.
It records the strongest applicable literature construction found,
checks its exact transfer, and gives two additional counterexamples
to proposed ways of extending that transfer. The new propositions in
this note are written proofs only. The definitions of heads,
admissibility, and $W$ are unchanged.

The supplied \texttt{scientist-report.tex} and the relevant mechanisms
in \texttt{mechanism-audit.tex} were read first. Several statements
below explicitly identify facts already established there; they are
not claimed as new results.

\section{The exact model transfer}

An admissible score is a constant plus a sum of the repository's
\texttt{FracAtom} evaluations. A sufficient condition for one affine
ratio $A/B$ to be an atom is
\[
 A=a_0+\sum_{i=1}^n a_ix_i,\qquad
 B=b_0+\sum_{i=1}^n b_ix_i,\qquad b_0,b_1,\ldots,b_n>0.
\]
This is an exact, one-head transfer, proved in the existing file
\texttt{Atoms/PositiveAffineRatio.lean}, by
\texttt{FracAtom.ofPositiveAffineRatio} and
\texttt{FracAtom.ofPositiveAffineRatio\_eval}.
In particular, positivity of this $B$ holds on the entire cube.
Positivity only on a promised sample is insufficient. The converse
description of every atom allows either a common positive slope
orientation, a common negative slope orientation, or a constant
denominator. Arbitrary mixtures of positive and negative slopes
cannot silently be substituted for the actual atom class.

All strict comparisons below have a positive uniform margin on the
finite set on which they are claimed. No zero score is treated as a
strict negative sign.

\section{A real logarithmic gain on a promised set}

Erd\H{o}s and Moser, \emph{On the representation of directed graphs
as unions of orderings}, Sections 2 and 3,
\url{https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1964-22.pdf}, prove that every
oriented graph on $m$ vertices is the majority relation of
$O(m/\log m)$ linear orders. Their proof uses pairs of orders whose
votes cancel away from prescribed unions of disjoint directed
complete bipartite graphs. This is a genuine way to store
$\Theta(m\log m)$ bits of a majority representation per ordering.

The following transfer was already proved in the supplied audit under
``Majority rankings have a promised-domain fraction compiler.'' It is
repeated here because its rate is sufficient for a \emph{local}
logarithmic gain, even though it did not meet that run's quadratic
target.

\begin{proposition}[Existing promised-grid compiler]
For positive integers $r,s$, every strict sign table on
\[
 X_{r,s}=\{(e_i,e_j):1\leq i\leq r,\ 1\leq j\leq s\}
 \subseteq\{0,1\}^{r+s}
\]
is represented there by $O((r+s)/\log(r+s))$ admissible heads.
Every denominator is admissible on the whole ambient cube.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Complete the desired directed edges from $r$ row candidates to $s$
column candidates to a tournament on their disjoint union. Let
$\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_H$ be orders whose strict majority relation is
that tournament. Reverse all orders if required to make positive
rank differences correspond to the desired positive labels.
For an integer $R>6H$, put
\[
 A_h(z,w)=\sum_{i=1}^r R^{\pi_h(i)}z_i
                -\sum_{j=1}^s R^{\pi_h(r+j)}w_j,
 \qquad
 B_h(z,w)=1+\sum_{i=1}^r R^{\pi_h(i)}z_i
                +\sum_{j=1}^s R^{\pi_h(r+j)}w_j.
\]
These are admissible positive-slope ratios by the preceding transfer.
If $a>b\geq1$, then
\[
 0<1-\frac{R^a-R^b}{1+R^a+R^b}
   =\frac{1+2R^b}{1+R^a+R^b}<\frac3R.
\]
The reverse comparison has the symmetric error bound. At each
promised pair, the sum of the fractions is consequently within
$3H/R<1/2$ of the integer majority margin, whose absolute value is
at least one. This proves every desired strict sign.
\end{proof}

For balanced $r,s$ this assigns $\Theta(n^2)$ independently chosen
signs with $O(n/\log n)$ heads. Ordinary sign rank does not give this
statement: its factors would be products of functions of the two
blocks, rather than affine numerators divided by affine denominators.
The rankings above supply the missing local transfer explicitly.

\paragraph{Why this is not a full-cube bound.}
The promised set has only $rs$ points among $2^{r+s}$ cube vertices.
All the other inputs have defined scores but unconstrained signs.
Replacing one-hot vectors by binary addresses is invalid: even the
natural rank $i=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}2^kx_k$ gives
\[
 R^i=\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}(1+(R^{2^k}-1)x_k),
\]
which has nonzero mixed difference $(R-1)(R^2-1)$ in its first two
coordinates. Hence it is not affine when $d\geq2$.
Likewise, a sign test for membership in $X_{r,s}$ is not a free
numerical mask for multiplying this score.

\section{Exact masks destroy this particular gain}

The following obstruction does not use the common-slope restriction;
it is valid even for arbitrary affine denominators positive everywhere.

\begin{proposition}[Exact Hamming-ball localization]
Let $0\leq r\leq n$. Suppose a nonzero score
\[
 F(x)=c+\sum_{h=1}^H\frac{A_h(x)}{B_h(x)}
\]
has affine numerators and denominators, with $B_h(x)>0$ on the
whole cube. If $F(x)=0$ outside a Hamming ball of radius $r$, then
$H\geq n-r$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Complement coordinates so that the ball is centered at zero. This
preserves affinity, positivity on the cube, and degree; no assertion
that it preserves individual atom admissibility is needed in this
relaxed lower-bound proof. Choose a support set $T$ of largest
cardinality at which $F(\mathbf1_T)\ne0$, and write $t=|T|\leq r$.
Every strict superset of $T$ has zero score by the choice of $T$.

Clear denominators:
\[
 D=\prod_{h=1}^H B_h,\qquad
 P=cD+\sum_{h=1}^H A_h\prod_{k\ne h}B_k.
\]
Then $D>0$ on the cube and $\deg P\leq H$.
Fix the coordinates in $T$ to one and vary the remaining $n-t$
coordinates. The restriction of $P$ is nonzero only at the zero
vertex of this face. Its full alternating sum is therefore the
nonzero value $P(\mathbf1_T)$. Any polynomial of degree less than
$n-t$ has zero full alternating sum: each monomial misses one of
the free variables, and the two terms differing in that variable
cancel. Thus $H\geq n-t\geq n-r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact product-star masks]
A nonzero score supported exactly inside a product of two
radius-one Hamming balls in disjoint coordinate blocks requires
at least $n-2$ heads. The same lower bound holds for a score
supported inside $X_{r,s}$, with $n=r+s$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Both supports are contained in a radius-two Hamming ball in the
ambient $n$ coordinates. Apply the proposition.
\end{proof}

Consequently an independent-module construction which extends each
promised table by exact zeros cannot retain its $O(n/\log n)$
cost. This excludes that specific gluing step. It does \emph{not}
exclude approximating zero, allowing cross-module cancellation, or
assigning only strict output signs globally. Those distinctions matter:
the target permits all three.

\section{Direct dominant-rank extension also has an obstruction}

One possible way to avoid masks is to evaluate the ranking ratios on
all inputs and use their limiting majority votes. On a nonempty set
of active coordinates, a dominant-rank ratio returns the label of the
first active coordinate in a fixed order. Such a rule is a decision
list with a common literal orientation. The following exact lower
bound rules out this direct extension.

\begin{proposition}[Common-orientation decision lists and parity]
Let each $g_h:\cube\to\mathbb R$ choose a real output associated
with the first coordinate with $x_i=1$ in its own ordering, and
choose a default real output if all coordinates are zero.
If $c+\sum_{h=1}^H g_h$ strictly sign-represents parity, then
\[
 H\geq\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\qquad(n\geq2).
\]
The same result holds if every list instead uses $x_i=0$ as its
active literal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $y_i=1-x_i$. For a list ordered as
$\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(n)$, with outputs $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ and default
$a_0$, its exact multilinear polynomial is
\[
 a_1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(a_{k+1}-a_k)
       \prod_{j=1}^k y_{\pi(j)}
       +(a_0-a_n)\prod_{j=1}^n y_{\pi(j)}.
\]
Indeed, if the first active coordinate has position $k$, exactly
the prefixes of lengths less than $k$ equal one, and the sum
telescopes to $a_k$. If none is active, it telescopes to $a_0$.
Thus each list has monomial support in one chain of subsets.

Write the unique multilinear polynomial of the total score as
$P(y)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}d_S\prod_{i\in S}y_i$. Strict parity
signs imply, for one fixed $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$,
$\sigma(-1)^{|T|}P(\mathbf1_T)>0$ for every $T$. Boolean
inversion gives
\[
 \sigma(-1)^{|S|}d_S
 =\sum_{T\subseteq S}\sigma(-1)^{|T|}P(\mathbf1_T)>0.
\]
Every coefficient is therefore nonzero. In particular all
$\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ monomials of the middle degree
must occur. Each chain supplies at most one of them, and the bias
supplies none when $n\geq2$. This proves the bound. Complementing
all coordinates proves the other common orientation.
\end{proof}

For clarity, this proposition does not cover a mixture of the two
literal orientations. Nor does it cover a rational score whose leading
ranking votes tie and whose smaller terms decide the sign. The
promised-grid transfer above works precisely by a nonzero integer vote
margin; extending \emph{that} transfer directly to all subsets falls
under the proposition. Rational cancellation beyond this margin
argument would be a new mechanism.

\section{Other literature routes and their exact gaps}

\paragraph{Depth-dependent memorization.}
Park, Lee, Yun, and Shin, \emph{Provable Memorization via Deep Neural
Networks using Sub-linear Parameters}, COLT 2021,
\url{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v134/park21a/park21a.pdf},
state in Theorem 1 a depth-parameter tradeoff for separated inputs.
Their gains use depth growing with sample count. Vardi, Yehudai,
and Shamir, \emph{On the Optimal Memorization Power of ReLU Neural
Networks}, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03187}, obtain nearly
square-root parameter counts using depth and high bit complexity.
The Boolean cube satisfies a separation requirement, so this is not
an input-geometry obstruction. The missing transfer is a flattening
of their successive nonlinear decoding operations into a sum of
admissible affine ratios with a quantitatively adequate head count.
No such flattening is established. A claim of its existence with
the desired bound would be a theorem-strength replacement for the
original target, so this route is blocked here.

\paragraph{Shallow threshold circuits.}
Kane and Williams, \emph{Super-Linear Gate and Super-Quadratic Wire
Lower Bounds for Depth-Two and Depth-Three Threshold Circuits},
\url{https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~dakane/depth2LTF.pdf},
Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.1, give the relevant counting mechanism
and a random-function $\Omega(2^n/n^2)$ lower bound.
This is not a universal upper construction. More decisively, a
constant-cost replacement of arbitrary hidden threshold gates by
numerical head outputs is already excluded in the supplied audit
using Sherstov's intersection-of-two-halfspaces theorem. I do not
reuse the excluded replacement. The majority-order construction is
different: it has a proved approximation to the numerical votes on
the stated promised set.

\paragraph{Sign rank.}
Alon, Moran, and Yehudayoff, \emph{Sign rank versus VC dimension},
COLT 2016, \url{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v49/alon16.html},
study low-rank sign matrices and obtain an $O(N/\log N)$
\emph{approximation factor} for computing sign rank. This factor
must not be mistaken for a universal rank bound of that order.
A decomposition $M_{ij}=\sum_k u_k(i)v_k(j)$ does not itself yield
one head per summand: arbitrary functions of a binary block are not
affine coordinate functions, and their product is not an affine
numerator. On the one-hot grid, arbitrary factors are affine within
their own blocks, but multiplication still requires a separate
compiler. The explicit ranking ratios above avoid this product
issue locally; the exact-mask obstruction prevents one proposed
global extension.

\paragraph{Rational-network dimension estimates.}
Pardo and Sebasti\'an, \emph{Erzeugungsgrad, VC-Dimension and Neural
Networks with rational activation function},
\url{https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11345v1}, bound the complexity of
parameterized families using intersection theory. These are upper
bounds on capacity, not constructions that shatter the prescribed
Boolean cube. The required transfer would be an explicit strict
orthant-surjectivity theorem for the admissible reciprocal family.
Such a theorem at the desired parameter count is at least as strong
as the target and is not supplied by a dimension estimate.

\paragraph{A November 2026 sigmoid result.}
Li, Reichman, Williams, and Wu, \emph{New Gate, Weight and Wire
Complexity Bounds for Sigmoid Neural Networks},
\url{https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21910v1}, Section 6.1, rationalize
Boolean-input sigmoid networks by exponentiating their parameters.
The resulting hidden output has the form
\[
 \frac1{1+B\prod_j W_j^{x_j}},\qquad B,W_j>0.
\]
Its denominator is generally not affine: its mixed difference in
coordinates $i,j$, with all other coordinates zero, is
$B(W_i-1)(W_j-1)$, which need not vanish. Thus its rationalization
does not transfer a gate to one atom. Lemma 4.5 supplies a general
rational approximation without poles on a real interval, but not
a decomposition into admissible affine ratios with a controlled
head count. The paper's improvement for inner product modulo two
uses growing depth, as stated after Theorem 1.7. Neither mechanism
therefore closes the current one-layer gap. Its bounded-parameter
existence theorem also supplies no universal low-head construction.

\section{A precise remaining lemma for the logarithmic route}

Here is a concrete cover to which the missing composition theorem
could apply. Write $n=a+b$, where $a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and
$b=\lceil n/2\rceil$. Complementing the existing upward-star
covers gives downward closed-star covers $\mathcal C_m$ with
$|\mathcal C_m|=O(2^m/m)$ for $m=a,b$. This uses the existing
\texttt{monomialStarCoverNumber\_spec} and
\texttt{monomialStarCoverNumber\_real\_le\_eleven}.
For each center $S\ne[m]$, select $i_S\notin S$ and add the
center $S\cup\{i_S\}$. The resulting family $\mathcal D_m$ has
size at most $2|\mathcal C_m|$. Its \emph{open} downward stars
\[
 \partial^- S=\{S\setminus\{i\}:i\in S\}
\]
cover every vertex except $[m]$: a vertex already covered as a child
remains covered, and a vertex covered only as a center is now the
child of its selected added center.

The products $\partial^-S\times\partial^-T$, with
$S\in\mathcal D_a$ and $T\in\mathcal D_b$, therefore cover
\[
 U=(\{0,1\}^a\setminus\{\mathbf1\})
       \times(\{0,1\}^b\setminus\{\mathbf1\})
\]
using $O(2^n/n^2)$ cells. On one such cell the coordinates
$y_i=1-x_i$ indexed by $S$ and $T$ are two one-hot blocks.
The local ranking formula uses these $y_i$; give every unused
coordinate an additional denominator slope $\varepsilon$ in $y_i$,
with $0<\varepsilon\leq1/n$. The baseline on the promised cell is
then between one and two. Taking $R>8H$ gives the same strict
comparison proof with error $4H/R<1/2$. On the whole cube the
denominator is one plus a sum of strictly positive multiples of
$1-x_i$, so it is a valid negative-orientation atom, using
\texttt{FracAtom.ofNegativeAffineRatio\_eval}.
Thus each cell really has a local $O(n/\log n)$-head compiler,
including cells whose centers have mixed zero and one coordinates.

The following is the \emph{unproved} missing lemma:

\begin{quote}
For the covers just defined, there is an absolute constant $K$ such
that every strict labeling $f$ of $U$ admits one admissible score
$F_{S,T}$ for each cell, each with at most $Kn/\log n$ heads,
which has the prescribed signs on its own cell, and for which
$\sum_{S,T}F_{S,T}$ has the prescribed strict signs on all of $U$.
\end{quote}

\begin{proposition}[The stated lemma would finish the logarithmic route]
If the missing lemma holds eventually in $n$, then
$W(n)=O(2^n/(n\log n))$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The total head count supplied on $U$ is the product
$O(2^n/n^2)O(n/\log n)=O(2^n/(n\log n))$.
It remains to justify, rather than assume, the negligible cost of
the two excluded faces. On the face with first block all ones,
interpolate the difference between the desired values $f\in\{-1,1\}$
and the existing score by a multilinear polynomial in the other
$b$ coordinates. It has at most $2^b$ monomials. Multiplying by
the monomial in all first-block coordinates extends this correction
by zero outside the face. Correct the second exceptional face
in the same way with at most $2^a$ monomials. Its prescribed
correction at the intersection is zero because that point was
already corrected. The resulting formal sum has the correct
strict signs everywhere.

Every Boolean monomial $x_V=\prod_{i\in V}x_i$ can be approximated
uniformly by one admissible head:
\[
 q_{V,\eta}(x)=
 \frac{\eta}{\eta+\sum_{i\in V}(1-x_i)
                 +\eta^2\sum_{i\notin V}(1-x_i)},
 \qquad\eta>0.
\]
Every variable slope is strictly negative and the denominator's
minimum on the cube is $\eta>0$. As $\eta\to0$, the ratio tends
to one where all coordinates in $V$ are one and to zero otherwise.
The convergence is uniform because the cube is finite. Apply this
to the finitely many correction monomials, absorbing their real
coefficients into their affine numerators. Sufficiently small
$\eta$ makes the total error smaller than half the minimum strict
margin. At most $2^a+2^b$ additional heads suffice.
Finally $2^a+2^b=o(2^n/(n\log n))$ for the balanced split.
\end{proof}

This conditional proposition is not a claimed asymptotic result.
The missing lemma has comparable strength to the logarithmic target,
so the route is blocked there. Its exact-zero interpretation is
false by the localization proposition. Its direct common-orientation
dominant-vote interpretation is false by the parity proposition.
Approximate masking with controlled interference or genuinely global
cancellation would have to supply the new part. No such construction
was obtained.
