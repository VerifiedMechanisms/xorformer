\paragraph{Status and scope.}
This note does not prove an improvement in the worst-case function
$W(n)=\texttt{HeadComplexity.worstCaseHeadComplexity}\ n$.
In particular, it proves neither a positive exponent improvement,
a superconstant saving, nor a matching lower bound for $W$.
It records the strongest applicable literature construction found,
checks its exact transfer, and proves several obstructions to proposed
ways of extending that transfer. The new propositions in this note
are written proofs, except for the partial-mask and singleton-degree
facts identified below that have also been checked in Lean. The definitions of heads,
admissibility, and $W$ are unchanged.

The supplied \texttt{scientist-report.tex} and the relevant mechanisms
in \texttt{mechanism-audit.tex} were read first. Several statements
below explicitly identify facts already established there; they are
not claimed as new results.

\section{The exact model transfer}

An admissible score is a constant plus a sum of the repository's
\texttt{FracAtom} evaluations. A sufficient condition for one affine
ratio $A/B$ to be an atom is
\[
 A=a_0+\sum_{i=1}^n a_ix_i,\qquad
 B=b_0+\sum_{i=1}^n b_ix_i,\qquad b_0,b_1,\ldots,b_n>0.
\]
This is an exact, one-head transfer, proved in the existing file
\texttt{Atoms/PositiveAffineRatio.lean}, by
\texttt{FracAtom.ofPositiveAffineRatio} and
\texttt{FracAtom.ofPositiveAffineRatio\_eval}.
In particular, positivity of this $B$ holds on the entire cube.
Positivity only on a promised sample is insufficient. The converse
description of every atom allows either a common positive slope
orientation, a common negative slope orientation, or a constant
denominator. Arbitrary mixtures of positive and negative slopes
cannot silently be substituted for the actual atom class.

All strict comparisons below have a positive uniform margin on the
finite set on which they are claimed. No zero score is treated as a
strict negative sign.

\section{A real logarithmic gain on a promised set}

Erd\H{o}s and Moser, \emph{On the representation of directed graphs
as unions of orderings}, Sections 2 and 3,
\url{https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1964-22.pdf}, prove that every
oriented graph on $m$ vertices is the majority relation of
$O(m/\log m)$ linear orders. Their proof uses pairs of orders whose
votes cancel away from prescribed unions of disjoint directed
complete bipartite graphs. This is a genuine way to store
$\Theta(m\log m)$ bits of a majority representation per ordering.

The following transfer was already proved in the supplied audit under
``Majority rankings have a promised-domain fraction compiler.'' It is
repeated here because its rate is sufficient for a \emph{local}
logarithmic gain, even though it did not meet that run's quadratic
target.

\begin{proposition}[Existing promised-grid compiler]
For positive integers $r,s$, every strict sign table on
\[
 X_{r,s}=\{(e_i,e_j):1\leq i\leq r,\ 1\leq j\leq s\}
 \subseteq\{0,1\}^{r+s}
\]
is represented there by $O((r+s)/\log(r+s))$ admissible heads.
Every denominator is admissible on the whole ambient cube.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Complete the desired directed edges from $r$ row candidates to $s$
column candidates to a tournament on their disjoint union. Let
$\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_H$ be orders whose strict majority relation is
that tournament. Reverse all orders if required to make positive
rank differences correspond to the desired positive labels.
For an integer $R>6H$, put
\[
 A_h(z,w)=\sum_{i=1}^r R^{\pi_h(i)}z_i
                -\sum_{j=1}^s R^{\pi_h(r+j)}w_j,
 \qquad
 B_h(z,w)=1+\sum_{i=1}^r R^{\pi_h(i)}z_i
                +\sum_{j=1}^s R^{\pi_h(r+j)}w_j.
\]
These are admissible positive-slope ratios by the preceding transfer.
If $a>b\geq1$, then
\[
 0<1-\frac{R^a-R^b}{1+R^a+R^b}
   =\frac{1+2R^b}{1+R^a+R^b}<\frac3R.
\]
The reverse comparison has the symmetric error bound. At each
promised pair, the sum of the fractions is consequently within
$3H/R<1/2$ of the integer majority margin, whose absolute value is
at least one. This proves every desired strict sign.
\end{proof}

For balanced $r,s$ this assigns $\Theta(n^2)$ independently chosen
signs with $O(n/\log n)$ heads. Ordinary sign rank does not give this
statement: its factors would be products of functions of the two
blocks, rather than affine numerators divided by affine denominators.
The rankings above supply the missing local transfer explicitly.

\begin{proposition}[Three-head cancellation at the lower local layers]
For $p>0$ and $u,v\geq0$, define
\[
 J_p(u,v)=\frac{u-v}{p+u+v}-\frac{u}{p+u}+\frac{v}{p+v}.
\]
Then
\[
 J_p(u,v)=\frac{(u-v)uv}{(p+u+v)(p+u)(p+v)}.
\]
It vanishes when $u=0$ or $v=0$. If $1\leq p\leq2$,
$u=R^a$, $v=R^b$, $a,b\geq1$ are distinct integers, and $R\geq2$,
then
$|J_p(u,v)-\operatorname{sgn}(a-b)|\leq7/R$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Subtracting the last two displayed fractions from the first uses
\[
 \frac{u}{p+u}-\frac{v}{p+v}
 =\frac{p(u-v)}{(p+u)(p+v)},\qquad
 (p+u)(p+v)-p(p+u+v)=uv,
\]
which proves the identity and the vanishing. By symmetry take $a>b$.
For $Q=(u-v)/(p+u+v)$ one has $0<Q<1$ and
$1-Q=(p+2v)/(p+u+v)\leq3/R$, since $a\geq b+1\geq2$.
Also, with $M=uv/((p+u)(p+v))$,
\[
 0<M<1,\qquad
 1-M\leq\frac{p}{u}+\frac{p}{v}\leq\frac4R.
\]
The identity gives $J_p=QM$, and
$1-QM\leq(1-Q)+(1-M)\leq7/R$.
\end{proof}

This is an actual three-ratio construction on the whole cube. For
example take $u,v$ to be nonnegative linear combinations of disjoint
blocks of $y_i=1-x_i$, and put
$p(x)=1+\varepsilon\sum_i y_i$ with $0<\varepsilon\leq1/n$.
All three denominators then have strictly negative $x$-slopes,
are everywhere positive, and satisfy $1\leq p\leq2$.
Summing these expressions over rankings retains a strict majority
margin if $R>14H$. It also vanishes exactly when either local block
has no deletions. This removes the center and first-deletion layers
without assuming a numerical indicator gate.

\paragraph{Adversarial check on triangular gluing.}
One might next try to process cells by cardinality, preserving already
fixed higher layers by localizing this expression to the downset of
its center. The direct localization denominator adds a large term
$T\sum_{j\notin S}x_j$. Its slopes are positive outside $S$ but
negative in the active deletion coordinates inside $S$. Such a
denominator is not an admissible atom, even when it is positive on
the whole cube. This explicit proposed triangular compiler therefore
fails. No constant-cost admissible replacement of that step is claimed.

\begin{proposition}[A concrete unmasked repair fails]
Take six coordinates, a first block $\{1,2,3\}$, a second block
$\{4,5,6\}$, and downward-star centers $S=\{1,2\}$ and
$T=\{4,5\}$. Let
\[
 u(x)=R^2(1-x_1)+R^4(1-x_2),\qquad
 v(x)=R(1-x_4)+R^3(1-x_5),\qquad
 p(x)=1+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^6(1-x_i),
\]
where $R>1$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Write $J(x)=J_{p(x)}(u(x),v(x))$.
For the vertices with supports
\[
 x=\{2,5\},\qquad y=\{2,3,5,6\},
\]
no real scalar $\lambda$ makes $-1+\lambda J(x)>0$ and
$-1+\lambda J(y)<0$ simultaneously.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At both points $u=R^2>v=R>0$. The first lies in
$\partial^-S\times\partial^-T$, whereas the second lies outside
the downset of its center. Moreover
$p(x)=1+4\varepsilon>1+2\varepsilon=p(y)>0$.
The exact identity for $J_p$ has a fixed positive numerator and
a product of three positive factors strictly increasing with $p$.
Consequently $0<J(x)<J(y)$. The first requested inequality forces
$\lambda>1/J(x)>0$, and hence $\lambda J(y)>1$, contradicting
the second inequality.
\end{proof}

This counterexample tests one positive-ranking correction against a
specified residual. It does not rule out signed sums of rankings,
additional affine corrections, function-dependent overlap choices,
or general rational cancellations.

The exact identity, axis cancellations, positivity, strict decrease
with the baseline, and resulting scalar-repair obstruction are also
Lean-checked in \texttt{Results/PartialRankingMask.lean}, as
\texttt{partialRankingMask\_eq},
\texttt{partialRankingMask\_zero\_left},
\texttt{partialRankingMask\_zero\_right},
\texttt{partialRankingMask\_pos},
\texttt{partialRankingMask\_strictAnti\_parameter}, and
\texttt{not\_partialRankingMask\_scalar\_repair}.
The $7/R$ approximation estimate and the six-coordinate instantiation
above remain written proofs.

\paragraph{Why this is not a full-cube bound.}
The promised set has only $rs$ points among $2^{r+s}$ cube vertices.
All the other inputs have defined scores but unconstrained signs.
Replacing one-hot vectors by binary addresses is invalid: even the
natural rank $i=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}2^kx_k$ gives
\[
 R^i=\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}(1+(R^{2^k}-1)x_k),
\]
which has nonzero mixed difference $(R-1)(R^2-1)$ in its first two
coordinates. Hence it is not affine when $d\geq2$.
Likewise, a sign test for membership in $X_{r,s}$ is not a free
numerical mask for multiplying this score.

\section{Reversing the grid denominator orientation has a cost}

An alternative to the forbidden mixed-slope gate would replace the
positive-orientation grid compiler by negative-orientation heads
before localization. The following bounds address the precise
numerical replacement step: approximating one ranking vote uniformly
by negative-orientation heads. They do not preclude representing an
arbitrary grid labeling directly by a different construction.

Write $s_{ij}=1$ if $i\geq j$ and $s_{ij}=-1$ otherwise, for
$1\leq i,j\leq m$. This table is the ranking comparison of the
interleaved row and column candidates. On the two-one-hot grid, a
negative-orientation denominator has the form
\[
 B_{ij}=c-b_i-d_j,\qquad b_i,d_j\geq0,\qquad
 c>\sum_i b_i+\sum_jd_j.
\]
The last inequality is required by positivity on the entire cube,
not merely the promised grid. Allowing zero slopes enlarges the
actual negative-orientation class and also includes constant
denominators, so it is legitimate for these lower bounds.

\begin{proposition}[One opposite-orientation ratio cannot saturate a ranking]
Let $m\geq3$. Suppose $F_{ij}=(a_0+a_i+e_j)/B_{ij}$ with the
denominator conditions above. If
$|F_{ij}-s_{ij}|\leq\varepsilon$ for every $i,j$, then
\[
 \varepsilon>\frac{m-2}{2m-3}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $A_{ij}=a_0+a_i+e_j$. In the rectangle with rows and columns
$i,i+1$, only the upper-right target sign is negative. Both $A$ and
$B$ satisfy the additive rectangle identity. Multiplying the four
approximation inequalities by their positive denominators and
using that identity gives
\[
 2B_{i,i+1}
 \leq\varepsilon(B_{ii}+B_{i+1,i+1}+B_{i,i+1}+B_{i+1,i})
 =2\varepsilon(B_{ii}+B_{i+1,i+1}).
\]
Indeed, the alternating rectangle sum of the four target values
$s_{kl}B_{kl}$ is $2B_{i,i+1}$, whereas that of $A$ is zero.
Set $k=m-1$ and
$E=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}b_i+\sum_{j=2}^m d_j<c$.
Summing the preceding inequalities gives
\[
 kc-E\leq\varepsilon
   \sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(B_{ii}+B_{i+1,i+1})
 \leq\varepsilon(2kc-E).
\]
The second inequality follows because each coefficient included in
$E$ is subtracted at least once in the sum of diagonal denominators.
Here $\varepsilon\geq0$ follows from its definition as an error
bound. Thus
\[
 \varepsilon\geq\frac{kc-E}{2kc-E}
 >\frac{k-1}{2k-1}=\frac{m-2}{2m-3},
\]
where the strict inequality uses $E<c$ and $k\geq2$.
\end{proof}

The next proposition allows arbitrarily large affine numerators,
arbitrary signs of the summands, and severe cancellation. No bound
on an individual head's magnitude is assumed.

\begin{proposition}[A bounded number of opposite-orientation heads is insufficient]
Let $H\geq1$ and $0\leq\varepsilon<1$. Suppose a bias plus $H$
affine ratios, all with negative-orientation denominators positive
on the entire $2m$-dimensional cube, gives a score satisfying
$|F(e_i,e_j)-s_{ij}|\leq\varepsilon$ on the grid. If $m\geq8H$,
then
\[
 \frac m2\leq
 \left(1+\frac{2(1+\varepsilon)}{1-\varepsilon}\right)^{H2^H}.
\]
Consequently, for any fixed $\varepsilon<1$, such a numerical
ranking compiler needs $H=\Omega(\log\log m)$ heads.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write the $h$th denominator as
$c_h-b_{hi}-d_{hj}$ on the grid, with nonnegative slopes and
$c_h>\sum_i b_{hi}+\sum_jd_{hj}$.
Absorb the global bias into the numerator of the first head;
this preserves its affine form and the head count. Define
\[
 t_i=\sum_{h=1}^H\frac{b_{hi}+d_{hi}}{c_h}.
\]
Then $\sum_i t_i<H$. The set
$I=\{i:t_i\leq2H/m\}$ has cardinality at least $m/2$.
For $i,j\in I$, put
\[
 D_{ij}=\prod_{h=1}^H
 \left(1-\frac{b_{hi}}{c_h}-\frac{d_{hj}}{c_h}\right).
\]
Every factor belongs to $(0,1]$, and
\[
 \sum_{h=1}^H\left(\frac{b_{hi}}{c_h}+\frac{d_{hj}}{c_h}\right)
 \leq t_i+t_j\leq\frac{4H}{m}\leq\frac12.
\]
The elementary inequality $\prod_h(1-u_h)\geq1-\sum_hu_h$
for $0\leq u_h\leq1$ proves $1/2\leq D_{ij}\leq1$.

Let $P_{ij}=D_{ij}F(e_i,e_j)$ on the retained subgrid. Clearing
denominators expresses $P$ as a sum of $H$ matrices, each an
entrywise product of one additive numerator matrix and $H-1$
additive denominator matrices. Each additive matrix has rank at
most two. If $M=\sum_{u=1}^r a_u b_u^{\mathsf T}$ and
$N=\sum_{v=1}^s c_vd_v^{\mathsf T}$, their entrywise product
equals $\sum_{u,v}(a_u\circ c_v)(b_u\circ d_v)^{\mathsf T}$,
so its rank is at most $rs$. Therefore
$\operatorname{rank}P\leq H2^H$.

Set $L=(1-\varepsilon)/2>0$ and $U=1+\varepsilon$.
The approximation and denominator bounds imply
\[
 |P_{ij}|\leq U,\qquad s_{ij}P_{ij}\geq L.
\]
List $I$ in increasing order. Any two different rows of $P$ are
at distance at least $2L$ in the maximum norm: if their indices
are $i<j$, the column $j$ has opposite prescribed signs in those
rows. Let $r$ be the dimension of the row space. In this
$r$-dimensional normed space, the open balls of radius $L$ about
the $|I|$ row vectors are disjoint and lie in the ball of radius
$U+L$ about zero. Comparing their $r$-dimensional volumes gives
\[
 |I|\leq\left(1+\frac UL\right)^r
 \leq\left(1+\frac{2(1+\varepsilon)}{1-\varepsilon}\right)^{H2^H}.
\]
Together with $|I|\geq m/2$, this proves the finite bound.
For fixed $\varepsilon$, use $H2^H\leq4^H$ and take two
logarithms. This gives $H=\Omega(\log\log m)$ when $m\geq8H$;
the alternative $m<8H$ gives the stronger $H>m/8$.
\end{proof}

Thus a constant-factor replacement of each existing positive-slope
ranking ratio by uniformly accurate negative-slope numerical votes
is impossible, even with unbounded cancellation. This does not rule
out a direct negative-orientation compiler of many ranking votes
together, a compiler with a slowly growing overhead, or a different
global construction. Sign-only comparison is easy: the affine
numerator $i-j+1/2$ on the grid already gives these signs with one
constant-denominator head. The obstruction concerns bounded numerical
votes suitable for summation.

With $L=O(m/\log m)$ majority orders, an integer vote margin may
be only one. Uniform error less than $1/(2L)$ per compiled order
is sufficient to preserve that margin, whereas fixed constant
error alone does not justify their summation. At the uniform
accuracy $O(\log m/m)$ adequate for the worst-case number of orders,
the opposite-orientation cost is much larger than the preceding
constant-accuracy lower bound.

\begin{proposition}[Accurate opposite-orientation votes require logarithmically many heads]
Under the preceding proposition's hypotheses with $m\geq8H$,
\[
 H2^H\geq\frac m2-(m\varepsilon+4H)^2.
\]
In particular, for every fixed $A>0$, if
$\varepsilon\leq A\log_2m/m$, then for all sufficiently large $m$,
\[
 H\geq\log_2m-\log_2\log_2m-\log_2 3.
\]
The eventual conclusion holds without the hypothesis $m\geq8H$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the retained subgrid and cleared matrix $P$ from the preceding
proof. Let $l=|I|\geq m/2$, and let $T$ be its comparator sign
matrix, with rows and columns in increasing order. The earlier
bound actually gives $1-4H/m\leq D_{ij}\leq1$. Therefore
\[
 |P_{ij}-T_{ij}|
 =|D_{ij}(F_{ij}-T_{ij})+(D_{ij}-1)T_{ij}|
 \leq\varepsilon+4H/m,
\]
and hence $\|P-T\|_{\mathrm F}^2\leq(m\varepsilon+4H)^2$.

Every singular value of $T$ is at least one. To verify the exact
bound without a spectral formula, write $y=Tx$. For $l\geq2$,
\[
 2x_1=y_1+y_l,\qquad 2x_i=y_i-y_{i-1}\quad(2\leq i\leq l).
\]
Applying $(a\pm b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2$ and summing gives
$4\|x\|_2^2\leq4\|y\|_2^2$. For $l=1$ the claim is direct.
If $v_1,\ldots,v_d$ is an orthonormal basis of the kernel of
$P$, then
\[
 d\leq\sum_{k=1}^d\|Tv_k\|_2^2
   =\sum_{k=1}^d\|(T-P)v_k\|_2^2
   \leq\|T-P\|_{\mathrm F}^2.
\]
The last inequality follows by extending these vectors to an
orthonormal basis of $\mathbb R^l$ and expressing the squared
Frobenius norm in that basis. Thus
\[
 H2^H\geq\operatorname{rank}P=l-d
 \geq\frac m2-(m\varepsilon+4H)^2,
\]
as claimed.

For the final assertion, $H>\log_2m$ already implies the desired
bound. Otherwise, for sufficiently large $m$ one has $m\geq8H$,
and the squared error is at most
$(A+4)^2(\log_2m)^2=o(m)$. The finite inequality gives
$H2^H\geq m/3$. Taking binary logarithms and using
$\log_2H\leq\log_2\log_2m$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

Thus separately replacing every numerical ranking vote to this
accuracy incurs a logarithmic head cost and erases the known local
logarithmic saving. This statement permits unrestricted numerator
sizes and cancellation within each replacement. It does not rule
out jointly compiling all votes, proving larger majority margins
with an adequate head count, or constructing the desired local
sign table directly.

There is an opposite-orientation axis-zero identity. For $u,v\geq0$
and $p>u+v$,
\[
 \frac{u-v}{p-u-v}-\frac{u}{p-u}+\frac{v}{p-v}
 =\frac{(u-v)uv}{(p-u-v)(p-u)(p-v)}.
\]
It follows by the same common-denominator calculation as before.
Taking $u,v$ as nonnegative linear forms and choosing $p$ larger
than their total cube weight gives positive denominators throughout
the cube, negative slopes in the active variables, exact zeros
on both axes, and the correct strict comparison signs. It does
not supply uniform numerical saturation: when $u,v$ are much
smaller than $p$, the value has order $uv(u-v)/p^3$.

\section{Exact masks destroy this particular gain}

The following obstruction does not use the common-slope restriction;
it is valid even for arbitrary affine denominators positive everywhere.

\begin{proposition}[Exact Hamming-ball localization]
Let $r$ be an integer with $0\leq r\leq n$. Suppose a score
\[
 F(x)=c+\sum_{h=1}^H\frac{A_h(x)}{B_h(x)}
\]
has affine numerators and denominators, is nonzero at some cube
vertex, and satisfies $B_h(x)>0$ on the whole cube. If $F(x)=0$
outside a Hamming ball of radius $r$, then
$H\geq n-r$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Complement coordinates so that the ball is centered at zero. This
preserves affinity, positivity on the cube, and degree; no assertion
that it preserves individual atom admissibility is needed in this
relaxed lower-bound proof. Choose a support set $T$ of largest
cardinality at which $F(\mathbf1_T)\ne0$, and write $t=|T|\leq r$.
Every strict superset of $T$ has zero score by the choice of $T$.

Clear denominators:
\[
 D=\prod_{h=1}^H B_h,\qquad
 P=cD+\sum_{h=1}^H A_h\prod_{k\ne h}B_k.
\]
Then $D>0$ on the cube and $\deg P\leq H$.
Fix the coordinates in $T$ to one and vary the remaining $n-t$
coordinates. The restriction of $P$ is nonzero only at the zero
vertex of this face. Its full alternating sum is therefore the
nonzero value $P(\mathbf1_T)$. Any polynomial of degree less than
$n-t$ has zero full alternating sum: each monomial misses one of
the free variables, and the two terms differing in that variable
cancel. Thus $H\geq n-t\geq n-r$.
\end{proof}

The exact singleton core is also Lean-checked in
\texttt{Results/ExactMaskDegree.lean} as
\texttt{le\_totalDegree\_of\_cube\_supported\_at\_zero},
\texttt{le\_heads\_of\_clearedAffinePoly\_supported\_at\_zero}, and
\texttt{le\_heads\_of\_atom\_score\_supported\_at\_zero}.
The last declaration directly treats the repository's admissible
atoms. The restriction to a face at a maximal support vertex, which
extends the result to a radius-$r$ ball, remains written only.

\begin{corollary}[Exact product-star masks]
A nonzero score supported exactly inside a product of two
radius-one Hamming balls in disjoint coordinate blocks requires
at least $n-2$ heads. The same lower bound holds for a score
supported inside $X_{r,s}$, with $n=r+s$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Both supports are contained in a radius-two Hamming ball in the
ambient $n$ coordinates. Apply the proposition.
\end{proof}

Consequently an independent-module construction which extends each
promised table by exact zeros cannot retain its $O(n/\log n)$
cost. This excludes that specific gluing step. It does \emph{not}
exclude approximating zero, allowing cross-module cancellation, or
assigning only strict output signs globally. Those distinctions matter:
the target permits all three.

\section{Direct dominant-rank extension also has an obstruction}

One possible way to avoid masks is to evaluate the ranking ratios on
all inputs and use their limiting majority votes. On a nonempty set
of active coordinates, a dominant-rank ratio returns the label of the
first active coordinate in a fixed order. Such a rule is a decision
list with a common literal orientation. The following exact lower
bound rules out this direct extension.

\begin{proposition}[Common-orientation decision lists and parity]
Let each $g_h:\cube\to\mathbb R$ choose a real output associated
with the first coordinate with $x_i=1$ in its own ordering, and
choose a default real output if all coordinates are zero.
If $c+\sum_{h=1}^H g_h$ strictly sign-represents parity, then
\[
 H\geq\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\qquad(n\geq2).
\]
The same result holds if every list instead uses $x_i=0$ as its
active literal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $y_i=1-x_i$. For a list ordered as
$\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(n)$, with outputs $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ and default
$a_0$, its exact multilinear polynomial is
\[
 a_1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(a_{k+1}-a_k)
       \prod_{j=1}^k y_{\pi(j)}
       +(a_0-a_n)\prod_{j=1}^n y_{\pi(j)}.
\]
Indeed, if the first active coordinate has position $k$, exactly
the prefixes of lengths less than $k$ equal one, and the sum
telescopes to $a_k$. If none is active, it telescopes to $a_0$.
Thus each list has monomial support in one chain of subsets.

Write the unique multilinear polynomial of the total score as
$P(y)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}d_S\prod_{i\in S}y_i$. Strict parity
signs imply, for one fixed $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$,
$\sigma(-1)^{|T|}P(\mathbf1_T)>0$ for every $T$. Boolean
inversion gives
\[
 \sigma(-1)^{|S|}d_S
 =\sum_{T\subseteq S}\sigma(-1)^{|T|}P(\mathbf1_T)>0.
\]
Every coefficient is therefore nonzero. In particular all
$\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ monomials of the middle degree
must occur. Each chain supplies at most one of them, and the bias
supplies none when $n\geq2$. This proves the bound. Complementing
all coordinates proves the other common orientation.
\end{proof}

For clarity, this proposition does not cover a mixture of the two
literal orientations. Nor does it cover a rational score whose leading
ranking votes tie and whose smaller terms decide the sign. The
promised-grid transfer above works precisely by a nonzero integer vote
margin; extending \emph{that} transfer directly to all subsets falls
under the proposition. Rational cancellation beyond this margin
argument would be a new mechanism.

\section{Other literature routes and their exact gaps}

\paragraph{Depth-dependent memorization.}
Park, Lee, Yun, and Shin, \emph{Provable Memorization via Deep Neural
Networks using Sub-linear Parameters}, COLT 2021,
\url{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v134/park21a/park21a.pdf},
state in Theorem 1 a depth-parameter tradeoff for separated inputs.
Their gains use depth growing with sample count. Vardi, Yehudai,
and Shamir, \emph{On the Optimal Memorization Power of ReLU Neural
Networks}, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03187}, obtain nearly
square-root parameter counts using depth and high bit complexity.
The Boolean cube satisfies a separation requirement, so this is not
an input-geometry obstruction. The missing transfer is a flattening
of their successive nonlinear decoding operations into a sum of
admissible affine ratios with a quantitatively adequate head count.
No such flattening is established. A claim of its existence with
the desired bound would be a theorem-strength replacement for the
original target, so this route is blocked here.

\paragraph{Shallow threshold circuits.}
Kane and Williams, \emph{Super-Linear Gate and Super-Quadratic Wire
Lower Bounds for Depth-Two and Depth-Three Threshold Circuits},
\url{https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~dakane/depth2LTF.pdf},
Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.1, give the relevant counting mechanism
and a random-function $\Omega(2^n/n^2)$ lower bound.
This is not a universal upper construction. More decisively, a
constant-cost replacement of arbitrary hidden threshold gates by
numerical head outputs is already excluded in the supplied audit
using Sherstov's intersection-of-two-halfspaces theorem. I do not
reuse the excluded replacement. The majority-order construction is
different: it has a proved approximation to the numerical votes on
the stated promised set.

\paragraph{Sign rank.}
Alon, Moran, and Yehudayoff, \emph{Sign rank versus VC dimension},
COLT 2016, \url{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v49/alon16.html},
study low-rank sign matrices and obtain an $O(N/\log N)$
\emph{approximation factor} for computing sign rank. This factor
must not be mistaken for a universal rank bound of that order.
A decomposition $M_{ij}=\sum_k u_k(i)v_k(j)$ does not itself yield
one head per summand: arbitrary functions of a binary block are not
affine coordinate functions, and their product is not an affine
numerator. On the one-hot grid, arbitrary factors are affine within
their own blocks, but multiplication still requires a separate
compiler. The explicit ranking ratios above avoid this product
issue locally; the exact-mask obstruction prevents one proposed
global extension.

\paragraph{Rational-network dimension estimates.}
Pardo and Sebasti\'an, \emph{Erzeugungsgrad, VC-Dimension and Neural
Networks with rational activation function},
\url{https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11345v1}, bound the complexity of
parameterized families using intersection theory. These are upper
bounds on capacity, not constructions that shatter the prescribed
Boolean cube. The required transfer would be an explicit strict
orthant-surjectivity theorem for the admissible reciprocal family.
Such a theorem at the desired parameter count is at least as strong
as the target and is not supplied by a dimension estimate.

\paragraph{A November 2026 sigmoid result.}
Li, Reichman, Williams, and Wu, \emph{New Gate, Weight and Wire
Complexity Bounds for Sigmoid Neural Networks},
\url{https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21910v1}, Section 6.1, rationalize
Boolean-input sigmoid networks by exponentiating their parameters.
The resulting hidden output has the form
\[
 \frac1{1+B\prod_j W_j^{x_j}},\qquad B,W_j>0.
\]
Its denominator is generally not affine: its mixed difference in
coordinates $i,j$, with all other coordinates zero, is
$B(W_i-1)(W_j-1)$, which need not vanish. Thus its rationalization
does not transfer a gate to one atom. Lemma 4.5 supplies a general
rational approximation without poles on a real interval, but not
a decomposition into admissible affine ratios with a controlled
head count. The paper's improvement for inner product modulo two
uses growing depth, as stated after Theorem 1.7. Neither mechanism
therefore closes the current one-layer gap. Its bounded-parameter
existence theorem also supplies no universal low-head construction.

\section{A precise remaining local lemma}

Here is a concrete cover to which the missing composition theorem
could apply. Write $n=a+b$, where $a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and
$b=\lceil n/2\rceil$. Complementing the existing upward-star
covers gives downward closed-star covers $\mathcal C_m$ with
$|\mathcal C_m|=O(2^m/m)$ for $m=a,b$. This uses the existing
\texttt{monomialStarCoverNumber\_spec} and
\texttt{monomialStarCoverNumber\_real\_le\_eleven}.
For each center $S\ne[m]$, select $i_S\notin S$ and add the
center $S\cup\{i_S\}$. The resulting family $\mathcal D_m$ has
size at most $2|\mathcal C_m|$. Its \emph{open} downward stars
\[
 \partial^- S=\{S\setminus\{i\}:i\in S\}
\]
cover every vertex except $[m]$: a vertex already covered as a child
remains covered, and a vertex covered only as a center is now the
child of its selected added center.

The products $\partial^-S\times\partial^-T$, with
$S\in\mathcal D_a$ and $T\in\mathcal D_b$, therefore cover
\[
 U=(\{0,1\}^a\setminus\{\mathbf1\})
       \times(\{0,1\}^b\setminus\{\mathbf1\})
\]
using $O(2^n/n^2)$ cells. On one such cell the coordinates
$y_i=1-x_i$ indexed by $S$ and $T$ are two one-hot blocks.
The local ranking formula uses these $y_i$; give every unused
coordinate an additional denominator slope $\varepsilon$ in $y_i$,
with $0<\varepsilon\leq1/n$. The baseline on the promised cell is
then between one and two. Taking $R>8H$ gives the same strict
comparison proof with error $4H/R<1/2$. On the whole cube the
denominator is one plus a sum of strictly positive multiples of
$1-x_i$, so it is a valid negative-orientation atom, using
\texttt{FracAtom.ofNegativeAffineRatio\_eval}.
Thus each cell really has a local $O(n/\log n)$-head compiler,
including cells whose centers have mixed zero and one coordinates.
Its orientation is negative in $x$, however. The new finite gluing
theorem in \texttt{exponent-mechanisms.tex}, entitled ``Gluing
positive-slope axis-zero modules,'' proves global sign compatibility
when local denominators instead have positive $x$-slopes and the
local score vanishes whenever either deletion block is empty,
throughout the center's downset. It uses no additional heads.
Its complete written proof was reviewed independently: descending
center cardinality, exact axis zeros, and a positive denominator
penalty outside the current downset preserve every protected strict
sign. This is a proved composition step, not an assumed one.

Consequently the remaining requirement can be stated entirely
locally. One explicit version that suffices for a fallback is the
following \emph{unproved} lemma:

\begin{quote}
There is an absolute $K>0$ such that for every $r,s\geq1$ and every
table $f:[r]\times[s]\to\{-1,1\}$ there is a bias plus at most
\[
 K\left(1+\frac{r+s}{\log\log(r+s+4)}\right)
\]
affine ratios in $(z,w)\in\{0,1\}^{r+s}$, with all denominator
slopes strictly negative and all denominators positive on that
whole cube, whose score $F$ satisfies
$f(i,j)F(e_i,e_j)>0$ for all $i,j$, and
$F(0,w)=F(z,0)=0$ for every Boolean $z,w$.
\end{quote}

This is the reverse-orientation, axis-zero local compiler. Natural
logarithms are used in its displayed rate; the added four makes the
iterated logarithm positive in every stated dimension.

It would also suffice to allow nonpositive slopes in this missing
lemma. After complementing the deletion variables, run the finite
gluing proof first with weakly nonnegative $x$-slopes. The same
penalty preserves weak orientation and positive denominators.
Once the finite sum has a positive strict margin on $U$, replace
each resulting denominator $B(x)$ by
$B(x)+\delta\sum_i x_i$. For $\delta>0$ its slopes become
strictly positive and its positive constant term is unchanged.
As $\delta\to0$ the scores converge pointwise, hence uniformly
on the finite cube. Choosing $\delta$ below the positive margin
threshold preserves the required signs with the same head count.
Exact axis zeros are needed during gluing, but need not survive
this final perturbation. This observation relaxes a hypothesis of
the unproved local lemma; it does not change atom admissibility.

\begin{proposition}[The local lemma would give an explicit superconstant saving]
If the missing local lemma holds, then
$W(n)=O(2^n/(n\log\log n))$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In a nonempty cell use the lemma with $r=|S|$, $s=|T|$,
$z_i=1-x_i$ on $S$, and $w_j=1-x_j$ on $T$.
After this substitution all active-coordinate denominator slopes
are strictly positive. Extend each affine numerator independently
of coordinates outside $S\cup T$ and give those coordinates any
strictly positive denominator slopes. On the center's downset
these outside coordinates are zero, so neither the score nor its
axis zeros change. The extended denominator is positive on the
whole ambient cube because its constant term and all slopes are
positive.

Uniformly for $k=r+s\leq n$, the displayed local rate is
$O(n/\log\log n)$. For $k\leq\sqrt n$, use the positive lower
bound for $\log\log(k+4)$ to bound the rate by $O(\sqrt n)$.
For $k>\sqrt n$, use
$\log\log(k+4)\geq\log\log\sqrt n
=\log\log n-\log2$ and $k\leq n$.
Apply the proved finite gluing theorem to the product-star cover.
The total number of heads supplied on $U$ is therefore
\[
 O(2^n/n^2)O(n/\log\log n)=O(2^n/(n\log\log n)).
\]
It remains to justify, rather than assume, the negligible cost of
the two excluded faces. On the face with first block all ones,
interpolate the difference between the desired values $f\in\{-1,1\}$
and the existing score by a multilinear polynomial in the other
$b$ coordinates. It has at most $2^b$ monomials. Multiplying by
the monomial in all first-block coordinates extends this correction
by zero outside the face. Correct the second exceptional face
in the same way with at most $2^a$ monomials. Its prescribed
correction at the intersection is zero because that point was
already corrected. The resulting formal sum has the correct
strict signs everywhere.

Every Boolean monomial $x_V=\prod_{i\in V}x_i$ can be approximated
uniformly by one admissible head:
\[
 q_{V,\eta}(x)=
 \frac{\eta}{\eta+\sum_{i\in V}(1-x_i)
                 +\eta^2\sum_{i\notin V}(1-x_i)},
 \qquad\eta>0.
\]
Every variable slope is strictly negative and the denominator's
minimum on the cube is $\eta>0$. As $\eta\to0$, the ratio tends
to one where all coordinates in $V$ are one and to zero otherwise.
The convergence is uniform because the cube is finite. Apply this
to the finitely many correction monomials, absorbing their real
coefficients into their affine numerators. Sufficiently small
$\eta$ makes the total error smaller than half the minimum strict
margin. At most $2^a+2^b$ additional heads suffice.
Finally $2^a+2^b=o(2^n/(n\log\log n))$ for the balanced split.
\end{proof}

This conditional proposition is not a claimed asymptotic result.
The missing local lemma would finish the stated fallback and remains
unproved; the route is blocked there. It asks for zeros only on the
two axes, not outside the cell, so the exact Hamming-ball localization
lower bound does not refute it. The common-orientation decision-list
lower bound also does not apply to the finite rational cancellations
it permits. A sublogarithmic-cost reversal of individual numerical
ranking votes at the needed uniform accuracy is excluded above.
A joint or direct local construction remains open. No target or
requested fallback has been obtained.
