\paragraph{Scope.}
These are complete finite algebraic statements and transfer obstructions.
They neither bound the capacity of all reverse decoders nor prove an
improved worst-case head bound. They have not been formalized in Lean.

\section{Exact axis kernel and inactive rows}
For $0<u<d<1$, write
\[
 Q(A,d;a,u)=\frac{A+a}{d-u}-\frac A d-\frac a{1-u}.
\]
Putting the three summands over a common denominator gives
\[
 Q(A,d;a,u)=
 \frac{Au(1-u)+ad(1-d)}{d(d-u)(1-u)}.
\]
Set
\[
 t=\frac{u}{1-u},\quad v=a(1+t)^2,\quad
 p=\frac d{1-d},\quad C=\frac A{d(1-d)}.
\]
Substitution of $u=t/(1+t)$, $d=p/(1+p)$ gives exactly
\[
 Q(A,d;a,u)=\frac{Ct+v}{p-t}.
\]
At the boundary choice $d=1,A=0$, the original expression is identically
zero for every $a$ and $u\ne1$. Hence exact-axis gadgets can be isolated
on disjoint row groups by assigning this boundary row data to inactive
heads. This uses weak slopes on ignored rows; a final strict-slope
perturbation is permitted only after the global strict signs have been
assembled. The identity does not supply an increasing ratio of decoded
rows to heads.

\section{A Möbius correction and the cross-ratio obstruction}
There is a useful distinction between additive numerator transfer and
whole-cube admissibility. Let
\[
 g(x)=\frac{\alpha x+\beta}{\gamma x+\delta},
 \qquad \Delta=\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma\ne0.
\]
For all arguments where the denominators are nonzero,
\[
 \frac{a}{g(x)-g(y)}
 =\frac{a(\gamma y+\delta)^2}{\Delta(x-y)}
   +\frac{\gamma a(\gamma y+\delta)}{\Delta}.
\]
Indeed, $g(x)-g(y)=\Delta(x-y)/[(\gamma x+\delta)
(\gamma y+\delta)]$, and
$\gamma x+\delta=(\gamma y+\delta)+\gamma(x-y)$.
Thus a head with column-only numerators transfers under a common
fractional-linear reparameterization, up to a column-only additive term.
On a one-hot grid that correction is affine in the column bits.
This algebra alone does not ensure an admissible extension to the full
Boolean cube.

\begin{proposition}[Necessary cross-ratio bound]
Let $r,s\geq4$ be even. Choose $u_0<u_1<d_0<d_1$. Form an $r$ by $s$
additive denominator table by repeating each row pole $r/2$ times and
each column pole $s/2$ times. Suppose a common fractional-linear map,
defined at these four points, produces two row values above both column
values. Suppose this transformed table extends, up to a positive common
scale, to an affine denominator whose row and column slopes are all
nonpositive and whose values are positive on the entire $(r+s)$-cube.
Then
\[
 \kappa:=\frac{(d_1-d_0)(u_1-u_0)}
 {(d_1-u_0)(d_0-u_1)}<\frac{2}{rs}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $D_-<D_+$ be the transformed row values and $U_-<U_+$ the
transformed column values. Put $G=D_+-U_->0$ and
\[
 a=\frac{D_+-D_-}{G},\qquad b=\frac{U_+-U_-}{G}.
\]
Every affine extension of an additive one-hot table has the form
$B(z,w)=\Gamma-\sum_i p_i z_i-\sum_jq_jw_j$, where
$p_i=\Gamma-D_i+c$ and $q_j=U_j-c$ for a common shift $c$.
Nonnegative $p_i,q_j$ require $\Gamma+c\geq D_+$ and $c\leq U_-$.
The whole-cube minimum is
\[
 B(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)
 =\sum_iD_i-\sum_jU_j-(r-1)\Gamma+(s-r)c.
\]
Equivalently, setting $L=\Gamma+c$, it is
\[
 \sum_iD_i-\sum_jU_j-(r-1)L+(s-1)c
 \leq G-\frac r2(D_+-D_-)-\frac s2(U_+-U_-).
\]
Strict positivity therefore implies $(r/2)a+(s/2)b<1$.
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives $ab<1/(rs)$.
Since $r,s\geq4$, one also has $a+b<1/2$.

The displayed cross ratio is invariant under a fractional-linear map.
The two within-block orders are either both preserved or both reversed:
opposite orders would give a negative transformed cross ratio, whereas
the original one is positive and both row values exceed both column
values. Consequently, after sorting the transformed values,
\[
 \kappa=\frac{(D_+-D_-)(U_+-U_-)}
 {(D_+-U_-)(D_--U_+)}=\frac{ab}{1-a-b}
 <\frac2{rs}.
\]
\end{proof}

For example, $(u_0,u_1,d_0,d_1)=(1,2,3,4)$ gives $\kappa=1/3$.
Repeating each value twice gives a four by four table. The necessary
bound is $\kappa<1/8$, so no common fractional-linear transformation
followed by an additive reverse extension can make this table admissible
on the whole cube. This is a counterexample to that transfer method,
not to joint resynthesis with several heads.

\section{Why signed permutation residues do not automatically expose all bits}
Let $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_H$ be distinct and let
$\psi_j\in\mathbb R$. A permutation sheet assigns the pair
$(\lambda_{\pi(h)},\psi_{\pi(h)})$ to head $h$.
One natural plan reads the position of the largest-magnitude residue,
then erases it from later queries and repeats. The required erasure
has an exact compatibility condition.

\begin{proposition}[Location-independent erasure]
Suppose two distinct row denominators $d\ne e$ and corresponding row
numerators $A,B$ satisfy, for every $j$ in a set $J$,
\[
 \frac{A+\psi_j}{d-\lambda_j}
 =\frac{B+\psi_j}{e-\lambda_j}=k_j,
\]
with all denominators nonzero. Then all $k_j$ are equal to one number
$k$, and the points $(\lambda_j,\psi_j)$, $j\in J$, lie on the line
$\psi_j+k\lambda_j=kd-A$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Subtracting $A+\psi_j=k_j(d-\lambda_j)$ and
$B+\psi_j=k_j(e-\lambda_j)$ gives
$A-B=k_j(d-e)$. Since $d-e\ne0$, the number $k_j$ is independent
of $j$. Substitution gives the claimed line.
\end{proof}

For the interpolation choice
\[
 p(T)=\prod_{j=1}^H(T-\lambda_j),\qquad
 q(T)=\prod_{i=1}^H(T-D_i),\qquad
 \psi_j=-\frac{q(\lambda_j)}{p'(\lambda_j)},
\]
where every $D_i>\lambda_H>\cdots>\lambda_1$, the residues have
alternating nonzero signs in increasing order of $j$. Any three
consecutive residue points therefore fail to be collinear: their
ordinates have signs $+,-,+$ or $-,+,-$, which a linear function
on increasing abscissae cannot have. The erasure proposition thus
prevents varying row denominators while making these three consecutive
symbols simultaneously location-independent. It does not prohibit
arbitrary joint multi-slot queries or another coding architecture.

\section{A mixed-column decoder and its first-moment obstruction}
The signed interpolation family supplies a materially different proposed
readout. If column $j$ stores an ordered list of pole-residue pairs
$(b_{hj},c_{hj})$, a multi-hot input with active column set $T$ evaluates
\[
 F_T(D)=-1+\sum_h\frac{\sum_{j\in T}c_{hj}}
 {D-\sum_{j\in T}b_{hj}}.
\]
Although every one-column query can be constrained to zero at the fixed
row arguments, a multi-column query depends on relative assignments of
the pole-residue pairs to heads. Thus these queries can distinguish
some permutation sheets without head-specific row coefficients.
Their ability to decode arbitrary row tables, however, does not follow
from the number of sheets. Already the first mixed moment is an
obstruction in a generic small-pole regime.

\begin{proposition}[Constant signs from a nonzero first mixed moment]
Let $H\geq3$, let $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_H$ be distinct positive
numbers, and let $D_1,\ldots,D_H$ be distinct positive numbers. Put
\[
 p_0(T)=\prod_{j=1}^H(T-\lambda_j),\quad
 w_j=\frac1{p_0'(\lambda_j)},\quad
 q(T)=\prod_{i=1}^H(T-D_i),\quad
 c_j(\varepsilon)=-\frac{q(\varepsilon\lambda_j)}
 {\varepsilon^{H-1}p_0'(\lambda_j)}.
\]
For a permutation $\pi$, define
\[
 M_\pi=\sum_jw_j(\lambda_{\pi(j)}+\lambda_{\pi^{-1}(j)}),
 \qquad
 F_{\pi,\varepsilon}(D)=-1+
 \sum_j\frac{c_j(\varepsilon)+c_{\pi(j)}(\varepsilon)}
 {D-\varepsilon(\lambda_j+\lambda_{\pi(j)})}.
\]
For every fixed $D>0$,
\[
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \varepsilon^{H-2}F_{\pi,\varepsilon}(D)
 =-\frac{q(0)M_\pi}{D^2}.
\]
Consequently, if $M_\pi\ne0$, then for all sufficiently small positive
$\varepsilon$, the values at all $D_i$ have the same strict sign.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Lagrange interpolation for the polynomials one and $T$, followed by
comparison of their $T^{H-1}$ coefficients, gives
$\sum_jw_j=\sum_jw_j\lambda_j=0$, since $H\geq3$.
For each fixed $j$ and $D>0$, Taylor expansion at zero gives
\[
 q(\varepsilon\lambda_j)
 =q(0)+\varepsilon q'(0)\lambda_j+O(\varepsilon^2),
 \qquad
 \frac1{D-\varepsilon(\lambda_j+\lambda_{\pi(j)})}
 =\frac1D+\frac{\varepsilon(\lambda_j+\lambda_{\pi(j)})}{D^2}
  +O(\varepsilon^2).
\]
There are finitely many summands, so the errors can be summed. The
constant term in their product sum is
$q(0)\sum_j(w_j+w_{\pi(j)})/D=0$.
The contribution involving $q'(0)$ in the linear coefficient is
$2q'(0)\sum_jw_j\lambda_j/D=0$. The remaining linear coefficient is
\[
 \frac{q(0)}{D^2}
 \sum_j(w_j+w_{\pi(j)})(\lambda_j+\lambda_{\pi(j)})
 =\frac{q(0)M_\pi}{D^2}.
\]
The minus sign in the residues gives the asserted limit; the constant
term $-1$ vanishes after multiplication by $\varepsilon^{H-2}$.
If $M_\pi\ne0$, the limiting values are nonzero and all have the same
sign. Finiteness of the row set then supplies one positive upper bound
on $\varepsilon$ preserving that sign at every row. All displayed
denominators are positive after decreasing this upper bound if needed.
\end{proof}

This obstruction permits exceptional permutations with $M_\pi=0$,
parameters not in the small-pole limit, and jointly changing the
unordered pole sets. It therefore does not refute the mixed-column
decoder. Any attempt using this mechanism must provide the required
moment cancellations and the resulting arbitrary strict row patterns,
rather than only the component count.

\section{Exact butterfly-window readers require increasing Cauchy degree}
The number of signed interpolation sheets suggests storing a large family
of permutations. A butterfly network does give sufficiently many
independent binary choices. Its natural block-sum readout, however,
encounters an explicit obstruction for alternating residues.

\begin{proposition}[Constant windows of an alternating-residue reader]
Let $\lambda_1<\cdots<\lambda_H$ and let $c_1,\ldots,c_H$ be nonzero
with $c_jc_{j+1}<0$. For $K\geq1$, consider the symbol function
\[
 \phi(j)=\delta+\sum_{\ell=1}^K
 \frac{A_\ell+\gamma_\ell c_j}{D_\ell-\lambda_j},
 \qquad D_\ell\notin\{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_H\}.
\]
If $\phi$ equals one constant on $L$ consecutive symbols and $L>2K$,
then $\phi$ equals that constant on every symbol.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $b$ be the asserted constant and multiply $\phi(j)-b$ by
$d(\lambda_j)$, where $d(T)=\prod_{\ell=1}^K(D_\ell-T)$.
There are polynomials $P,Q$ with
\[
 \deg P\leq K-1,\qquad \deg Q\leq K,
 \qquad c_jP(\lambda_j)+Q(\lambda_j)=0
\]
on the $L$ consecutive symbols. Here
\[
 P(T)=\sum_\ell\gamma_\ell\prod_{m\ne\ell}(D_m-T),
 \quad Q(T)=(\delta-b)d(T)
       +\sum_\ell A_\ell\prod_{m\ne\ell}(D_m-T).
\]
If exactly one of $P,Q$ is zero, the other has $L>K$ distinct roots,
which is impossible. Suppose neither is zero. At a sampled node where
$P$ vanishes, $Q$ also vanishes, so $PQ$ has a root of multiplicity at
least two. Let $z$ be the number of these nodes. At each remaining
sampled node,
\[
 \operatorname{sgn}(P(\lambda_j)Q(\lambda_j))
   =-\operatorname{sgn}(c_j).
\]
Of the $L-1$ adjacent pairs of sampled nodes, at most $2z$ have a
vanishing endpoint. Each of the other pairs has opposite signs of
$PQ$ at its endpoints and therefore contributes a distinct intervening
root. Together with the nodal roots, this gives at least
$2z+\max(0,L-1-2z)\geq L-1$ roots counted with multiplicity.
But $\deg(PQ)\leq2K-1$, contradicting $L>2K$.
Thus both $P,Q$ are zero, and the original rational expression equals
$b$ at every symbol, as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Butterfly block readout obstruction]
Let $H=2^r$. The $r$ stages of a butterfly network successively switch
coordinate $0,1,\ldots,r-1$ of a binary word, with an independent switch
for each pair differing only at the current coordinate. Its
$Hr/2$ switch settings give $2^{Hr/2}$ distinct permutations.

Fix a stage $k$ with $1\leq k<r$ and one switch at that stage. A
standard block-sum readout of this switch has the form
\[
 \mathcal R(\pi)=\sum_{x\in S}\phi(\pi(x)),
\]
where $S$ consists of the $2^k$ inputs having specified coordinates
above $k$ and coordinate $k$ equal to zero. Suppose $\mathcal R$ is
exactly one of two distinct real values according to the specified
switch setting, for every setting of all the other switches. Order
the output symbols by bit reversal, and assign alternating nonzero
residues in that order. If $\phi$ has the $K$-term form in the preceding
proposition, then
\[
 K\geq 2^{r-k-1}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For an input $x$ and output $y$, the state after the first $k$ stages
has its $k$ lower coordinates from $y$ and the remaining coordinates
from $x$. Thus there is only one possible input-to-output path.
A complete permutation consequently determines every switch setting,
proving the asserted number of distinct permutations.

Partition the outputs into $2^k$ blocks $R_b$ according to their lower
$k$ coordinates $b$. Each block has $L=2^{r-k}$ outputs, and is an
interval in bit-reversal order. The first $k$ stages map $S$ bijectively
onto all lower-coordinate words while preserving its fixed higher
coordinates and its zero coordinate $k$. Thus $\pi(S)$ contains
exactly one output in each $R_b$.

Moreover any transversal of these blocks is attainable. Keep the first
$k$ stages fixed. Choose the stage-$k$ switch separately for each lower
word $b$ to select the desired output coordinate $k$. The remaining
stages preserve the lower $k+1$ coordinates and act independently in
the resulting subnetworks. The unique selected path in each such
subnetwork can be routed to any prescribed higher-coordinate word by
choosing its successive switches. These choices for different $b$
are disjoint, and complete settings of the unused switches give a
permutation.

The target switch controls the chosen output coordinate $k$ in one
specific block $R_{b_*}$. Since $k\geq1$, there is an inactive block
$R_b$ with $b\ne b_*$. Varying its chosen output while fixing every
other member of the transversal leaves the target switch fixed.
The assumed exact readout therefore forces $\phi$ to be constant on
all $L$ consecutive symbols of $R_b$. It cannot be constant on all
symbols, since then the readout would take only one value. The preceding
proposition yields $L\leq2K$, as required.
\end{proof}

This rules out exact constant-degree Cauchy windows as readers for all
but a bounded number of butterfly stages. It does not rule out readers
that only separate the two settings by a strict sign, allow background
variation, use functions depending separately on the input slot, or
encode permutations by a different architecture. In particular, it
does not upper-bound the full compact decoder's sign capacity.

\paragraph{Unresolved capacity question.}
The finite five-row construction disproves the naive bound of $2H$
readable signs from $2H$ column parameters. None of the statements here
proves a linear upper bound on the full compact rational decoder, and
none constructs $H\log H$, $H\log\log H$, or any other superlinear
number of independently readable signs. That precise universality
question remains open in this continuation.
