\title{One adaptive denominator statistic cannot supply a logarithmic saving}
\maketitle

\paragraph{Scope and status.}
This is a negative result for a restricted direct, global construction.
All heads may have arbitrary affine numerators and arbitrary distinct or
repeated poles, but their row slopes must be proportional to one common
affine statistic. That statistic may depend on the entire target labeling.
The algebraic clearing and its strict-sign equivalence are kernel checked
in \texttt{JointFamilyCommonStatistic.lean}. The counting consequence
below is a written application of an external theorem, not a Lean theorem.
It does not apply to banks with independently chosen row directions,
the finite-plane construction, or the general original model.

\paragraph{The family.}
Put $M=2^k$. At $x\in\{0,1\}^k$, consider
\[
 F(x)=\tau+\sum_{h=1}^H
 \frac{a_{h0}+\sum_{i=1}^k a_{hi}x_i}{b_h-\phi_w(x)},
 \qquad \phi_w(x)=\sum_{i=1}^k w_i x_i.
\]
Every denominator is assumed positive on the full cube. We allow all
real $w_i$, a relaxation of either uniform slope orientation. A constant
term in $\phi_w$ is absorbed into the $b_h$. A positive head-specific
multiple of the common statistic can likewise be normalized into its
pole and numerator.

Let $Q(T)=\prod_h(b_h-T)$ and let $Q_h(T)$ omit its $h$th factor.
Multiplying by the positive $Q(\phi_w(x))$ gives
\[
 Q(\phi_w(x))F(x)=P_0(\phi_w(x))+
       \sum_{i=1}^k x_iP_i(\phi_w(x)),
\]
where $P_0=\tau Q+\sum_h a_{h0}Q_h$ and
$P_i=\sum_h a_{hi}Q_h$. All these polynomials have degree at most $H$.
We enlarge the family by allowing their $(k+1)(H+1)$ coefficients to
vary freely. Thus poles add no further degrees of freedom to this
enlarged sign class. The reduction remains valid when poles coincide.

\paragraph{The external counting input.}
Theorems 12 and 13 of Basu and Rizzie,
\emph{Multi-degree bounds on the Betti numbers of real varieties and
semi-algebraic sets and applications}, bound sign conditions for
polynomials with specified degrees in separate variable blocks.
We use the explicit $G_{\min}$ formula in Section 2.2, with two blocks,
and retain its binomial sum rather than the weaker final asymptotic
estimate printed there. A primary author copy is
\href{https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/multidegree-may-24-2017.pdf}
{the May 24, 2017 manuscript}, page 11.

Write $p=(k+1)(H+1)$ and $D=p+k$. The coefficient variables form a
block of $p$ variables with degree at most one. The direction variables
form a block of $k$ variables with degree at most $H$. Set
$h=\max\{2,H\}$ and use degree bounds $(2,h)$ in that theorem.
Its uniform bound for $G_{\min}$, obtained by using
$\frac12G_{\rm gen}(2d',\mathbf k,1)$, is
\[
 G_{\min}(d',\mathbf k,j)
 \leq 1+4(D+1)^2 108^D h^k
 \leq 4096^D h^k.
\]
Here each coordinate of $d'$ is the smallest even integer above its
corresponding degree bound, so doubling it costs at most four times the
original degree. The multinomial reciprocal in $G_{\rm gen}$ is at
most one. Finally $(D+1)^2\leq4^D$ for $D\geq1$.

For $D\leq M$, the number $S$ of strict sign words on the cube therefore
satisfies
\[
 S\leq4096^D h^k\sum_{j=1}^D4^j\binom Mj
 \leq h^k\left(\frac{2^{16}M}{D}\right)^D.
\]
The final estimate uses
$\sum_{j=0}^D\binom Mj\leq(eM/D)^D$ and $4e<16$.
There is no assumption that the common statistic is fixed before labels
are chosen: its $k$ parameters have already been counted.

\begin{theorem}
For all sufficiently large $k$, universality of the common-statistic
family on the full $k$-cube requires $H=\Omega(2^k/k)$.
In particular, $H=o(2^k/k)$ cannot realize every strict Boolean labeling.
With the deliberately coarse constants above, $k\geq16$ and
$1\leq H\leq2^k/(256k)$ already preclude universality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Universality requires $S=2^M$, hence
\[
 M\leq k\log_2 h+D\log_2(2^{16}M/D).
\]
If $H=o(M/k)$ then $D=o(M)$, while $k\log_2 h=O(k^2)=o(M)$.
The right-hand side is $o(M)$, a contradiction.

For the explicit range, $H\leq M/(256k)$ gives $D\leq M/64$.
Also $k^2\leq M/16$ for $k\geq16$. The function
$t\mapsto t\log_2(2^{16}/t)$ increases on $0<t\leq1/64$,
so the right-hand side is at most
$M/16+22M/64<M$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Implication for the research route.}
The common-statistic class cannot recover even the known universal
$O(M/(k\log k))$ bound by a direct global synthesis. The exponentially
many subset sums of a generic $w$ do not change that conclusion.
Independently chosen denominator directions, or a genuinely different
organization of parameters across a shared local family, remain possible.

\paragraph{Extension to any fixed global direction dictionary.}
The same written counting argument covers a larger restricted class:
fix arbitrary row directions $d_h\in\mathbb R^k$ before the target labels,
and allow arbitrary offsets $b_h$, affine numerators $A_h$, and threshold.
For $\tau+\sum_h A_h(x)/(b_h+d_h\cdot x)$, positive denominator clearing
has degree one in $p=H(k+1)+1$ numerator and threshold parameters, and
degree at most $H$ in the $H$ offset parameters. Thus, with $D=p+H$,
\[
 S\leq \max\{2,H\}^{H}
       \left(\frac{2^{16}M}{D}\right)^D
 \qquad(D\leq M).
\]
If $H=o(M/k)$, then $H\log H=o(M)$ and $D=o(M)$, again contradicting
$S=2^M$. This allows exponentially many distinct subset sums, arbitrary
pole permutations, and unrestricted affine numerator coefficients.
It still does not constrain a dictionary whose directions depend on the
target labeling, or on the whole independently labeled local family.
It is an obstruction for direct global synthesis with a fixed dictionary,
not a lower bound for the unrestricted original quantity.
