\paragraph{The original model is unchanged.}
In \texttt{Atoms/FracAtom.lean}, an atom remains
\[
 \frac{\eta+\sum_i\rho_i\alpha^{x_i}(m_i+\delta x_i)}
 {\gamma+\sum_i\rho_i\alpha^{x_i}},
 \qquad \gamma>0,\quad\alpha>0,\quad\rho_i>0.
\]
There is no bound on numerator magnitudes or parameter precision.  The
positive and negative affine-ratio conversion theorems construct these
atoms exactly.  Positive denominator slopes use $\alpha>1$; strictly
negative slopes with a positive whole-cube minimum use $0<\alpha<1$.
No coordinate is appended.  The definitions of \texttt{HStar} in
\texttt{Model/Head.lean} and \texttt{worstCaseHeadComplexity} in

still the finite supremum of the former over all Boolean functions.
The comparison of the two branches shows no modification of these model
or invariant definitions.

\paragraph{One compact correction really is one reverse head.}
For a prescribed row numerator $A_i$, row pole $d_i\in(1-\delta,1)$,
and column token $t_j\in(0,\delta)$, use
\[
 A(z,w)=\sum_i A_i z_i,\qquad
 B(z,w)=1-\sum_i(1-d_i)z_i-\sum_jt_jw_j.
\]
Every denominator slope is strictly negative, and on the entire Boolean
$(r+s)$-cube,
\[
 B(z,w)>1-(r+s)\delta>0.
\]
At $(e_i,e_j)$ its value is exactly $A_i/(d_i-t_j)$.  In the affine-plane
construction, the row pole for the correction associated with point $g$
is the pole of the slope joining $g$ to the queried point.  When their
first coordinates agree, the numerator is zero; the arbitrary totalized
slope in that case does not affect the identity or admissibility.
The choice $\delta=1/[4(r+s+1)]$ meets the stated budget.  This is a
whole-cube proof, not a verification only at weight-two inputs.

\paragraph{The rank correction has its claimed cost.}
For one arbitrary real rank-one table $f_i g_j$, choose
$C=1+\sum_i|f_i|$, $u_i=C+f_i$, and $M=4(r+1)C$.
Then $0<u_i<2C$ and the weak reverse head
\[
 \frac{M\sum_jg_jw_j}
 {1-\sum_i\frac{u_i}{M+u_i}z_i}
\]
has grid value $(M+C+f_i)g_j$.  Its denominator is positive on the
whole cube, since the sum of all its drops is less than $1/2$.
For $L$ rank factors, a single additional affine numerator over the
constant denominator $1$ subtracts all $L$ column shifts and adds all
desired row and column biases.  The total is $L+1$ weak reverse heads,
not $2L$ or $L$ times the number of columns.

\paragraph{Weak slopes are repaired without head loss.}
If $D$ is a weak reverse denominator on $N=r+s$ bits and
$m_0=D(1,\ldots,1)>0$, set $\varepsilon=m_0/(N+1)$ and
\[
 D'(x)=D(x)+\varepsilon\left(2-\sum_i x_i\right).
\]
All slopes become strictly negative.  Its minimum is attained at the
all-one vertex and equals $3\varepsilon>0$.  At each grid point the
weight is exactly two, so $D'=D$ there.  This exact grid-preserving
operation applies to the rank heads and their single correction head;
it adds no heads and requires no sign perturbation argument.

\paragraph{Finite compiler and exact axis repair.}
For a field of order $q=2^k$, there are $q^2$ affine-plane points and
$q^2$ nonvertical lines.  The line aggregates have rank at most $q^2$,
so the preceding construction costs $q^2+1$ heads.  One compact
correction per point costs a further $q^2$ heads.  The shared-line
identity leaves exactly the Cauchy decoder of the token at the queried
point.  A token stores $k$ bits, yielding
\[
 r\leq k4^k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 H_{\rm grid}\leq2\cdot4^k+1
\]
for every number of columns.  The statement is
\texttt{hasStrictReverseGridCompiler\_affinePlane}.

The exact axis repair in \texttt{ReverseGridAxisRepair.lean} replaces
each strict reverse ratio by three such ratios, cancelling its values
on both complete Boolean axes.  Concretely, subtract the ratio's value
at the origin times its denominator from its numerator, then subtract
its row-only and column-only restrictions using denominators whose
missing-coordinate slopes are a common negative upper bound on the
original slopes.  These denominators dominate the original denominator
and hence remain positive.  The grid discrepancy is a row term plus a
column term.  All discrepancies, including the original scalar bias,
are aggregated and corrected by three further strict reverse heads.
Thus \texttt{exists\_reverse\_axis\_repair} gives exactly
$3H_{\rm grid}+3$ heads with the same grid values and zero values on
\emph{every} point of each axis.  The resulting data has no unattached
scalar bias.  More generally, \texttt{absorb\_bias\_family} absorbs a
bias into one numerator as $A\mapsto A+cB$ before localization, with
unchanged denominators and head count.

\paragraph{The gluing order and admissibility.}
For center $(S,T)$ the cell consists of
$(S\setminus\{i\},T\setminus\{j\})$, and the containing downset is
$X\subseteq S$, $Y\subseteq T$.  Centers are ordered by decreasing
$|S|+|T|$, with arbitrary ties.  On a center downset, local deletion
coordinates are $z_i=1-x_i$ on the center and zero off it.  Negative
local slopes therefore become strictly positive ambient slopes on
center coordinates.  Choose slope $1$ outside the center.  The ambient
constant denominator is the local denominator at the center and is
strictly positive.  Adding a nonnegative multiple of
\[
 q_{S,T}(x)=\sum_{i\notin S\cup T}x_i
\]
preserves strictly positive ambient slopes, vanishes on the downset,
and is strictly positive outside it.  These are exact original-model
atoms through \texttt{FracAtom.ofPositiveAffineRatio}.

If an earlier cell vertex $(X,Y)$ lies in a later downset, then its
earlier center has size $|X|+|Y|+2$.  The order gives
$|S|+|T|\leq|X|+|Y|+2$.  Thus either $X=S$, or $Y=T$, or both deletion
counts equal one.  In the first two cases the later module is exactly
zero by the axis identities.  In the third case it already has the
desired sign.  This proves the nonnegative signed compatibility needed
on every earlier vertex inside the later downset, including overlaps.

At each finite gluing step, a positive numerator scale makes the new
module dominate the old score on its new cell.  On protected vertices
inside its downset the contribution is nonnegative in the target sign.
On protected vertices outside the downset, the localized module tends
to zero as its penalty tends to infinity.  A sufficiently large finite
penalty preserves all their earlier strict signs simultaneously.
The dependence on finite strict margins is legitimate and costs no
heads.  This is implemented by \texttt{glue\_step},
\texttt{glue\_finite}, and \texttt{glue\_reverse\_axis\_modules}.

\paragraph{Cover transfer and exact total budget.}
Complement a checked closed upward-star cover in dimension $a$.
Whenever its complemented center itself needs covering, add one parent
of that proper center.  This produces an open downward cover of all
proper vertices with at most twice as many centers.  The existing
checked constant-eleven bound therefore gives at most
$22\cdot2^a/a$ centers.  The product of two such covers uses at most
\[
 484\frac{2^{a+b}}{ab}
\]
local modules and covers every vertex except the two faces on which
one entire block is one.  This proves the required
$O(2^n/n^2)$ module count after a balanced split.

An arbitrary real residual on a positive coordinate face with $m$
free coordinates has an exact squarefree expansion with $2^m$ terms.
Multiplication by the fixed-face monomial makes every term an ambient
positive monomial.  Each signed monomial is uniformly approximable by
one original atom: its denominator is
$1+\sum_i w_i(1-x_i)$, with large positive $w_i$ on the monomial's
support and small positive $w_i$ elsewhere.  This is the negative
orientation converter, with admissible $0<\alpha<1$.

First add the exact residual on the first exceptional face.  Define
the second residual against this already corrected score.  It is
therefore zero on the intersection of the faces, so the second exact
correction preserves the first.  The resulting finite-cube score has
strict desired signs everywhere.  Approximate the two residuals
uniformly closely enough to preserve its minimum strict margin.
The total face cost is exactly $2^a+2^b$, including the intersection
without a third repair.  Consequently, for a proved axis compiler
budget $M$,
\[
 \W(a+b)\leq484\frac{2^{a+b}M}{ab}+2^a+2^b.
\]
This is \texttt{worstCaseHeadComplexity\_real\_le\_of\_reverse\_axis\_compiler},
with the original invariant.  For $a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and
$b=\lceil n/2\rceil$, the exceptional cost is
$O(2^{\lceil n/2\rceil})$.
