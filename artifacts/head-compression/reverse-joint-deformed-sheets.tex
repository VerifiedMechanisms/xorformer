\paragraph{Status.}
The exact sheet construction and the readout identities below have
complete written proofs and a finite exact-arithmetic checker. They
are not Lean formalized. The required superlinear number of independently
readable signs remains unproved. This note changes no atom definition,
input dimension, or global complexity statement.

Fix $H$ distinct positive tokens $\lambda_j$ and $H$ distinct poles
$d_i>\max_j\lambda_j$. Write
\[
 C_{ij}=\frac1{d_i-\lambda_j},\qquad S_i=\sum_jC_{ij}.
\]
Let $E\in\mathbb R^{H\times H}$ be an arbitrary fixed matrix.
For $u\in\mathbb R^H$ and $a\in\mathbb R^H$, define $2H$ queries
\[
 F_i^0(u,a)=\sum_h\frac{E_{ih}+a_h}{d_i-u_h},\qquad
 F_i^1(u,a)=\sum_h\frac{E_{ih}+a_h+1}{d_i-u_h}-S_i.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact regular sheets]
Every permutation $\pi$ defines a regular common zero of all $2H$
queries by
\[
 u_h^\pi=\lambda_{\pi(h)},\qquad
 e_i^\pi=\sum_hE_{ih}C_{i,\pi(h)},\qquad
 a^\pi=-C_\pi^{-1}e^\pi,
\]
where $(C_\pi)_{ih}=C_{i,\pi(h)}$.
Thus there are at least $H!$ distinct real regular sheets. The residue
$a_h^\pi$ can depend on the entire permutation and need not be a
permuted list of token residues.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Cauchy's identity makes $C$ and hence $C_\pi$ invertible.
The definition of $a^\pi$ gives $F_i^0=0$. The difference
$F_i^1-F_i^0=\sum_h1/(d_i-u_h)-S_i$ vanishes at every permutation
of the tokens, so $F_i^1=0$ as well.

Order the parameters as $(u,a)$ and subtract the first block of
Jacobian rows from the second. The Jacobian becomes
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}M_\pi&C_\pi\\C_\pi\circ C_\pi&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 (M_\pi)_{ih}=(E_{ih}+a_h^\pi)C_{i,\pi(h)}^2.
\]
Its determinant is
$(-1)^H\det(C_\pi)\det(C_\pi\circ C_\pi)$, independently of
$M_\pi$. Borchardt's identity expresses the second determinant as
$\det(C_\pi)\operatorname{perm}(C_\pi)$; the permanent is positive.
This proves regularity.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Readouts from independent row-pole perturbations.}
For $i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$ and $v\in\mathbb R^H$, let
\[
 Q_{i,v,\eta}(u,a)=
       \sum_h\frac{E_{ih}+a_h}{d_i+\eta v_h-u_h}.
\]
At each sheet $Q_{i,v,0}=0$, and exact differentiation gives
\[
 \left.\frac{\partial Q_{i,v,\eta}(u^\pi,a^\pi)}{\partial\eta}
       \right|_{\eta=0}
 =-\sum_hv_h(E_{ih}+a_h^\pi)C_{i,\pi(h)}^2.
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus the arbitrary matrix $E$ produces $H$ separate linear feature
maps of the permutation states,
\[
 \Psi_i(\pi)=\bigl((E_{ih}+a_h^\pi)C_{i,\pi(h)}^2\bigr)_{h=1}^H.
\]
If a finite collection of strict signs of the right side of (1)
is realized at designated sheets, it is realized by the actual
$Q$ queries for every sufficiently small common $\eta>0$.
This follows by dividing each query value by $\eta$, taking its
limit in (1), and taking the minimum of the finitely many resulting
positive parameter bounds. The inverse function theorem then lets
all $2H$ regular query signs vary independently in sufficiently small
neighborhoods of those sheets while preserving the strict readouts.
Consequently, $q$ independently readable derivative signs would give
$2H+q$ universally programmable rows. No superlinear $q$ is proved here.

\paragraph{Full-cube transfer.}
All these formulas may be placed in an arbitrarily compact box.
For any fixed total number $r$ of rows and any $s$ columns, choose
$\varepsilon<1/[100(r+s+1)]$, take distinct
$\lambda_j\in(0,\varepsilon)$ and distinct
$d_i\in(1-\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon/2)$, and then choose $\eta$
small enough that every additional pole $d_i+\eta v_h$ remains in
that interval. The inverse-function neighborhoods may be chosen with
every $u_h\in(0,\varepsilon)$.
For the parameters selected for column $j$, set the column slope of
head $h$ to $-u_{hj}$; if its pole on row $\rho$ is $d_{\rho h}$,
set its row slope to $-(1-d_{\rho h})$. Then
\[
 B_h(z,w)=1-\sum_\rho(1-d_{\rho h})z_\rho
                    -\sum_j u_{hj}w_j
\]
has strictly negative slopes and full-cube minimum greater than
$1-(r+s)\varepsilon>0$. Its grid value is exactly
$d_{\rho h}-u_{hj}$. All numerator values above are supplied by
the affine numerator whose row coefficients are $E_{ih}$ or
$E_{ih}+1$ and whose column coefficients are $a_{hj}$.

The row biases $-S_i$ require one additional head. For example
$D(z,w)=1-\frac\varepsilon2(|z|+|w|)$ is positive with strictly
negative slopes, and on the grid the numerator
$(1-\varepsilon)\sum_\rho c_\rho z_\rho$ divided by $D$
equals the desired row bias $c_\rho$.
Thus any successful readout family would give an $H+1$-head
strict reverse promised-grid compiler. The separately proved general
axis repair in \texttt{reverse-grid-axis-repair.tex} converts this
to exact axis zero with at most $3H+6$ heads. These observations
preserve a hypothetical superconstant saving but do not establish one.

\paragraph{A useful exact preconditioning identity.}
Writing $E=CU$ exposes how the residues mix the whole permutation.
For $j\neq k$,
\[
 C_{ij}C_{ik}=\frac{C_{ij}-C_{ik}}{\lambda_j-\lambda_k}.
\]
Define the permutation-selected vector
$t_k^\pi=U_{k,\pi^{-1}(k)}$ and set
\[
 b_j^\pi=\sum_{h:\pi(h)\neq j}
                \frac{U_{jh}}{\lambda_j-\lambda_{\pi(h)}}
       -\sum_{\ell\neq j}
                \frac{U_{\ell,\pi^{-1}(j)}}{\lambda_\ell-\lambda_j}.
\]
Expanding $e_i^\pi=\sum_{j,h}U_{jh}C_{ij}C_{i,\pi(h)}$
and separating its diagonal terms gives
\[
 e^\pi=Cb^\pi+(C\circ C)t^\pi,
 \qquad
 a_h^\pi=-b_{\pi(h)}^\pi-
           \bigl(C^{-1}(C\circ C)t^\pi\bigr)_{\pi(h)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The signs in (2) are checked by the companion exact-arithmetic script.
For random $U$, the vector $t^\pi$ selects one entry in each row and
column of $U$. This is a possible source of richer joint information,
but Gaussian independence of these selected entries does not itself
prove a uniform separation estimate for the complete readout features.

\paragraph{Unresolved quantitative issue.}
As the poles and tokens enter a very compact far-pole regime, the
inverse Cauchy matrix is ill conditioned. Generic unscaled choices
of $E$ can be dominated by one barycentric mode, making the different
feature maps strongly correlated. A proof must control all selected
permutation pairs, not merely most pairs in a finite numerical sample.
It remains open whether a structured $E$ and permitted readouts yield
$q/H\to\infty$, let alone $q=\Omega(H\log\log H)$.
