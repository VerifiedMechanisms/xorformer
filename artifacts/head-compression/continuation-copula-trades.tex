\paragraph{Status and scope.}
This is a complete finite construction in the unchanged attention model.
It is written mathematics with independently checkable rational witnesses,
not a Lean theorem. It is not an asymptotic improvement for unrestricted
head complexity. In particular, it does not resolve the requested upper
bound. Its purpose is to exhibit actual independent sign capacity from
target-dependent permutations, rather than merely count parameter sheets.
The companion checker uses only Python's standard library and exact
\texttt{Fraction} arithmetic. There are no floating-point tests or numerical
optimization assumptions in the certificate.

\begin{theorem}[Four admissible heads program eight mixed-fiber signs]
Let $u,v_1,v_2,v_3\in\{0,1\}$. Prescribe arbitrary signs
$s(u,v)\in\{-1,1\}$ at the eight vertices with
$v_1+v_2+v_3\ge2$. There is a sum $F$ of four original
\texttt{FracAtom} evaluations on these four input coordinates such that
\[
 F(u,v)=0\quad\text{if }v_1+v_2+v_3\le1,
 \qquad s(u,v)F(u,v)\ge1\quad\text{otherwise}.
\]
Every denominator is strictly decreasing in every coordinate and is at
least $1$ on the whole Boolean cube. The four numerators have zero constant
term and no $u$ term. All coefficients can be chosen rational. Only three
denominator templates are needed, selected according to the requested
signs.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{The construction}
Index the four heads by $h\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and set
\[
 (\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)=(1,2,3,4),\qquad
 q=(78/55,-12/5,1,0),\qquad
 r=(52/15,-22/5,0,1).
\]
Write $\mathrm{id}=(0,1,2,3)$. The three allowed permutations for
the third memory coordinate are
\[
 \pi^{(0)}=(0,1,2,3),\qquad
 \pi^{(1)}=(0,2,3,1),\qquad
 \pi^{(2)}=(1,3,0,2).
\]
Choose one of these for $\pi_3$, and put
$\pi_1=\pi_2=\mathrm{id}$. For $j=1,2,3$ choose independent
vectors $c_j=\theta_{2j-2}q+\theta_{2j-1}r$. Define
\[
 F(u,v)=\sum_{h=0}^3
 \frac{\sum_{j=1}^3 c_{j,\pi_j(h)}v_j}
 {14-u-\sum_{j=1}^3\lambda_{\pi_j(h)}v_j}.
 \tag{1}
\]
At $v=0$ this is zero. Direct fraction arithmetic gives
\[
 \sum_{h=0}^3\frac{q_h}{d-\lambda_h}
 =\sum_{h=0}^3\frac{r_h}{d-\lambda_h}=0
 \qquad(d=14,13).
 \tag{2}
\]
On $v=e_j$, permutation invariance of the sum and (2) prove the
asserted exact vanishing for both values of $u$.

\subsection*{Whole-cube admissibility in the original model}
For one head let
\[
 a=(1,\lambda_{\pi_1(h)},\lambda_{\pi_2(h)},
                         \lambda_{\pi_3(h)}),\qquad
 t=(0,c_{1,\pi_1(h)},c_{2,\pi_2(h)},c_{3,\pi_3(h)}).
\]
All $a_i$ are positive and $\sum_i a_i\le13$. Consequently the
denominator $14-\sum_i a_ix_i$ is at least $1$ for every cube vertex.
Here is an explicit original \texttt{FracAtom}, not just a relaxed
affine ratio:
\[
 \alpha=\frac1{28},\qquad \delta=0,\qquad
 \rho_i=\frac{28a_i}{27},\qquad
 \gamma=14-\sum_i\rho_i,\qquad
 m_i=-\frac{t_i}{a_i},\qquad \eta=-\sum_i\rho_i m_i.
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed $\alpha>0$, every $\rho_i>0$, and
$\gamma\ge14-28\cdot13/27=14/27>0$. On a Boolean coordinate,
$\rho_i\alpha^{x_i}=\rho_i-a_ix_i$. Substituting (3) in the
original numerator and denominator gives exactly
\[
 \frac{\eta+\sum_i\rho_i\alpha^{x_i}(m_i+\delta x_i)}
      {\gamma+\sum_i\rho_i\alpha^{x_i}}
 =\frac{\sum_i t_ix_i}{14-\sum_i a_ix_i}.
\]
Thus no limiting construction, zero attention weight, or denominator
repair is involved.

\subsection*{Exact sign coverage by three rational charts}
Order the eight remaining vertices by the memory masks
$3,5,6,7$, where $v_j$ has binary weight $2^{j-1}$, and within each
mask by $u=0,1$. Formula (1) defines an $8\times6$ rational matrix
$A_k$ for each of the three templates. Explicitly, for mask $S$
and $d=14-u$, its column $(j,w)$, with $w=q$ or $r$, is
\[
 (A_k)_{(S,d),(j,w)}=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\sum_{h=0}^3
 \frac{w_{\pi_j(h)}}{d-\sum_{\ell\in S}\lambda_{\pi_\ell(h)}}
       &j\in S,\\[3pt]
 0&j\notin S.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4}
\]
The mixed-output vector is $A_k\theta$.

\begin{lemma}[Strict orthant alternative]
Let $A$ be a real $m\times d$ matrix and $s\in\{-1,1\}^m$.
The image of $A$ meets the strict orthant $s$ if and only if
there is no nonzero $z\in\ker A^\top$ with $s_i z_i\ge0$
for every $i$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
One direction follows from
$z^\top A\theta=0$: if all $s_i(A\theta)_i>0$ and all
$s_i z_i\ge0$, with some $z_i\ne0$, their scalar product is positive.
For the converse let $a_i$ be the rows of $A$ and consider the compact
convex hull $K$ of $s_i a_i$. If $0\in K$, its convex coefficients
$w_i\ge0$, $\sum_iw_i=1$, give the forbidden nonzero vector
$z_i=s_iw_i$. Otherwise choose the point $p\in K$ of least Euclidean
norm. The derivative at zero of
$\|p+t(s_i a_i-p)\|^2$, for $t\ge0$, is nonnegative. Hence
$\langle p,s_i a_i\rangle\ge\|p\|^2>0$ for every $i$.
Taking $\theta=p$ realizes the desired strict signs.
\end{proof}

The two columns listed for each template in the appendix are independent
integer vectors spanning $\ker A_k^\top$. This is verified by
multiplying (4) by each column and by exhibiting a nonzero $6\times6$
minor of $A_k$. Encode an orthant by an integer $p\in\{0,\ldots,255\}$:
bit $i$ is one exactly when output coordinate $i$ is positive. The
orthants forbidden by the preceding lemma are as follows. The table
lists only representatives $p<128$; include $255-p$ for each listed
representative.
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
 k&\text{forbidden representatives}\\ \hline
0&42,64,65,66,68,69,72,74,80,81,84,85,96,98,104,106\\
1&41,66,70,74,82,86,98,105,106,107\\
2&41,64,65,68,73,80,84,86,97,105
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
Here is a fully rational way to verify the table. If the two columns
of a dual basis are $a,b$, sort the distinct rational roots
$-a_i/b_i$ for which $b_i\ne0$. Evaluate $a+tb$ at each root, at
the midpoint of each consecutive pair, and at the outermost roots
minus and plus $1$. Also evaluate $b$. Include each resulting vector
and its negative; at each zero coordinate allow either sign. These are
exactly all compatible orthants: every nonvertical line in the dual
plane has the form $\mathbb R(a+tb)$, and every coordinate sign is
constant on each of the stated open intervals. Independence ensures
that no sampled vector is zero.

The intersection of the first two representative lists is
$\{66,74,98,106\}$, disjoint from the third list. The same is true
after taking complements. Thus no orthant is forbidden by all three
charts. The lemma proves existence of a strict witness for every
requested pattern. Since the matrices are rational, a rational vector
can be chosen in each nonempty open solution set. Scaling it makes
all eight signed outputs at least $1$. Together with (2) and (3), this
proves the theorem.

\subsection*{Direct certificates and reproducibility}
The adjacent file \texttt{copula-eight-fibers-certificates.json} contains
one explicit chart and one rational six-vector for every one of the
$256$ orthants. The script
\texttt{audit\_copula\_eight\_fibers.py} independently checks the
following exact statements:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Each matrix (4) has rank six, and the supplied independent
dual vectors annihilate it.
\item Exact enumeration of dual-plane signs gives exactly (5), and
the three forbidden sets have empty intersection.
\item Each of the $256$ rational witnesses has the specified eight
strict output signs, with signed output at least $1$.
\item For every witness and every one of the $16$ cube vertices,
the original parameters (3) are strictly positive where required,
the original numerator and denominator equal (1), every denominator
is at least $1$, and each of the eight axis values is exactly zero.
\end{enumerate}
Run the script without arguments to verify the existing witnesses.
With \texttt{--write-certificates}, it reconstructs them by enumerating
the $\binom86$ square submatrices in each chart, solving the selected
six equalities with right hand sides $\pm1$, and checking all eight
inequalities. All matrix operations use exact fractions.

\subsection*{The precise scaling problem remains open}
For general $H$ poles, $r$ row nodes, and $b$ memory coordinates,
the same axis-preserving idea allows each residue column to lie in an
$(H-r)$-dimensional Cauchy nullspace. The target mixed outputs number
$r(2^b-b-1)$, while a fixed permutation template supplies only
$b(H-r)$ real residue parameters. The four-head theorem shows that
changing templates can genuinely increase the simultaneous strict-sign
capacity beyond the dimension of any one chart. It does not show that
the increase grows with $H$, or that all target signs remain independently
programmable for $b\to\infty$. Establishing such a scalable construction,
with full-cube admissibility and the entire head cost included, is still
required before this mechanism implies an asymptotic saving.
