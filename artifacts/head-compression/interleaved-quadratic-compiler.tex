\paragraph{Status.}
Complete written proof, with exact rational checks of the central sign
construction and the matrix identities. This note is not Lean checked.
The resulting head-to-query ratio is a constant. It proves neither
accepted asymptotic HeadComplexity target.

\begin{lemma}[An interleaved common-factor cone]
Let $g\geq1$, $d=g+2$, and let $Y=(Y_0,\ldots,Y_{g+1})$.
Let $P_Y$ be the polynomial of degree at most $g+1$ with
$P_Y(2i)=Y_i$, and put $T_l(Y)=P_Y(2l+1)$ for $0\leq l\leq g$.
Every prescribed strict sign word of the $2g+2$ expressions
\[
 Y_0,\ldots,Y_{g+1},\qquad T_0(Y)T_1(Y),\ldots,T_0(Y)T_g(Y)
 \tag{1}
\]
is attained by a rational vector $Y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Prescribe the desired signs at the even nodes. At the odd nodes prescribe
$(1,\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_g)$, where the $\tau_l$ are the desired product
signs. Alternatively negate all odd-node signs. Every two consecutive
nodes have opposite parity, so these two words have complementary
sign-change sets among their $2g+2$ gaps. One word has at most $g+1$
sign changes. Put one rational root strictly inside each such gap and
multiply the corresponding linear factors, choosing the overall sign
at the first node. The resulting polynomial has degree at most $g+1$
and exactly the chosen strict signs. Its values at the even nodes give
$Y$; uniqueness of interpolation identifies it with $P_Y$. Negating all
odd signs does not change their products with the first odd sign.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A constant-ratio reverse compiler]
For every $H\geq2$, every $r\geq1$, and every sign table on
$[r]\times[3H-1]$, there is a sum of $H+1$ affine ratios with those
strict grid signs. Every denominator is positive on the entire Boolean
$(r+3H-1)$-cube and has strictly negative slopes. The row slope drops
may be made arbitrarily small. No axis-zero assertion is made here.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $g=H-1$, $d=H+1$, and
\[
 a^0=(1,\ldots,1,-g).
\]
Choose a small rational $\eta>0$. For $1\leq l\leq g$, let $w_l$ have
entries $1+\eta$ at $h=l$, $1+\eta/g$ at $h=H$, and $1$ elsewhere.
Then $w_l\cdot a^0=0$. The $g$ rows $w_l$ are independent: their
differences are $\eta(e_l-e_k)$, and each row has a nonzero last entry.
The $g$ rows
\[
 v_l=(a^0_h w_{lh}^2)_{h=1}^H
\]
are also independent. Indeed, their common last entry is nonzero; a
linear relation first has coefficient sum zero and then, in coordinate
$l$, gives $(2\eta+\eta^2)c_l=0$.

On the $d$-dimensional space of variables $(\lambda,P)\in
\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^H$, consider the surjective linear map
\[
 (\lambda,P)\longmapsto
 (\lambda,v_1\cdot P,\ldots,v_g\cdot P).
\]
The odd-node evaluation map $Y\mapsto(T_0(Y),\ldots,T_g(Y))$ in the
lemma is likewise surjective. Both maps have one-dimensional kernels,
so an invertible rational change of coordinates $Y=Y(\lambda,P)$
identifies them. Consequently all signs of the $d$ coordinate forms
$Y_j$ and the $g$ quadratic forms
\[
 Q_l=\lambda v_l\cdot P                                      \tag{2}
\]
are independent. Multiplying any $Y_j$ by a positive constant preserves
this assertion.

The normalized score family is
\[
 f_j(a,p)=\sum_{h=1}^H\frac{a_h+b_{hj}}{D_{hj}-p_h}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Its first $2g$ queries occur in pairs: for pair $l$, take $b=0$ and
$D=1/w_l$, respectively $D=\kappa/w_l$, with a rational $\kappa>1$
close to one. For the remaining $d$ queries write the desired linear
form, after positive rescaling if necessary, as
\[
 Y_j(\lambda,P)=\alpha_j\lambda+z_j\cdot P.
\]
Choose positive $w_j$ arbitrarily close to the all-one vector with
$a^0\cdot w_j=\alpha_j$, possible after making $\alpha_j$ small by
that rescaling. Set $D_{hj}=1/w_{jh}$ and
$b_{hj}=z_{jh}/w_{jh}^2$. The constant score at $(a,p)=(0,0)$ is
$K_j=\sum_h b_{hj}w_{jh}$. One additional head will subtract $K_j$
exactly at every row of query $j$; put $K_j=0$ on the paired queries.
The corrected extra score has derivative exactly $Y_j$ in the direction
$(a,p)=(\lambda a^0,P)$.

Now prescribe all $3H-1=2g+d$ signs. Use the lemma to choose a rational
$(\lambda,P)$ with the extra-query signs and with each $Q_l$ having
the desired first sign of pair $l$. Choose real numbers $x_l$ as follows.
If the two signs in pair $l$ agree, take $x_l=0$. Otherwise take
$x_l=-Q_l/t_*$ for a fixed rational $1<t_*<\kappa$.
The two values $Q_l+x_l$ and $Q_l+\kappa x_l$ then have the required
signs. Because the rows $w_l$ are independent, choose rational $A$ with
$w_l\cdot A=x_l$ for every $l$.

Set
\[
 a=\delta\lambda a^0+\delta^2 A,\qquad p=\delta P.
\]
For the paired query with scale $t\in\{1,\kappa\}$, Taylor expansion
gives
\[
 f_{l,t}=\delta^2\left(\frac{x_l}{t}+
                                  \frac{Q_l}{t^2}\right)+O(\delta^3).
 \tag{4}
\]
Every corrected extra query is $\delta Y_j+O(\delta^2)$.
All leading signs are strict. There are only finitely many words, so
one sufficiently small positive rational $\delta$ works for all of them.
Choose the row data for the requested table from these witnesses.

Here are explicit full-cube bounds. Write $s=3H-1$ and
$\nu=1/(100s)$. Make the preceding choices small enough that every
normalized $D_{hj}$ belongs to $[1-\nu,1+\nu]$. Put
$\alpha=1-4\nu$, choose $0<\rho<\min\{\nu,1/(100r)\}$, and shrink
$\delta$ until $|\alpha\delta P_{hi}|<\rho/2$ for all chosen rows.
Define
\[
 \widehat p_{hi}=\rho+\alpha\delta P_{hi},\qquad
 \widehat q_{hj}=1-\rho-\alpha D_{hj},
\]
\[
 B_h=1-\sum_i\widehat p_{hi}z_i-\sum_j\widehat q_{hj}w_j,
 \qquad
 A_h=\alpha\left(\sum_i a_{hi}z_i+\sum_j b_{hj}w_j\right).
\]
At the grid these ratios equal (3). All slope drops are positive, and
\[
 \sum_i\widehat p_{hi}<3/200,
 \qquad 0<\widehat q_{hj}<5\nu,
 \qquad \sum_i\widehat p_{hi}+\sum_j\widehat q_{hj}<3/200+1/20<1.
\]
Thus every denominator is positive on the entire cube.

For the correcting head, put
$B_0=1-(100r)^{-1}\sum_i z_i-(100s)^{-1}\sum_jw_j$ and let
$D_0=1-(100r)^{-1}-(100s)^{-1}$ be its constant grid value.
Take $A_0=-D_0\sum_jK_jw_j$. Its grid score is exactly $-K_j$;
its slopes are strictly negative and its cube minimum is $49/50$.
This completes the construction. The arbitrary choice of smaller
$\rho$ proves the row-slope assertion, also for the correcting head
by replacing its row drop with any sufficiently small positive number.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Limitation.}
The construction gives a uniform constant ratio and does not provide
the missing superconstant saving. Applying the existing reverse-grid
axis repair to $H+1$ heads costs $3H+6$ heads. No altered atom model,
extra input coordinates, asymptotic claim, or unproved gluing claim is
used in the theorem above.
