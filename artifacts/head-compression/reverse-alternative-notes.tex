\paragraph{Scope and status.}
The construction below is unconditional and has actual admissible atoms in
the original input dimension. It gives a useful baseline with exact real
interpolation, but its linear local cost does not establish either requested
global logarithmic saving. The central construction and its atom realization
are formalized in
\texttt{HeadComplexity/Results/ReverseAxisCompiler.lean}.
No definition of the original model, \texttt{HStar}, or
\texttt{worstCaseHeadComplexity} is changed.

\begin{proposition}[Exact reverse interpolation]
Let $r,s\geq1$, set $m=r+s$, and let
$M\in\mathbb R^{r\times s}$. There is a sum of $r+1$ affine ratios
with strictly negative denominator slopes, positive denominators on the
entire Boolean $m$-cube, exact grid values $M_{ij}$, and exact zero on both
coordinate axes. Transposing the construction gives
$\min(r,s)+1$ ratios.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $z\in\{0,1\}^r$ and $w\in\{0,1\}^s$, put
\[
 D(z,w)=m+2-\sum_i z_i-\sum_j w_j,
 \qquad L_i(w)=m(m-1)\sum_j M_{ij}w_j.
\]
Define
\[
 F(z,w)=\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{L_i(w)}{D(z,w)-z_i}
             -\frac{\sum_{i=1}^r L_i(w)}{D(z,w)}.
\]
Every numerator and denominator is affine. The common denominator has
slope $-1$ on every coordinate; the $i$-th row denominator has slope $-2$
on $z_i$ and slope $-1$ elsewhere. On the full Boolean cube,
$D\geq2$ and $D-z_i\geq1$. Thus all denominators satisfy the strict
negative-orientation admissibility criterion. The exact conversion
\texttt{FracAtom.ofNegativeAffineRatio} realizes every term with one
original atom, without any new input coordinates or limiting argument.

If $z=0$, all denominators in the first sum are $D$, so the sum cancels
the last term. If $w=0$, every numerator is zero. At $(e_a,e_b)$,
$D=m$. Every summand with $i\ne a$ cancels its contribution from the
common term, leaving
\[
 F(e_a,e_b)=m(m-1)M_{ab}
       \left(\frac1{m-1}-\frac1m\right)=M_{ab}.
\]
For a sign table $M=\sigma$, this yields
$\sigma_{ab}F(e_a,e_b)=1>0$ exactly. Finally, exchanging the two blocks
and using $M^{\mathsf T}$ exchanges $r+1$ and $s+1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact interpolation limit of the common-baseline grouping]
Consider the direct grouped generalization
\[
 G(z,w)=\sum_{h=1}^H\frac{L_h(w)}{D(z,w)-U_h(z)}
       -\frac{\sum_h L_h(w)}{D(z,w)},
 \quad U_h(z)=\sum_i u_{hi}z_i,
 \quad L_h(w)=\sum_j\ell_{hj}w_j,
\]
where its displayed denominators are positive on the cube.
The grid matrix $[G(e_i,e_j)]$ has real rank at most $H$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The common baseline is $D(e_i,e_j)=m$ on the entire grid. Hence
\[
 G(e_i,e_j)=\sum_{h=1}^H
       \frac{u_{hi}}{m(m-u_{hi})}\ell_{hj}.
\]
This is a product of an $r$ by $H$ matrix and an $H$ by $s$ matrix.
In particular, exact interpolation of every real matrix by this grouped
ansatz needs $H\geq\min(r,s)$. This conclusion is about exact matrix
interpolation in the displayed ansatz. It is not a lower bound for the
requested sign-only compiler, which may use column-dependent denominators
or a different assembly rule.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Other direct attempts.}
An extraction-array construction was examined with one head assigned to
one row group. It would need each head to select a target-dependent group
and emit its chosen subset there, while keeping its contributions small in
all other groups. The known positive-orientation interval kernel obtains
this behavior from poles and exponentially separated positive offsets.
The reverse orientation requires the whole-cube sum-of-slopes buffer,
so that identity does not transfer. No replacement interval or group
selector was proved.

A permutation assignment of groups to heads cancels symmetric baseline
terms, but the remaining asymmetric reciprocal queries still need to read
many independent bits of the assignment. The cancellation alone does not
supply that decoder. Neither the number of permutations nor numerical
feasibility on finitely many tables establishes universality.
These attempted assemblies do not establish a new obstruction to all
reverse local compilers and do not settle the global target.
