\paragraph{Scope.}
The proposed token-address decoder would require an actual flat limit
with columns comparable to $V_hW^{k_h(\pi)}$, with sufficiently many
independently varying exponents $k_h(\pi)$. No such flat is proved here.
The exact finite-field probes below did not produce it. The mask
proposition is an unconditional written construction, but it does not
establish either requested asymptotic bound.

Consider the columns
\[
 K_h(\epsilon)_i=
 \frac1{D_i(\epsilon)-\alpha_h(\epsilon)V_{ih}
                      -\beta_{\pi(h)}(\epsilon)},
 \qquad D_i(\epsilon)=1-\epsilon^w W_i.
\]
All exponents in $\alpha_h=\pm\epsilon^{a_h}$ and
$\beta_j=\epsilon^{b_j}$ are positive. Since the query and memory
sets are finite, these grid denominators admit a strict reverse
whole-cube realization for all sufficiently small positive
$\epsilon$: choose an intercept just above the maximum row pole,
so every row drop and column drop tends to zero while the intercept
tends to one. This observation verifies admissibility of the finite
families, not their shattering power.

\begin{proposition}[Independent profiles do not create a ladder]
Subtract the common token kernel
$C_j(\epsilon)_i=(D_i(\epsilon)-\beta_j(\epsilon))^{-1}$.
If the vectors $V_h=(V_{ih})_i$ are linearly independent, then the
column-space limit of $K_h-C_{\pi(h)}$ is exactly
$\operatorname{span}\{V_h:1\leq h\leq H\}$, independently of $\pi$
and of the positive exponents.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The exact identity is
\[
 \frac{K_h-C_{\pi(h)}}{\alpha_h}
 =\frac{V_h}{(D-\beta_{\pi(h)})
                  (D-\beta_{\pi(h)}-\alpha_hV_h)}.
\]
The right-hand side converges coordinatewise to $V_h$. Division by
the nonzero scalar $\alpha_h$ does not change the column space.
The limiting columns are independent, so their span is the limit.
In particular, a permutation-dependent power of $W$ cannot be
inserted in this argument without an additional cancellation.
\end{proof}

Deliberately dependent profiles remain a separate possibility.
For the linearized subtracted kernel, their successive moment
matrices have the form $S,S\operatorname{diag}(\beta),
S\operatorname{diag}(\beta)^2,\ldots$. One must analyze the full
least nonzero exterior coefficients, including exceptional moment
relations; dimension counting alone does not identify the flat.

\paragraph{Exact finite-field probes.}
The script \texttt{reverse-unbalanced-flat-probe.py} computes the
Grassmann limit over $\mathbb F_{1000003}((\epsilon))$ by invertible
Laurent-series column operations. At every step it divides out the
lowest valuation, normalizes a unit pivot series, and eliminates the
pivot row from all remaining columns. It reduces the available
series precision after every division, and rejects unresolved
dependence rather than treating unknown coefficients as zero.
The leading columns are independent. Reversing the input columns is
also checked to give the same canonical leading subspace.

The profile of slot $h$ is the vector consisting of one followed by
its binary digits; the queries are pairs of a token address and a
profile coordinate. Thus the profile rank is logarithmic in $H$,
as contemplated by the token-address proposal. Both raw and
common-kernel-subtracted columns were tested, including negative
head shifts with $a_h=b_h$, unequal head exponents, widely separated
token exponents, and a shared row perturbation of order
$\epsilon^3$.

For $H=4$, all 24 permutations in the saved configurations have
union-flat dimension at most six. For $H=6$, ten sampled positive
shift configurations and seven negative shift configurations, each
with 100 sampled permutations, have union-flat dimension at most
eight. For $H=8$, five negative-shift configurations with 100 sampled
permutations and shared row exponent three have union-flat dimension
at most ten; the subtracted families all have union dimension eight.
The reproducible saved $H=4$ output is
\texttt{reverse-unbalanced-flat-probe.jsonl}. These are finite-field
experiments. They do not constitute an upper bound over the reals,
do not exclude other profiles, and do not prove a universal
obstruction to amplification.

\begin{proposition}[Boolean masks with low matrix rank]
Suppose $H$ strict reverse heads have grid entries
\[
 R_h(i,j)=\frac{A_{hi}+a_{hj}}{b_h-p_{hi}-u_{hj}},
 \qquad p_{hi},u_{hj}>0,
 \qquad b_h-\sum_i p_{hi}-\sum_j u_{hj}>0.
\]
Let $V_{ih}\in\{0,1\}$ have real matrix rank at most $L$. Then
$\sum_h V_{ih}R_h(i,j)$ has an exact strict reverse grid
representation with at most $H+L+1$ heads.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Replace the row slopes and row numerator terms of head $h$ by
$V_{ih}p_{hi}$ and $V_{ih}A_{hi}$. Removing negative denominator
slopes only increases its whole-cube minimum. The resulting weak
reverse head has grid value
\[
 V_{ih}R_h(i,j)+(1-V_{ih})q_{hj},
 \qquad q_{hj}=\frac{a_{hj}}{b_h-u_{hj}}.
\]
Factor $V_{ih}=\sum_{\ell=1}^L f_{i\ell}g_{\ell h}$.
The required correction to the sum of the $H$ modified heads is
\[
 -\sum_h q_{hj}
 +\sum_{\ell=1}^L f_{i\ell}
                  \left(\sum_hg_{\ell h}q_{hj}\right).
\]
This is a column term plus $L$ rank-one factors. The independently
checked exact rank-correction construction supplies it with $L+1$
weak reverse heads. Apply the checked weight-two gauge to every
denominator to obtain strictly negative slopes, with unchanged grid
values and no added heads.
\end{proof}

A common Boolean row mask has $L\leq1$, so it costs only $H+2$
heads. By transposition this also holds for a Boolean column mask.
Combining it with the checked $2H+2$ row-sign construction improves
the row-sign-plus-column-mask count from $4H+6$ to $2H+4$.
The proposition is a written corollary of the checked rank repair
and gauge; it has not been added as a Lean declaration.

The baseline must be row independent in this argument. Multiplying
an arbitrary row-dependent token filter $Q_i(\lambda_j)$ by the
slot-bit mask generally creates up to $H\log H$ independent memory
features. The proposition does not silently supply that missing
head-selective filter at cost $O(H)$.
