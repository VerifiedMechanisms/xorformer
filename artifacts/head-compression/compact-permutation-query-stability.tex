\title{Variable exterior queries on a fixed permutation pool}
\author{}\date{}\maketitle
\paragraph{Status and scope.}
This is a complete written limitation of one compact decoder family, with
an exact small-instance checker. It is not a bound for HeadComplexity.
The tokens and their uniform scaling are fixed. Query masks and query
thresholds may vary with the scale, but must be fixed across target
permutations. Target-dependent common or block biases, adaptive residues,
nonuniformly scaled tokens, and poles comparable to the query addresses
are outside the statement.

Fix $H\geq1$, real tokens $a_1,\ldots,a_H$ and
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_H$, and a positive integer $p$.
For a permutation $\pi\in S_H$, consider $q$ scores
\[
 F_j(\pi)=\sum_{h=1}^H
 \frac{m_{hj}a_{\pi(h)}}{(t_j-\epsilon\lambda_{\pi(h)})^p}
 -\beta_j,\qquad t_j>0.                         \tag{1}
\]
The application motivating the note has $p=2$ and arbitrary masks.

\begin{theorem}
There is $\delta>0$, depending only on the fixed tokens and $p$, such
that whenever $0<\epsilon/t_j<\delta$ for every $j$, all denominators
in (1) are positive and the number of strict $q$-bit words attained by
varying $\pi$ is at most
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{H-1}\binom qi.                     \tag{2}
\]
In particular, this family cannot shatter $q\geq H$ queries. The masks,
thresholds, and query addresses in this assertion may depend arbitrarily
on $\epsilon$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $v(s)_y=a_y/(1-s\lambda_y)^p$ and let $X(s)$ be the finite
configuration of all coordinate permutations of $v(s)$. Every entry of
its affine-coordinate matrix, with rows $(1,P_\pi v(s))$, is rational
in $s$. Each minor is either identically zero or has a constant nonzero
sign throughout some sufficiently small interval $0<s<\delta$.
There are finitely many minors. Choose a common such interval that
also makes $1-s\lambda_y>0$ for all $y$.

We spell out why all strict affine dichotomies of $X(s)$ are constant
on this interval, including degenerate configurations. The rank is
constant, and one fixed collection of independent columns spans the
column space throughout a smaller interval. For a proposed dichotomy,
Gordan's alternative makes infeasibility equivalent to a nonzero
nonnegative dependence between the appropriately signed rows. A
nonnegative dependence of minimal support is a circuit: any independent
second dependence can be added until a positive coefficient vanishes.
For a circuit of size $k$, choose $k-1$ independent rows and $k-1$
columns on which they remain independent. Cramer's rule expresses the
unique circuit coefficients, up to a common nonzero factor, as signed
$(k-1)$-minors. Their signs and zeros are constant on the chosen
interval. Thus the existence of a nonnegative circuit is constant,
and Gordan's alternative proves the assertion about dichotomies.

Fix any $s_0$ in the interval. The sign of (1) is the sign of an affine
functional on $X(\epsilon/t_j)$, after multiplication by the positive
number $t_j^p$. Therefore its dichotomy on the permutations is also
realized by some affine functional on the single configuration
$X(s_0)$. Do this separately for each of the $q$ queries. All words
from (1) now occur among the regions of $q$ affine hyperplanes on the
affine span of $X(s_0)$. That span has dimension at most $H-1$, because
every point has the same coordinate sum. The usual hyperplane-region
recurrence gives at most $\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}\binom qi$ regions, also
when some hyperplanes or points are degenerate. Strict word classes
are convex intersections of open half spaces, so each uses at most
one region. If $q\geq H$, this sum is strictly smaller than $2^q$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The precise escape left open.}
The argument does not show uniform stability over a changing token
family $a_y(\epsilon),\lambda_y(\epsilon)$. Algebraic minors can
vanish to competing orders when poles or residues have several scales;
the query address can then change their leading signs. It also does
not transfer a bias that varies with the permutation: replacing each
query's separating affine functional independently need not preserve
such a shared numeric parameter. These are substantive gaps between
this fixed-pool result and the desired joint compiler.
