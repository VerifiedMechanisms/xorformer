Exact finite computation, not an asymptotic theorem

JointFamilyKrylovRank.py computes ranks modulo 65521 of the integer columns
  (1,x_1,...,x_k) * phi(x)^r,   phi(x)=sum_i 2^i x_i,
on the Boolean cube. The committed JSON records every prefix rank for k=1..10.
A nonzero modular minor proves that the corresponding integer columns are
linearly independent over the reals. A zero modular minor alone does NOT
prove real dependence; the rank deficiencies here are only modular findings.

The first degree giving full modular row rank is respectively
  0, 1, 2, 3, 6, 11, 18, 31, 57, 103.
These values make no assertion for larger k and establish no sign coverage.

Motivation and exact algebraic comparison

For distinct exterior poles b_h, the feature span generated by
  B(x)/(phi(x)+b_h), B in span(1,x_1,...,x_k),
equals the common positive factor 1/P(phi(x)) times
  span { B(x)*phi(x)^r : 0<=r<H }, P(t)=product_h(t+b_h).
Indeed P(t)/(t+b_h), h=1..H, are a basis of polynomials of degree below H:
their evaluations at the distinct points -b_g form a diagonal matrix with
nonzero diagonal entries. This is a written elementary proof, not a new
Lean declaration in this file.

The square-denominator features in the permutation tangent construction
need not have the same rank: multiplying by P(phi)^2 produces the squares
of these Lagrange polynomials and allows degree up to 2H-2. The finite
full-rank certificates for that feature family are recorded separately by
the large-domain agent. Neither rank assertion proves the independent-column
orthant-cover condition needed for an improved bound on W(n).

Reproduce the finite computation:
  heavy python3 artifacts/head-compression/JointFamilyKrylovRank.py --max-k 10 --output /tmp/joint-family-krylov-ranks.json
