\title{A Majority of Separated Comparisons as Two Positive Products}

\paragraph{Status and purpose.}
This is a complete elementary normalization with exact finite checks.
It is not a reverse-orientation head construction and gives no new
bound on worst-case head complexity by itself. Its purpose is to
isolate a structured joint transfer target with a fixed relative
margin, rather than a generic high-degree cleared numerator.
No Lean verification is asserted.

\begin{theorem}
Let $L\geq1$, $R\geq8L$, and $u_h,v_h>0$. Suppose each ratio
$r_h=u_h/v_h$ is either at least $R$ or at most $1/R$, and write
$s_h=1$ in the first case and $s_h=-1$ in the second. Put
\[
 P=\prod_{h=1}^L(2u_h+v_h),\qquad
 Q=\prod_{h=1}^L(u_h+2v_h).
\]
If $\sum_hs_h>0$, then $P/Q\geq3/2$. If $\sum_hs_h<0$,
then $P/Q\leq2/3$. Consequently the strict majority sign, whenever
nonzero, equals $\operatorname{sgn}(P-Q)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $g(r)=(2r+1)/(r+2)$. For every $r>0$, $g(r)>1/2$.
If $r\geq R$, then
\[
 g(r)=2-\frac3{r+2}\geq2\left(1-\frac2R\right).
\]
Let $a$ and $b$ be the counts of positive and negative votes.
If $a-b\geq1$, Bernoulli's inequality gives
\[
 \frac P Q=\prod_hg(r_h)
 \geq2^{a-b}\left(1-\frac2R\right)^a
 \geq2\left(1-\frac{2a}R\right)
 \geq2\left(1-\frac{2L}R\right)\geq\frac32.
\]
The base of the Bernoulli power is positive because $R\geq8$.
Swapping $u_h$ and $v_h$ negates every vote and exchanges $P,Q$;
this proves the negative-majority assertion. Odd $L$ guarantees
that one of the two cases applies, but oddness is otherwise unused.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Joint sign tables and the full cube.}
Suppose the comparison data are $u_h(i),v_h(j)>0$, and every grid
pair obeys the same separation hypothesis. On local Boolean variables
$z\in\{0,1\}^r$, $w\in\{0,1\}^s$, put
\[
 U_h(z)=\sum_i u_h(i)z_i,\qquad V_h(w)=\sum_jv_h(j)w_j.
\]
The theorem gives the majority signs on the one-hot grid using
$\prod_h(2U_h+V_h)-\prod_h(U_h+2V_h)$.
All factor slopes are strictly positive. The factors vanish at the
origin, so to make them strictly positive on the entire cube, add a
common constant $\delta>0$ to every factor. Let $b>0$ be the
minimum of all original factors over the finite grid and choose
$\delta\leq b/(8L)$. Each product increases by a factor between
one and $(1+\delta/b)^L$. For $0\leq t<1/L$,
\[
 (1+t)^L\leq\sum_{k\geq0}(Lt)^k=\frac1{1-Lt},
\]
since $\binom Lk\leq L^k$. Therefore a positive-majority ratio
remains at least $(3/2)(7/8)=21/16>1$ after this perturbation.
The reciprocal argument handles a negative majority. Thus this is
also a difference of two products of affine factors that have strict
positive slopes and are positive on the full local cube.

\paragraph{Exact remaining transfer problem.}
The score in this note is a difference of products, not a sum of
admissible affine fractions. A cyclic linear determinant represents
such a binomial, but elimination of that matrix introduces products
or non-affine rational entries. It does not supply heads in the
unchanged model. Likewise, replacing separate groups of votes by
their product differences introduces group-dependent positive
multipliers and need not preserve the outer sum. No efficient
negative-slope axis-zero realization is asserted here.
