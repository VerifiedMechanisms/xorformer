\paragraph{Status.}
This is an exact realization of a candidate family for the joint reverse
compiler, followed by finite exact sign experiments. It does not prove a
superconstant saving. In particular, the large number of discrete
assignments below is not a shattering theorem. No result in this note is
Lean formalized, and no model definition is changed.

Let $d\geq4$, $H=2^d$, and $G=H/2$. Choose a power of two $D$ with
\[
 d/4\leq D\leq d/2,
 \qquad k=\log_2D.
\]
Then $D\leq d-k$. Partition the vertices of the $d$-cube according to
the first $k$ bits, obtaining $D$ subcubes. In the subcube with address
$\ell\in\{1,\ldots,D\}$, match every vertex along coordinate $k+\ell$.
This is a perfect matching with exactly $G/D$ edges of each of the $D$
chosen coordinate directions.

Fix distinct positive numbers $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_G$. A balanced assignment
is a function $\ell:\{1,\ldots,G\}\to\{1,\ldots,D\}$ taking every value
$G/D$ times. The proposed score family, on query pairs $(v,t)$, is
\[
 f_{\ell,c}(v,t)=\sum_{g=1}^G
       \frac{c_gv_{\ell(g)}}{(t-\xi_g)^2},
 \qquad c\in\mathbb R^G.                         \tag{1}
\]
Here $v\in\mathbb R^D$ and $t>\max_g\xi_g$. The transfer below additionally
requires the compact full-cube budget on the finitely many query values.
It cannot be silently extended to arbitrary nearby query and token poles.

\begin{proposition}[Exact strict-sign transfer with $H$ heads]
Fix finitely many rows $(v_i,t_i)$, and finitely many columns
$(\ell_j,c^{(j)})$ of (1). Suppose
\[
 t_i=1-p_i,
 \quad p_i>0,
 \quad \xi_g>0,
 \quad \sum_i p_i+s\max_g\xi_g<1.                 \tag{2}
\]
Every strict sign in their score table is realized, simultaneously, by
$H=2G$ affine-ratio heads whose denominators are positive on the entire
Boolean $(r+s)$-cube and have strictly negative slopes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the fixed matching above. In each column $j$, biject its groups $g$
to matching edges of the requested color $\ell_j(g)$; balance permits
this. Orient each such edge as $h^-\to h^+$, where the selected bit
increases from zero to one. Write
\[
 V_i(h)=\sum_{a=1}^D(v_i)_a h_{k+a}.
\]
Assign the same column denominator coefficient $q_{hj}=\xi_g$ to the two
endpoints of the edge assigned group $g$. For $\alpha>0$, set
\[
 B_h(z,w)=1-\sum_i(p_i+\alpha V_i(h))z_i
                   -\sum_jq_{hj}w_j.
\]
Give the corresponding column numerator coefficients the values
$c_g^{(j)}/\alpha$ at $h^+$ and $-c_g^{(j)}/\alpha$ at $h^-$. Thus
$A_h(w)=\sum_j a_{hj}w_j$ is affine. At a grid point $(e_i,e_j)$, the
contribution of this edge is exactly
\[
 \frac{c_g^{(j)}}{\alpha}
 \left(\frac1{t_i-\xi_g-\alpha V_i(h^+)}
       -\frac1{t_i-\xi_g-\alpha V_i(h^-)}\right)
 =\frac{c_g^{(j)}(v_i)_{\ell_j(g)}}
 {(t_i-\xi_g-\alpha V_i(h^+))
  (t_i-\xi_g-\alpha V_i(h^-))}.                 \tag{3}
\]
Consequently its limit as $\alpha\downarrow0$ is the corresponding
summand of (1). There are finitely many entries, so all specified strict
signs survive for sufficiently small positive $\alpha$.

For completeness, let $M=\max_{i,h}|V_i(h)|$ and let
$\delta=1-\sum_i p_i-s\max_g\xi_g>0$. Choose $\alpha$ so small that
$\alpha M<\tfrac12\min_i p_i$ and $r\alpha M<\delta/2$; if $M=0$ these
conditions impose no restriction. Every slope coefficient
$p_i+\alpha V_i(h)$ is then positive, as is every $q_{hj}$. Moreover
\[
 B_h(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)
 \geq1-\sum_i p_i-r\alpha M-s\max_g\xi_g>\delta/2.
\]
This is the minimum on the full cube. It proves both original
orientation and positivity requirements. No numerical approximation
claim is used to infer universality: the limiting table itself must
already have the desired strict signs. The established general
strict-grid axis repair can subsequently be applied at constant cost.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Discrete assignment entropy and its limitation.}
There are
\[
 N=\frac{G!}{((G/D)!)^D}
 \geq \frac{D^G}{(G+1)^D}                       \tag{4}
\]
balanced assignments. Indeed the balanced multinomial coefficient is
maximal, there are at most $(G+1)^D$ count vectors, and all multinomial
coefficients sum to $D^G$. Since $D=\Theta(\log H)$, (4) gives
$\log N=\Theta(H\log\log H)$.

These assignments do define distinct analytic functions when every
$c_g=1$: the coefficient vector at the double pole $\xi_g$ records its
assigned coordinate. They also define distinct sign functions on the
open query domain. If two vector-valued sums had the same strict
halfspaces in the $v$ variable for every $t$, their coefficient vectors
would be positive scalar multiples. Their coordinate sums are the
same positive function $\sum_g(t-\xi_g)^{-2}$, so that scalar is one.
Rational-function uniqueness then identifies every assignment.

This proves only the number of distinct functions on an unrestricted
query domain. It gives no bound on the number of queries needed to
separate them. To obtain the desired compiler through this family one
still needs, for example, $O(H\log\log H)$ compact queries carrying
$\exp(\Omega(H\log\log H))$ different strict sign vectors. A Sauer
argument would then give a shattered subset of that same order.

\paragraph{Exact finite probes.}
The companion script uses the rational chamber enumerator from
\texttt{reverse-joint-affine-plane-search.py}. For each balanced
assignment, (1) is a linear $G$-plane in score space. Every reported sign
has a rational coefficient witness checked against the original
matrix. A total is called complete only when all the enumerated central
arrangements are simple. Degenerate totals are witness lower bounds.
The token poles are $\xi_g=(g+1)/10000$, and the row poles are close to
one. For $G=4$, $D=2$, seed 23, the complete results are
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r|r}
\text{row design}&r&\text{covered orthants}&\text{all orthants}\\\hline
\text{generic mixed rows}&7&84&128\\
\text{generic mixed rows}&9&186&512\\
\text{two coordinate clouds}&7&108&128\\
\text{two coordinate clouds}&9&282&512
\end{array}
\]
The generic rows gave the same oriented matroid for all six assignments.
The two-cloud construction uses
$v_i=(1,(-1)^i+10^{-8}e_i)$ with small integer $e_i$; it exposes additional
assignment-dependent signs, but no full shattering at these row counts.
For $G=D=4$, the 24 balanced assignments are permutations. With ten rows,
generic mixed profiles cover 260 of 1024 orthants, while a rank-two
leading profile matrix with an order-$10^{-8}$ perturbation covers 650.
These $G=D=4$ tests concern the abstract dictionary (1); they do not
meet the $D=\Theta(\log H)$ matching parameters above and are not claimed
to have an eight-head cube realization. The separate transfer test uses
the valid three-bit matching with $G=4,D=2$ and checks 18 strict signs,
72 exact pair identities, and all 512 vertices of its ambient cube.
These are finite exact facts only. Neither establishes a growing
row-to-head ratio, and no asymptotic conclusion is drawn.
