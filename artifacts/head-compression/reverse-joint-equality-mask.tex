\paragraph{Scope.}
This note audits a directly attempted construction for the requested
logarithmic or double-logarithmic saving. Erd\H{o}s--Moser paired orders
encode edge-disjoint unions of disjoint bicliques. Replacing each such
union by a highly accurate reverse-orientation equality mask might
have avoided the known per-vote reversal loss. The following theorem
shows that this numerical replacement also needs logarithmically many
heads when the number of components grows polynomially.
It is not an obstruction to a joint sign-only compiler, nonuniform
assembly, or arbitrary admissible head sums. It is a written proof,
not a Lean theorem, and it supplies no new unrestricted global bound.

\begin{theorem}
Let $k,H\geq1$ and $0\leq\epsilon<1/(2k)$. Suppose
\[
 F(z,w)=c+\sum_{h=1}^H\frac{A_h(z,w)}{B_h(z,w)}
\]
on the Boolean $2k$-cube has affine numerators and denominators, and
each denominator is positive throughout that cube with weakly
nonpositive slopes. Suppose, for arbitrary signs $\tau_i\in\{-1,1\}$,
\[
 |F(e_i,e_i)-\tau_i|\leq\epsilon,\qquad
 |F(e_i,e_j)|\leq\epsilon\quad(i\neq j).
\]
Then
\[
 k-4H^2\leq H2^H.
\]
In particular, for $k\geq1024$,
\[
 H\geq\log_2k-\log_2\log_2k-1.
\]
The same conclusion applies to a matched $k$ by $k$ subgrid of a
larger Boolean cube with the same whole-cube denominator conditions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Divide each numerator and denominator by the positive constant term
of its denominator. Its grid denominator is then
\[
 B_h(e_i,e_j)=1-\alpha_{hi}-\beta_{hj},\qquad
 \alpha_{hi},\beta_{hj}\geq0,qquad
 \sum_i\alpha_{hi}+\sum_j\beta_{hj}<1.
\]
The last inequality follows by evaluating at the all-one vertex.
For every head discard indices $i$ with
$\alpha_{hi}+\beta_{hi}>1/(4H)$. Fewer than $4H$ indices are
discarded for that head, and fewer than $4H^2$ altogether. Let $I$
be the remaining set and $q=|I|$, so $q\geq k-4H^2$.
For $i,j\in I$,
\[
 1-\frac1{2H}\leq B_h(e_i,e_j)\leq1.
\]
Consequently the product $D_{ij}=\prod_h B_h(e_i,e_j)$ satisfies
\[
 \frac12\leq\left(1-\frac1{2H}\right)^H
                    \leq D_{ij}\leq1.
\]
The first inequality is Bernoulli's inequality, including $H=1$.

Let $P_{ij}=D_{ij}F(e_i,e_j)$ for $i,j\in I$.
Its diagonal entries have absolute value at least
$(1-\epsilon)/2$, whereas its off-diagonal entries have absolute
value at most $\epsilon$. Since
\[
 (q-1)\epsilon\leq(k-1)\epsilon<(1-\epsilon)/2,
\]
the matrix $P$ is strictly diagonally dominant by rows and hence
nonsingular. For completeness, if $Pv=0$ with $v\neq0$, choose
$i$ with maximal $|v_i|>0$. Then
$|P_{ii}v_i|>\sum_{j\neq i}|P_{ij}v_j|$, contradicting the
$i$-th equation. Thus $\operatorname{rank}P=q$.

Absorb $c$ into the first numerator, replacing $A_1$ by
$A_1+cB_1$. The cleared grid matrix is
\[
 P_{ij}=\sum_{h=1}^H A_h(e_i,e_j)
                     \prod_{g\neq h}B_g(e_i,e_j).
\]
Each affine grid factor is a sum of a row function and a column
function. Expanding the $H$ factors in any summand therefore writes
it as at most $2^H$ matrices of rank at most one. Rank subadditivity
gives $q\leq H2^H$, proving the finite inequality.

For $k\geq1024$, one has $4(\log_2k)^2\leq k/2$.
If $H\geq\log_2k$, the final lower bound is immediate.
Otherwise $q\geq k/2$, and $H2^H\geq k/2$ implies
\[
 H\geq\log_2k-\log_2H-1
   \geq\log_2k-\log_2\log_2k-1.
\]
For a subgrid of a larger cube the selected nonnegative slope sums
are still less than one, so the same proof applies.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequence for the attempted paired-order route.}
A bilevel module with $k$ disjoint components contains a matched
subgrid obtained by choosing one row and one column from each
component. Its intended numerical values on that subgrid are signed
unit diagonal entries and zero off the diagonal. Thus the theorem
applies to a uniform approximation of that module, even though the
component values can have either sign. If $k=m^{\gamma+o(1)}$ for
fixed $\gamma>0$ and the error is $O(\log m/m)$, then the error
condition holds whenever $\gamma<1$, and the head cost is
$\Omega(\log m)$. In particular $k$ of order $\sqrt m$ is covered.
A plan using $O(m/\log m)$ such modules cannot obtain a saving by
compiling each one with $O(\log m/\log\log m)$ heads at this
accuracy. This rules out that proposed sufficient intermediate
lemma. It does not prove that every paired-order assembly must use
that uniform accuracy or that all decompositions have many components.

\paragraph{Why cancellation and tiny slopes do not bypass this proof.}
Numerator coefficients are unrestricted in magnitude, and all heads
can cancel arbitrarily. The argument does not approximate the score
by a Taylor polynomial. It bounds the exact separation rank after
clearing denominators. The only quantitative approximation is in the
specified target values. Denominator comparability is obtained by
discarding a small number of indices, directly from whole-cube
admissibility.
