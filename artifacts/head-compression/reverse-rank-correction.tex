\title{Exact reverse rank corrections}
\author{}
\date{}
\maketitle

\begin{theorem}
Let $r,s,L\geq0$, let $f_h:[r]\to\mathbb R$ and
$g_h:[s]\to\mathbb R$ for $1\leq h\leq L$, and let
$p:[r]\to\mathbb R$, $q:[s]\to\mathbb R$ and $c\in\mathbb R$.
There are $L+1$ affine ratios whose denominators are positive on the
entire Boolean $(r+s)$-cube and have strictly negative slopes such that
 their sum, with scalar bias $c$, has grid values
\[
 c+p(i)+q(j)+\sum_{h=1}^L f_h(i)g_h(j).
\]
No bound on the magnitudes of the supplied entries is required.
The construction may instead have zero values on both Boolean axes,
with no scalar bias, using $3(L+1)+3$ strictly admissible ratios.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each factor set
\[
 C_h=1+\sum_{i=1}^r|f_h(i)|,\qquad
 M_h=4(r+1)C_h,\qquad u_{hi}=C_h+f_h(i).
\]
Then $0<u_{hi}<2C_h$. Define
\[
 B_h(z,w)=1-\sum_{i=1}^r
       \frac{u_{hi}}{M_h+u_{hi}}z_i,\qquad
 A_h(z,w)=M_h\sum_{j=1}^s g_h(j)w_j.
\]
Every slope of $B_h$ is nonpositive. Since
$u_{hi}/(M_h+u_{hi})<1/(2(r+1))$, its minimum on the whole cube
is greater than $1-r/(2(r+1))>1/2$. On the promised grid,
\[
 \frac{A_h(e_i,e_j)}{B_h(e_i,e_j)}
  =(M_h+C_h+f_h(i))g_h(j).
\]
Put $T_h=M_h+C_h$. One further ratio with constant denominator $1$
and affine numerator
\[
 \sum_i p(i)z_i+
 \sum_j\left(q(j)-\sum_hT_hg_h(j)\right)w_j
\]
therefore gives exactly the stated matrix.

To restore strict slopes, consider any weak denominator $B$ above
in $m=r+s$ variables. Let $\delta=B(\boldsymbol1)>0$ and
$\epsilon=\delta/(m+1)$. Replace it by
\[
 B'(x)=B(x)+\epsilon\left(2-\sum_{k=1}^m x_k\right).
\]
Every slope is now strictly negative, and its cube minimum equals
$\delta+\epsilon(2-m)=3\delta/(m+1)>0$. The denominator is
unchanged at every grid point, which has Hamming weight two.
Thus this exact strictification costs no heads and changes no
represented entry. The separately checked exact axis-repair theorem
uses $3H+3$ heads for a strict grid score with $H$ heads, giving the
last assertion with $H=L+1$.
\end{proof}

No original HeadComplexity definitions are changed.
This is an exact low-rank correction theorem. It does not assert that
arbitrary sign matrices have sublinear rank, and it does not establish
the requested global logarithmic or double-logarithmic saving.
