The finite embedding, transposition, and one-batch lemmas are Lean checked
in \texttt{Results/ReverseGridEmbedding.lean}. The dyadic finite iteration,
same-cost coordinate restriction, and dyadic budget estimate are now Lean

\texttt{ReverseAxisRestriction.lean}. The natural-log asymptotic

The density hypothesis is unproved at any growing logarithmic-saving

All logarithms in the rate estimates are natural.

\begin{lemma}[Embedding and one-time masking]
Let a strict reverse grid module on an $a\times b$ grid use $H$ heads.
It can be embedded into any specified $a\times b$ subrectangle of a
larger $R\times S$ grid with the same $H$ heads, preserving all grid
values in that rectangle and positivity on the entire larger cube.
Given row multipliers in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and a column mask, there is then
an axis-zero module of $12H+21$ heads with the multiplied grid values
and exact zero grid values outside the selected rectangle.
Every final denominator has strictly negative slopes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Extend each affine numerator and denominator by zero coefficients on
the unused coordinates. Each denominator at an ambient cube vertex
equals the old denominator on its restriction, so remains positive.
The new slopes are nonpositive. For each head, choose a sufficiently
small $\varepsilon>0$ and add
$\varepsilon(2-\sum_k x_k)$ to its denominator. This retains positivity
on the entire finite cube, makes every slope strictly negative, and
changes no weight-two grid value.

The checked row-multiplier construction costs $2H+2$ heads, with
row coefficient zero outside the desired row set. The checked column
multiplier costs $2(2H+2)+2=4H+6$ heads. The strict axis repair costs
$3(4H+6)+3=12H+21$ and preserves every grid value exactly.
Apply this procedure separately to each original batch. In particular,
the factor $12$ is not repeatedly applied to an already assembled sum.

These are the declarations
\texttt{exists\_strict\_reverse\_grid\_embedding},
\texttt{exists\_reverse\_axis\_grid\_patch}, and
\texttt{exists\_half\_dense\_reverse\_rectangle\_batch}.
The declaration \texttt{ReverseAxisData.transpose\_grid} checks the
zero-cost transposition of both blocks, including both axis identities.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Balanced half-density suffices]
Suppose there is a constant $C$ such that for every pair of positive
integers $a,b$ with $a\leq b\leq2a+1$, there is a family
$G_{a,b}\subseteq\{-1,1\}^a$ of size at least $2^{a-1}$ with the
following joint property. Every $a\times b$ table whose columns belong
to $G_{a,b}$ has a strict reverse grid realization of at most
\[
 C\left(1+\frac{a}{\log(a+2)}\right)
\]
heads, all denominators positive on the entire $(a+b)$-cube.
Then every $r\times s$ sign table has a strict reverse axis-zero
realization with
\[
 O_C\left(1+\frac{r+s}{\log(r+s+2)}\right)
\]
heads. In the unchanged HeadComplexity model, the audited balanced
gluing reduction then gives $W(n)=O_C(2^n/(n\log n))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First treat an $m\times m$ table. At a stage let its remaining rectangle
have dimensions $a\leq b\leq2a+1$. For each column pattern $v$, a
uniform row sign vector $d$ makes $d\cdot v$ uniform on the pattern
cube. Therefore the expected number of columns outside $G_{a,b}$ is
at most $b/2$. Some common $d$ sends at least $\lceil b/2\rceil$
columns into the good family. Select exactly that many such columns.
Fill all other columns with any one good pattern and apply the joint
compiler. The embedding lemma multiplies the rows back by $d$ and
masks precisely the selected columns, while setting every other
ambient grid value to zero. It costs at most
\[
 12C\left(1+\frac{a}{\log(a+2)}\right)+21.
\]

Remove these columns and transpose the remaining rectangle. Its new
dimensions are $a'=\lfloor b/2\rfloor$, $b'=a$. If $a'=0$, the process
stops. Otherwise $a'\leq b'\leq2a'+1$, so the same balanced hypothesis
applies again. Every two stages halve both dimensions, with integer
rounding downward. Each original grid point is handled in exactly one
batch. All the other batches are exactly zero there. Thus the sum
preserves every strict sign and both complete axis identities.

The row sizes at successive stages are
\[
 m,\ \lfloor m/2\rfloor,\ \lfloor m/2\rfloor,
 \ \lfloor m/4\rfloor,\ \lfloor m/4\rfloor,\ldots,
\]
until zero. Consequently the total head budget is at most
\[
 2\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor}
 \left[12C\left(1+
 \frac{m2^{-k}}{\log(m2^{-k}+2)}\right)+21\right].
 \tag{1}
\]
The function $x/\log(x+2)$ is increasing for $x\geq0$, which justifies
replacing integer row sizes by these upper bounds.

For completeness the sum in (1) is $O_C(m/\log(m+2))$. For the indices
with $m2^{-k}\geq\sqrt m$, the denominator is at least
$\tfrac12\log m$, and their numerators sum to at most $2m$.
The remaining numerators sum to at most $2\sqrt m$ and their
denominators are at least $\log2$. The number of constant summands is
$O(\log m)$. Both $\sqrt m$ and $\log m$ are
$O(m/\log(m+2))$. The finitely many small $m$ are absorbed in the
constant. This proves the square-grid bound.

For an arbitrary $r\times s$ table, extend its labels arbitrarily to
an $m\times m$ square with $m=\max(r,s)$. Restrict the resulting affine
ratios to the original coordinates by setting the unused coordinates
to zero. Positivity, strict negativity of every remaining slope, both
axis identities, and the required grid signs are all preserved with
no extra heads. Since $m\leq r+s$, the claimed local bound follows.

Finally use the already audited cover and gluing theorem with
$a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, $b=\lceil n/2\rceil$. It uses
$O(2^n/n^2)$ local modules, each of cost $O_C(n/\log n)$, plus
$O(2^{\lceil n/2\rceil})$ exceptional-face correction heads.
The latter term is $O(2^n/(n\log n))$, proving the conditional global
conclusion with the original atoms and original input dimension.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why the balanced qualification matters.}
This argument needs a shared decoder and full-cube budget only for
balanced rectangles. It does not require one fixed finite-scale decoder
to accommodate arbitrarily many opposite-block coordinates. Thus a
construction that works at a suitable size-dependent compact pole scale
could suffice; an all-small-scale continuation theorem is not a premise.
Separate realizability of each good column does not establish the joint
hypothesis, and a large number of zero branches does not establish the
required half-density.

\paragraph{Checked dyadic statements.}
The declaration \texttt{exists\_alternating\_dense\_square\_patch}
constructs the full embedded dyadic square, with strict signs and exact
zero outside. The compiler declarations
\texttt{hasReverseAxisCompiler\_of\_alternating\_half\_dense} and
\texttt{hasReverseAxisCompiler\_of\_alternating\_half\_dense\_le}
handle dyadic squares and all smaller rectangles. The latter uses
\texttt{HasReverseAxisCompiler.restrict}, which preserves the head count,
whole-cube positivity, strictly negative slopes, and both full Boolean
axis identities.

The exact budget is $B_0=12M(1,1)+21$ and
\[
 B_{k+1}=12M(2^{k+1},2^{k+1})+21+
          12M(2^k,2^{k+1})+21+B_k.
\]
If each already masked square or rectangular batch at row size $2^k$
costs at most $C2^k/(k+2)$, then the Lean declaration
\texttt{alternatingDenseBudget\_le} proves
$B_k\leq8C2^k/(k+2)$ for every $k$. These are conditional statements:
no logarithmic-rate half-dense family is supplied by these declarations.

\paragraph{Relation to the earlier checked batching bound.}
\texttt{ReverseDensityBatching.lean} keeps the query dimension fixed
through all batches and obtains $t(12M+21)+3R+6$. Used directly with
$M=O(m/\log m)$ and $t\asymp\log\log m$, that bound loses a
$\log\log m$ factor. The alternating argument changes the query
dimension after every batch and sums decreasing costs. No earlier
theorem is contradicted or assumed stronger than its statement.
