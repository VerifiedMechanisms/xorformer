\paragraph{Status.}
These are complete finite written calculations. They do not supply an
upper bound beyond the existing linear saving. They settle the proposed
repair that adds arbitrary multiples of the two layer equations to an
individual denominator while preserving its values on a product of open
downward stars. In particular, allowing separate headwise baselines does
not remove the stated obstruction.

\begin{theorem}[Exact extension criterion]
Let $r,s\geq2$, let $w_1,\ldots,w_r$ and
$v_1,\ldots,v_s$ be real numbers, and let $\varepsilon\in\mathbb R$.
On the product of the two co-one-hot sets, prescribe the denominator
values
\[
 d_{ij}=\varepsilon+w_i+v_j.
\]
There is an affine function $B(x,y)$ with nonnegative coefficients,
strictly positive constant coefficient, and these prescribed values at
$(\mathbf1-e_i,\mathbf1-e_j)$ if and only if
\[
 \varepsilon+\sum_i w_i+\sum_j v_j
 -(r-1)\max_iw_i-(s-1)\max_jv_j>0.
 \tag{1}
\]
The coefficient nonnegativity and positive constant ensure positivity
on the entire Boolean cube, not merely on the prescribed layer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $B=b_0+\sum_i b_i x_i+\sum_j c_jy_j$ and set
$D=b_0+\sum_i b_i+\sum_j c_j$. The prescribed values say
$D-b_i-c_j=\varepsilon+w_i+v_j$. Comparing two rows and
then two columns gives constants $\alpha,\beta$ such that
\[
 b_i=\alpha-w_i,\qquad c_j=\beta-v_j.
\]
Substituting in any one prescribed value gives
\[
 b_0=\varepsilon+\sum_iw_i+\sum_jv_j
       -(r-1)\alpha-(s-1)\beta.
\]
Nonnegative slopes require $\alpha\geq\max_iw_i$ and
$\beta\geq\max_jv_j$. As $r,s\geq2$, the largest possible
constant coefficient is exactly the left side of (1). Thus (1) is
necessary. For sufficiency, choose $\alpha=\max_iw_i$ and
$\beta=\max_jv_j$. These choices give the required slopes and
strictly positive constant. If strict positive slopes are desired, both
constants can be increased by sufficiently small positive amounts while
retaining the strict inequality.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Starting with
$\varepsilon+\sum_iw_i(1-x_i)+\sum_jv_j(1-y_j)$, the most
general affine alteration vanishing on this product layer is
\[
 \alpha\left(\sum_i x_i-(r-1)\right)
 +\beta\left(\sum_j y_j-(s-1)\right).
\]
Thus the theorem includes arbitrary separate row and column layer
shifts; a shift using only total Hamming weight is a special case.
The freedom to choose another additive splitting of $d_{ij}$ does not
help: replacing $w_i$ by $w_i+a$, $v_j$ by $v_j+b$, and
$\varepsilon$ by $\varepsilon-a-b$ leaves (1) unchanged.

\begin{corollary}[A geometric equality denominator fails the repair]
Let $r=s=m\geq3$, $B\geq4$, and $w_i=v_i=B^{i-1}$.
If $0<\varepsilon\leq1$, the denominator values
$\varepsilon+w_i+v_j$ have no extension of the kind above.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Set $W=B^{m-1}$. The geometric sum satisfies
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^mB^{i-1}<\frac{B}{B-1}W\leq\frac43W.
\]
Consequently the left side of (1) is strictly less than
$1+\frac83W-2(m-1)W\leq1-\frac43W<0$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What this does not exclude.}
The conclusion concerns preserving each denominator's local values.
It does not exclude resynthesis of the whole signed sum, additional
heads, a new nonnegative equality kernel, or a different covering and
gluing scheme. Such a mechanism must be constructed separately; it
does not follow from a layer shift or a headwise additive gauge.
