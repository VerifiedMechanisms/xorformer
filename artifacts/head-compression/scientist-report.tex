\paragraph{Target status.}
This run has not proved that an absolute constant $C>0$ satisfies
$W(n)\le C2^n/n^2$ for every positive integer $n$. No Lean declaration
of that statement was added. No construction in the record should be
read as establishing it.

\paragraph{Formal results.}
The inventory lists $80$ principal and companion declarations across
$31$ new Lean modules. The full project build passed with $9215$ jobs.
The inspected declarations use only \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
Among the construction results are

\texttt{HeadComplexity.affineFraction\_pair\_merge\_error\_le}

also verify an exact three-head cycle producing a checkerboard correction
on one face and zero correction on the opposite face. None asserts
universal existence of a small score. The new declarations
\texttt{HeadComplexity.booleanProjection\_vanishing\_iff\_linearCombination}
and \texttt{HeadComplexity.booleanProjection\_multilinear\_ext}
in \texttt{Results/BooleanProjectionIdeal.lean} check the exact projection

\texttt{Results/WeightedRootCompletion.lean}, including

target rate. The local maximum principle for reciprocal features
proves local convex support but supplies no sparsity bound.

\paragraph{Finite certificate.}
The file \texttt{certificates/random-n14-anchored-h292.json} gives
$292$ heads for one fixed fourteen-bit Boolean function. All $16384$
labels agree with the original trial target. An independent integer
audit verifies every strict sign and every denominator's whole-cube
admissibility. Its minimum margin exceeds $0.999999$.
Separate certificates for its original lower and upper faces use
$146$ and $147$ heads; their audited gluing uses $293$ heads.
These examples do not bound the maximum over fourteen-bit functions.

\paragraph{Unsuccessful routes.}
Fixed-denominator spanning, bounded-cancellation constructions,
local-cell packing, arithmetic memory decoding, dominant-term
constructions, and restricted confluent-jet constructions did not
produce the target bound. The writeup distinguishes proved obstructions
to specified subclasses from theorem-strength gaps in proposed general
constructions. Numerical failures and solver timeouts are never used
as lower bounds. The later pruning and denominator-coalescence outcomes
are recorded separately in the final continuation diagnostics.

\paragraph{Latest analytical audit.}
The elementary proof in \texttt{boolean-moment-tangent-audit.tex}
shows that for the explicit $(n+1)$-root Boolean permutation construction,
first-order product differences independent of the auxiliary variable
are precisely affine functions when $n\ge4$. This is a manual theorem
about that construction, not an additional Lean declaration or a
universal head upper bound. The continuation also classifies zero first
variations and proves a necessary sparsity condition at second order.
A separate level-set count excludes first-order ideal perturbations of
any target-dependent permutation list against a constant reference list
at the requested asymptotic rate. The later proof in
\texttt{laplace-sign-obstruction.tex} closes all ideal product
differences and ideal analytic leading terms at the target rate,
including two nonconstant lists, input-dependent root multisets,
arbitrary perturbation order, and repeated base roots. Positive
convolution kernels force common Laplace signs. Boolean exponential
factorization then gives too few sign tables. This is a manual
theorem about the construction family, not the full head model:
an explicit admissible score has no common strictly aligned Laplace
time. General cancellation across times remains available. A separate
manual count in \texttt{nonideal-variation-audit.tex} excludes fixed-root
analytic variations whose auxiliary degree is $o(n)$, using the new
low-level-fiber invariant. That note also gives a finite admissible
compiler for nonideal variations and an explicit example with no
common aligned Laplace time.
The separate note \texttt{boolean-reciprocal-cancellation.tex} gives
an exact three-term Boolean-only reciprocal identity and a projective
strict-convexity obstruction for relations with only one positive term.
Neither provides a universal sign construction.
