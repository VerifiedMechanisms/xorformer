\title{Parity limits normalized correlation of one shared affine-fraction atom}
\maketitle

\paragraph{Status.}
The parity energy inequality below, including arbitrary independent
columns, is kernel checked in
\texttt{HeadComplexity/Results/JointFamilyParityWeakLearner.lean}.
Its declarations are
\texttt{jointFamily\_affineFraction\_parity\_sq\_le\_two\_energy}
and
\texttt{jointFamily\_affineFraction\_columns\_parity\_sq\_le\_two\_energy}.
The matching-pursuit accounting after the theorem is a written argument.
This is an obstruction to a particular uniform weak-learning route,
not a lower bound on arbitrary sums of xorformer heads.

\begin{theorem}
Let $k\geq1$, $M=2^k$, $b>0$, and $w_i\geq0$. For arbitrary real
$a_0,a_i$, put
\[
 f(x)=\frac{a_0+\sum_i a_i x_i}{b+\sum_i w_i x_i},
 \qquad x\in\{0,1\}^k,
 \qquad L(f)=\sum_x(-1)^{|x|}f(x).
\]
Then
\[
 L(f)^2\leq 2\sum_x f(x)^2.
\]
More generally, for arbitrary columns $f_j$ of this form and arbitrary
real $\tau_j$,
\[
 \left(\sum_j\tau_j L(f_j)\right)^2
 \leq 2\left(\sum_j\tau_j^2\right)
              \left(\sum_{j,x}f_j(x)^2\right).
\]
The denominators and numerators may vary independently with the column;
in particular the conclusion holds when row slopes are shared.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a nonempty coordinate face $S$, write $L_S$ for its alternating sum,
based at zero. Since multiplication by the affine denominator gives an
affine function, every face with $|S|\geq2$ satisfies
\[
 \left(b+\sum_{i\in S}w_i\right)L_S(f)
   =\sum_{i\in S}w_i L_{S\setminus\{i\}}(f).
\]
The weights on the right, after division by the positive factor on the
left, have sum at most one. Induction therefore gives
\[
 |L_S(f)|\leq\max_{i\in S}|f(0)-f(e_i)|.
\]
Each edge difference satisfies
$|f(0)-f(e_i)|^2\leq2(f(0)^2+f(e_i)^2)\leq2\sum_x f(x)^2$.
This proves the first assertion. Cauchy--Schwarz in the column index
proves the second.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact shared-family consequence.}
Let the target matrix be $Y_{xj}=(-1)^{|x|}\tau_j$, with $\tau\neq0$.
Its squared Frobenius norm is $M\sum_j\tau_j^2$. Thus every nonzero
single affine-fraction column family $F$ obeys
\[
 \frac{\langle Y,F\rangle^2}{\|Y\|_F^2\|F\|_F^2}\leq\frac2M.
\]
The conclusion allows much more column freedom than the original model,
so the original admissibility restrictions cannot rescue this
single-atom correlation claim. Reversing all row coordinates changes
parity only by a global sign and preserves the bound.

\paragraph{The exact failed gain estimate.}
A standard normalized matching-pursuit guarantee with correlation at
least $\gamma$ at every residual gives
\[
 \|R_H\|_F^2\leq Ms\exp(-\gamma^2H)
\]
for an $M$ by $s$ sign matrix. To infer all strict signs by driving this
bound below one requires $H>\log(Ms)/\gamma^2$.
For $M=2^k$ and $s$ comparable to $k$, a local budget
$H=O(M/\sqrt{k})$ would require $\gamma^2$ of order
$k^{3/2}/M$. The parity family instead forces
$\gamma^2\leq2/M$. Consequently this uniform one-atom correlation
argument cannot establish the desired local budget. This says nothing
about a better analysis of a particular algorithm, nor about repair of
an explicitly bounded exceptional set with a separately proved cost.

\paragraph{The remaining positive possibility.}
Signed combinations of two or more heads can cancel large low-order
components. Their norm can be much smaller than the sum of the norms of
the individual heads, so the theorem does not extend by replacing one
head with a signed block. A useful block weak learner must prove a
uniform correlation gain after charging every head in the block,
column selection, and all strict-sign or exception repair costs.
Parity alone is insufficient evidence for such a gain.
