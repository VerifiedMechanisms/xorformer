\paragraph{Status.}
These are complete written finite calculations, not Lean declarations.
They audit one proposed global assembly operation. They neither bound
the minimum number of heads for an AND sign pattern nor exclude a
different joint compiler. In particular, an AND sign pattern alone has
an affine threshold representation. The issue here is multiplication
of an existing ratio while retaining its values.

\begin{proposition}[One literal and iterated literal differences]
Let $B>0$ on a finite Boolean cube and let $N$ be affine. Suppose $B$
does not depend on $k$ designated variables $z_1,\ldots,z_k$. For
positive $M_1,\ldots,M_k$, put
\[
 F_M(z)=\sum_{S\subseteq[k]}(-1)^{|S|}
       \frac{N}{B+\sum_{i\in S}M_i z_i}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The score is exactly zero whenever some $z_i=0$. As all $M_i$ tend to
infinity, uniformly on the finite cube,
\[
 F_M(z)\longrightarrow
 \frac{N}{B}\prod_{i=1}^k z_i.
 \tag{2}
\]
For $k=1$, the score uses two heads. If the $z_i$ are positive literals
and $B$ has nonnegative slopes, all displayed denominators have that
orientation and are positive on the whole cube. The same assertion
holds for negative literals $z_i=1-x_i$ and a denominator $B$ with
nonpositive slopes in the original coordinates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $z_i=0$, pair a subset not containing $i$ with the subset obtained
by adding $i$. Their denominators agree and their coefficients have
opposite signs. At $z_1=\cdots=z_k=1$, the empty subset contributes
$N/B$, and each other summand tends to zero. There are finitely many
cube points and finitely many terms, which proves uniform convergence.
Adding the indicated nonnegative literal penalties preserves positivity
and preserves the common slope orientation. Thus the construction is
valid before the usual final strict-slope perturbation. No positivity
claim about a sum of heads is needed for denominator admissibility.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[No linear compression within the shifted family]
Fix $b>0$ and $M_i>0$. The $2^k$ functions on $\{0,1\}^k$
\[
 h_S(z)=\frac1{b+\sum_{i\in S}M_i z_i},
 \qquad S\subseteq[k],
 \tag{3}
\]
are linearly independent. Consequently, the exact finite-$M$ score
$\sum_S(-1)^{|S|}h_S$ cannot be expressed as a linear combination of a
proper subfamily of these particular heads. The coefficients in this
statement may be arbitrary real numbers.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Index a cube point by $T=\{i:z_i=1\}$ and define
\[
 f(U)=\frac1{b+\sum_{i\in U}M_i},
 \qquad
 g(U)=\sum_{V\subseteq U}(-1)^{|U|-|V|}f(V).
\]
The evaluation matrix is $A_{S,T}=f(S\cap T)$. Let
$Z_{S,U}=1$ when $U\subseteq S$ and $Z_{S,U}=0$ otherwise. Boolean
M\"obius inversion gives
\[
 A=Z\,\operatorname{diag}(g(U))\,Z^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Order subsets by nondecreasing cardinality, breaking ties in any fixed
way. The matrix $Z$ is triangular with diagonal entries one.
Each diagonal entry in (4) is nonzero: the elementary reciprocal
integral gives
\[
 g(U)=\int_0^\infty e^{-bt}
           \prod_{i\in U}(e^{-M_i t}-1)\,dt.
 \tag{5}
\]
For every $t>0$, the integrand has strict sign $(-1)^{|U|}$.
It is integrable because the absolute value of each product factor
is at most one and $b>0$. Hence $g(U)\ne0$ for every $U$.
Both factors $Z$ and the diagonal factor in (4) are invertible, so
$A$ is invertible. The final assertion follows from uniqueness of
coordinates in an independent family and the fact that every
coefficient $(-1)^{|S|}$ is nonzero.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What a tree would have to share.}
For a negative-orientation ratio $N/B$ independent of $x_i$, the valid
downset-literal gate is
\[
 \frac{N}{B}-\frac{N}{B+M(1-x_i)}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The second term tends to the inherited function $(N/B)x_i$.
In a center tree, it therefore carries the entire child function on
the larger face, not just its values on the parent's one-hot grid.
An arbitrary parent grid compiler cannot be substituted for this
residual solely because their grid values agree. For example, the
function $z_1z_2$ is zero on the zero vector and every one-hot vector,
but is not zero at $(1,1)$. This elementary example isolates the
logical gap in the proposed substitution; it does not assert that
this particular polynomial is a hard head-complexity instance.

The independence proposition rules out telescoping the literal
differences within their own fixed shifted family. It leaves open
the use of new denominators, approximate cancellation with a proved
uniform error bound, or a global construction that constrains all
multihot values from the outset. No such construction is claimed here.
