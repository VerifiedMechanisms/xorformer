\paragraph{Status and scope.}
This is a complete written audit, not a Lean formalization. It does not
prove an improved asymptotic bound for head complexity. It isolates the
precise output-value and input-geometry requirements of the thin-shell
route. The scalar rational lower bound below concerns the explicitly
specified scalar compiler, not unrestricted attention heads on a Boolean
cube.

\section{A direct derivation of the shell batching mechanism}

The starting point is Michael Schmitt, \emph{Neural Networks with Local
Receptive Fields and Superlinear VC Dimension}, ECCC TR01-045 (2001),
Definition 2 and Theorem 2, especially pages 12--16 of
\url{https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2001/045/download/}.
The following proof gives the required finite-sample shell values
directly.

\begin{theorem}[Finite shell batching]
Let $v_1,\ldots,v_R\in\mathbb R^m$, where $R=h(m+1)$.
Assume that every nonempty subset of at most $m+1$ base points is
contained in a sphere of positive radius that contains no other base
point. For every binary labeling of
\[
 X=\{(v_i,e_j):1\le i\le R,\ 1\le j\le q\}
 \subset\mathbb R^{m+q},
\]
there are at most $h+2^q$ spherical shells whose indicator functions
sum to exactly the labeling on $X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}$, let $C_A$ be the rows whose
positive columns are exactly $A$. Partition each nonempty $C_A$ into
batches $S$ of size at most $m+1$. There are at most
\[
 \sum_{A:C_A\ne\varnothing}
       \left\lceil\frac{|C_A|}{m+1}\right\rceil
 \le \frac{R}{m+1}+2^q=h+2^q
\]
batches. Empty-positive-column batches may be omitted.

For one batch $S\subseteq C_A$, choose its sphere with center $c$ and
radius $r>0$. Write
\[
 \tau_i=\|v_i-c\|^2-r^2.
\]
Thus $\tau_i=0$ exactly for $i\in S$. Choose $s>0$ with $s<1/2$
and $4s<|\tau_i|$ for every $i\notin S$. Such an $s$ exists because
the list of nonzero residuals is finite. Put
\[
 d_j=\begin{cases}0&j\in A,\\-s&j\notin A,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 \rho^2=r^2+1+(q-|A|)s^2.
\]
For every sample point,
\[
 \|(v_i,e_j)-(c,d)\|^2-\rho^2
   =\tau_i+2s\,\boldsymbol1_{j\notin A}.
\]
The shell whose squared-distance residual has absolute value less
than $s$ therefore contains precisely $S\times A$. Indeed its
residual is zero on this rectangle, is $2s$ on
$S\times A^c$, and has absolute value greater than $2s$ off $S$.
Both shell radii $\sqrt{\rho^2-s}$ and $\sqrt{\rho^2+s}$ are real
and positive. Every positive sample belongs to exactly its own
batch's shell, and every negative sample belongs to none.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The required vote accuracy]
Suppose $K$ shell indicators sum to a binary labeling on a finite
sample. If each is replaced by a real-valued function with uniform
absolute error at most $\varepsilon$, where $K\varepsilon<1/2$,
then the sum of the replacements minus $1/2$ has the required strict
sign at every sample.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The total error has absolute value at most $K\varepsilon$ by the
triangle inequality. The original score is either $-1/2$ or $1/2$.
\end{proof}

Thus $\varepsilon=1/(4K)$ suffices. Correct shell membership signs
alone provide no such conclusion: a negative shell score can have
arbitrarily large magnitude and overwhelm the one active positive
shell.

\paragraph{The spheres are not supporting spheres.}
The proof requires $\tau_i\ne0$ off a batch, not $\tau_i>0$.
For the one-dimensional base points $0,1,2,3$, the sphere through
the batch $\{0,2\}$ has center $1$ and radius $1$. The excluded
base point $1$ is inside and the excluded base point $3$ is outside.
These four distinct real points satisfy the needed one-dimensional
general-position conditions. Accordingly Schmitt's proof explicitly
treats both sides of every sphere. A one-sided pole near a supporting
boundary does not directly supply these votes.

\section{The Boolean input and one-hot input budgets}

\begin{proposition}[Boolean general-position obstruction]
Let $m\ge1$. A subset of the Boolean $m$-cube in spherical general
position has at most $m+1$ elements. More generally, a subset in
affine general position has at most $2m$ elements, and the latter
bound remains true after any invertible affine transformation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All Boolean vertices lie on the sphere with center
$(1/2,\ldots,1/2)$ and radius $\sqrt m/2$. Spherical general position
forbids $m+2$ points on an $(m-1)$-dimensional sphere, giving the first
bound. For the second, each of the coordinate faces $x_1=0$ and
$x_1=1$ lies in an affine hyperplane. Affine general position permits
at most $m$ selected points in each. Invertible affine transformations
preserve affine dependence.
\end{proof}

The full spherical-general-position hypothesis used to guarantee
Schmitt's arbitrary batches therefore does not specialize directly
to many Boolean base points. A different batching argument could
weaken that hypothesis, but it would be a new construction.

Alternatively, one can encode the $R$ real base points by $R$ Boolean
one-hot row coordinates. Every prescribed row function can then be
put in the coefficients of an affine form. This consumes $R$ input
coordinates, not $m$. Moreover the exact support of each shell in
the construction above is a rectangle $S\times A$.

\begin{proposition}[An exact rectangle obstruction]
If shell batching on an $n$ by $n$ one-hot grid is used solely through
its exact nonnegative rectangular supports, some Boolean matrices
require at least $n$ shells.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take the identity matrix. Every nonempty rectangle contained in its
positive support consists of one diagonal entry: two distinct
diagonal entries in one rectangle force an off-diagonal entry in
that rectangle. All $n$ positive entries must be covered.
\end{proof}

Splitting the columns into blocks does not change this example. In
particular the theorem's numerical budget $h+2^q$, even with
$q$ of logarithmic size, cannot by itself be read as an $o(n)$-head
compiler for arbitrary $n$ by $n$ grids. Allowing signed cancellation
between new nonrectangular votes would be a different mechanism.

\section{A quantitative obstruction to a scalar shell compiler}

The squared distance on a product sample is additive in its row and
column indices. It is tempting to apply a rational scalar bump to
that distance. The following elementary theorem makes one limitation
of that transfer precise.

\begin{theorem}[Real-pole scalar flattening costs logarithmically]
Let $H\ge1$ and let
\[
 F(t)=c+\sum_{a=1}^H
       \frac{\alpha_a+\beta_a t}{\gamma_a+\delta_a t},
 \qquad -1\le t\le1,
\]
where every denominator is strictly positive throughout $[-1,1]$.
If $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$, $F(0)\ge1-\varepsilon$, and
$|F(t)|\le\varepsilon$ for all $t\in[1/2,1]$, then
\[
 \frac12\le \varepsilon\,16^H.
\]
In fact it suffices to require the small-value bound at the $H+1$
points $t_j=(H+j)/(2H)$ for $0\le j\le H$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Normalize each denominator by its positive value at zero. It becomes
$1+a_at$ with $|a_a|<1$. Thus
\[
 Q(t)=\prod_{a=1}^H(1+a_at),\qquad P(t)=Q(t)F(t)
\]
satisfy $Q(0)=1$, $0<Q(t)\le2^H$ on $[0,1]$, and
$\deg P\le H$. Hence $|P(t_j)|\le\varepsilon2^H$.
For the Lagrange interpolation basis at these points,
\[
 |L_j(0)|
 =\frac{\prod_{0\le k\le H,\ k\ne j}(H+k)}{j!(H-j)!}
 =\frac{(2H)!}{(H-1)!(H+j)j!(H-j)!}.
\]
Since $H+j\ge H$, summing gives
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^H|L_j(0)|
 \le \frac{(2H)!}{H!}
             \sum_{j=0}^H\frac1{j!(H-j)!}
 =2^H\binom{2H}{H}
 \le8^H.
\]
Interpolation therefore implies
\[
 \frac12\le1-\varepsilon\le P(0)
 \le\sum_{j=0}^H|P(t_j)||L_j(0)|
 \le\varepsilon16^H.
\]
\end{proof}

For $K$ summable shell votes, the sufficient error $1/(4K)$ in the
preceding lemma forces $16^H\ge2K$ for a compiler satisfying this
scalar theorem's uniform requirement. Thus its cost is
$\Omega(\log K)$ per shell. When the original shell batching gain is
of order $q\asymp\log K$, this cost erases that gain.

This conclusion has precise limitations. A specially chosen finite
sample need not contain the interpolation nodes. An original
attention head may use coordinates other than the scalar distance.
An affine line inside the continuous cube contains at most two
Boolean vertices. The theorem is consequently not a lower bound
for all admissible heads, and does not exclude a joint, geometry
dependent shell synthesis. Such a synthesis, with whole-cube
admissibility and summable votes, is the missing transfer step.
