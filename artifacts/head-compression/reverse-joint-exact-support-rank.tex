\paragraph{Status and scope.}
The following complete written proof rules out one proposed joint
replacement for an Erd\H{o}s--Moser pair of orders. It allows arbitrary
nonzero amplitudes on the pair's support and exact cancellation outside
that support. It is not a lower bound for an arbitrary sign table or for
worst-case head complexity. It neither proves target (A) nor target (B).
It is not Lean formalized. The original definitions are unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Cleared separation rank]
Let $H\geq1$ and let a matrix of grid scores be
\[
 F_{ij}=c+\sum_{h=1}^H
       \frac{a_{hi}+b_{hj}}{p_{hi}+q_{hj}},
 \qquad p_{hi}+q_{hj}\ne0.
\]
Put $D_{ij}=\prod_{h=1}^H(p_{hi}+q_{hj})$ and $P_{ij}=D_{ij}F_{ij}$.
Then
\[
 \operatorname{rank}P\leq 2^{H+1}-2.
\]
If an arbitrary rank-at-most-$L$ matrix $R$ is added to the displayed
score, the corresponding cleared matrix has rank at most
$(L+2)2^H-2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Absorb $c$ into the first numerator by replacing
$a_{1i}$ by $a_{1i}+cp_{1i}$ and
$b_{1j}$ by $b_{1j}+cq_{1j}$. Thus
\[
 P_{ij}=\sum_{h=1}^H (a_{hi}+b_{hj})
                         \prod_{g\ne h}(p_{gi}+q_{gj}).
\]
For $S\subseteq[H]$, define
\[
 \begin{split}
 r_S(i)&=\prod_{g\in S}p_{gi},&
 u_S(i)&=\sum_{h\in S}a_{hi}\prod_{g\in S\setminus\{h\}}p_{gi},\\
 t_S(j)&=\prod_{g\in S}q_{gj},&
 v_S(j)&=\sum_{h\in S}b_{hj}\prod_{g\in S\setminus\{h\}}q_{gj}.
 \end{split}
\]
The empty products are one and $u_\varnothing=v_\varnothing=0$.
Expansion, grouped according to the slots contributing row factors,
gives the exact identity
\[
 P_{ij}=\sum_{S\subseteq[H]}
       \bigl(u_S(i)t_{S^c}(j)+r_S(i)v_{S^c}(j)\bigr).
\]
For a term whose numerator contributes $a_{hi}$, its row-factor set
contains $h$ and it occurs exactly once in the first sum. For a term
whose numerator contributes $b_{hj}$, the row-factor set does not
contain $h$ and it occurs exactly once in the second sum. This also
checks the identity when a row or column factor is zero.
Each summand is an outer product. The first family has $2^H-1$
possibly nonzero members, and so does the second family. Rank
subadditivity proves the first conclusion.

Also $D_{ij}=\sum_{S\subseteq[H]}r_S(i)t_{S^c}(j)$, so
$\operatorname{rank}D\leq2^H$. Decomposing $R$ into at most $L$
outer products shows directly that the entrywise product $D\circ R$
is a sum of at most $L2^H$ outer products. Adding this contribution
proves the second conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Disjoint support components]
Suppose an affine-ratio grid score has nonzero values on each of $k$
disjoint nonempty rectangles $I_\ell\times J_\ell$, and is exactly zero
on $I_\ell\times J_{\ell'}$ whenever $\ell\ne\ell'$. Here the row sets
$I_\ell$ are pairwise disjoint, and so are the column sets $J_\ell$.
Every representation by $H\geq1$ affine-ratio heads with nonzero grid
denominators satisfies
\[
 k\leq2^{H+1}-2,\qquad H\geq\log_2(k+2)-1.
\]
With a separately permitted rank-$L$ correction, the necessary inequality
is $k\leq(L+2)2^H-2$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose one row from each $I_\ell$ and one column from each $J_\ell$.
The selected score submatrix is diagonal with nonzero diagonal entries.
Entrywise multiplication by its nonzero denominator product preserves
that property, so the resulting $k$ by $k$ matrix has rank $k$.
Apply the theorem. No normalization of its diagonal values is needed.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Application to the proposed paired-order compiler.}
Erd\H{o}s and Moser's Lemma 2 represents a bilevel graph, a vertex-disjoint
union of directed complete bipartite components, by a pair of linear
orders. The two numerical votes cancel on all other pairs.
See Section 2 of
\href{https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1964-22.pdf}{their original paper}.
A module intended to preserve exactly this support must obey the
corollary on a table containing one source row and one target column
from each component. Thus a universal constant-head exact-support
replacement is impossible when the number of components is unbounded.
Allowing unequal magnitudes, unbounded numerator coefficients, either
denominator orientation, or arbitrary cancellation among the heads
does not change this conclusion. Fixed-rank row/column corrections do
not change its logarithmic order either.

This does not rule out summing modules with nonzero off-support tails
that interact, choosing a different decomposition, or compiling all
order pairs jointly. Such a method would need a quantitative argument
controlling those interactions. Exact pairwise cancellation alone
cannot supply the missing saving.

\paragraph{Relation to the earlier approximate mask bound.}
The diagonal-dominance argument in
\texttt{reverse-joint-equality-mask.tex} discards fewer than $4H^2$
indices and proves that a cleared submatrix has full rank. Replacing
its older rank estimate $H2^H$ by the theorem above strengthens its
conclusion to
\[
 k-4H^2\leq2^{H+1}-2.
\]
Under its same hypotheses, $k\geq1024$ then implies
$H\geq\log_2k-2$: if $H\geq\log_2k$ there is nothing to show;
otherwise $4H^2\leq k/2$ and $k/2\leq2^{H+1}-2$.
This remains a restriction on accurate equality masks, not on arbitrary
strict sign computation.

\paragraph{Exact finite checks.}
The companion script checks the subset identity and tests original
strict reverse denominators on the full cube through their all-one
minimum. It obtains cleared ranks $2,6,14$ for $H=1,2,3$ respectively,
attaining the displayed bound in those cases. It also constructs the
paired orders of Lemma 2 on small disjoint block families and verifies
their exact support. These finite computations test the formulas and
show that a rank bound $2^H$ would already be false for $H=2$.
