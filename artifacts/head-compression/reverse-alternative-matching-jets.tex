\paragraph{Status.}
This is a written construction of an explicit restricted family of
joint sign tables. It has not been formalized in Lean. All axes and
admissibility conditions are exact, and the limiting identity is
checked symbolically in the companion Python file. The missing
statement is a sufficiently efficient universal decoder for this
family. A large number of matching states alone does not prove that
statement or either requested global bound.

\paragraph{Slots and states.}
Fix $k\geq1$. Let $E$ and $O$ be the even and odd vertices of
$\{0,1\}^k$. A state is a perfect matching $M:E\to O$ in the cube
graph, so $M(g)$ differs from $g$ in one coordinate. There are
$H=2^{k+1}$ slots, indexed by $(h,a)\in\{0,1\}^k\times\{0,1\}$.
The four slots
\[
                  (g,0),(g,1),(M(g),0),(M(g),1)
                  \tag{1}
\]
form a face for each $g\in E$. Define fixed slot signs
$\gamma_{h,a}=(-1)^{|h|+a}$.

For each $g\in E$, fix $\xi_g>0$ and $R_g>0$. To the four slots
in (1), assign, in the displayed order, pole values
\[
                  \xi_g+\eta R_g(0,1,2,3).
                  \tag{2}
\]
Write the resulting slot pole as $\lambda^M_{h,a}(\eta)$.
The multiset of all slot poles is independent of $M$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact full-cube transfer of strict matching readouts]
Fix $r,s\geq1$ and arbitrary states $M_1,\ldots,M_s$.
For each row $i$, fix an arbitrary function
$f_i:\{0,1\}^k\to\mathbb R$, a real $c_i$, and
$0<\varepsilon_i<1$. Choose $L$ such that all pole values in (2)
lie in $(0,L)$ for every $0<\eta\leq\eta_0$, and choose
$C>r+sL$. Set $t_i=C-\varepsilon_i$ and define
\[
 G_i(M)=\sum_{g\in E}
    \frac{R_g\bigl(f_i(M(g))-f_i(g)+2c_i\bigr)}{(t_i-\xi_g)^2}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Suppose all $G_i(M_j)$ are nonzero. Then, for sufficiently small
positive $\eta$, there is an $H$-head affine-ratio score on the
unchanged $(r+s)$-cube whose grid signs are those of $G_i(M_j)$,
whose two Boolean axes are exactly zero, and whose denominator slopes
are all strictly negative and denominators all strictly positive.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the slot $(h,a)$, use
\[
 \begin{split}
 B_{h,a}(z,w)&=C-\sum_{i=1}^r\varepsilon_i z_i
                   -\sum_{j=1}^s\lambda^{M_j}_{h,a}(\eta)w_j,\\
 A_{h,a}(z,w)&=\eta^{-1}\gamma_{h,a}
                   \sum_{i=1}^r\bigl(f_i(h)+a c_i\bigr)z_i.
 \end{split}
 \tag{4}
\]
Every slope of $B_{h,a}$ is strictly negative. Its minimum on the
whole Boolean cube exceeds $C-r-sL>0$.
On $z=0$, all numerators vanish. On $w=0$, every denominator is
the same, and the numerator sum vanishes because
\[
 \sum_{h,a}\gamma_{h,a}\bigl(f_i(h)+a c_i\bigr)
     =-c_i\sum_h(-1)^{|h|}=0.
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus both axis identities hold for every Boolean point, not merely
on the singleton grid.

At $(z,w)=(e_i,e_j)$ the contribution of a matching edge $g$ is
\[
 \eta^{-1}\left[
 \frac{f_i(g)}{t_i-\xi_g}
 -\frac{f_i(g)+c_i}{t_i-\xi_g-\eta R_g}
 -\frac{f_i(M_j(g))}{t_i-\xi_g-2\eta R_g}
 +\frac{f_i(M_j(g))+c_i}{t_i-\xi_g-3\eta R_g}
 \right].
 \tag{6}
\]
The constant Taylor coefficient of the bracket is zero. Its first
derivative at $\eta=0$ is
\[
 \frac{R_g\bigl[-(f_i(g)+c_i)-2f_i(M_j(g))
                   +3(f_i(M_j(g))+c_i)\bigr]}{(t_i-\xi_g)^2},
\]
which is exactly the $g$ term of (3). Therefore the grid score
converges to $G_i(M_j)$. There are only finitely many grid entries,
and all their target values are nonzero, so a common sufficiently
small positive $\eta$ preserves all strict signs.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The readout class in simpler coordinates.}
The odd values of $f_i$ are arbitrary. The even values only contribute
a matching-independent row term to (3), and can realize any chosen
row bias. Consequently the essential readouts are
\[
 b_i+\sum_{v\in O} a_{i,v}
       \frac{R_{M^{-1}(v)}}{(t_i-\xi_{M^{-1}(v)})^2}.
 \tag{7}
\]
The coefficient vector $(a_{i,v})_v$ is unrestricted. The query
poles $t_i$ can vary in the common interval $(C-1,C)$, above every
support point. Formula (7), rather than just the number of matching
states, is the outstanding decoder problem.

\paragraph{There are enough states in principle.}
For $k\geq2$, the cube graph is $k$-regular and bipartite, with
$H/4$ vertices in each part. Schrijver's proved permanent estimate
gives at least
\[
 \left(\frac{(k-1)^{k-1}}{k^{k-2}}\right)^{H/4}
 =\exp\bigl(\Omega(H\log k)\bigr)
 \tag{8}
\]
perfect matchings. The external source for this counting statement
is A. Schrijver, ``Counting 1-factors in regular bipartite graphs'',
\emph{Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B} 72 (1998), 122--135,
\url{https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/2154/2154D.pdf}.
This standard theorem has not been imported into Lean here.

Each matching is determined by the coordinate of $M(g)-g$ at each
even anchor $g$. Thus $\lceil\log_2 k\rceil$ bits per anchor would
identify a state using $O(H\log k)$ binary queries. Since
$k=\log_2(H/2)$, this is the desired $H\log\log H$ scale.
But no construction showing that (7) can read those bits has been
established.

\paragraph{Two limits of the immediate decoding ideas.}
If all group poles and weights are equal, the matching contribution
telescopes to a matching-independent sum. Distinct groups are
therefore necessary. Taking $a_i$ to be an odd-vertex indicator does
isolate its matched anchor, but gives only a value
$R_g/(t_i-\xi_g)^2$. For a single isolated anchor, the signs of $q$
such biased readouts are determined by $q$ quadratic polynomials in
the two parameters $(\xi_g,R_g)$, hence have only $O(q^2)$ possible
patterns. This does not supply $k$ independently assigned binary
codes in $q=O(\log k)$ queries. General mixed-vertex readouts in (7)
are not covered by that observation and remain under investigation.
