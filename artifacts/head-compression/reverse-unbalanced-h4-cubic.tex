\paragraph{Status.}
This is a written proof with a finite exact rational certificate. The
certificate and every subsequent arithmetic assertion are checked by
\texttt{reverse-unbalanced-h4-cubic-check.py}, using only Python integer
and fraction arithmetic. No numerical feasibility assertion is used in
the proof. This result is not Lean checked. It improves a local constant
and does not establish either asymptotic target.

\begin{theorem}
For every $r\geq1$ and every sign table
$\sigma:[r]\times[10]\to\{-1,1\}$, four affine ratios realize all its
strict grid signs. Every denominator has strictly negative slopes and
is positive on the full Boolean $(r+10)$-cube.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We use the normalized family
\[
 f_j(a,p)=\sum_{h=1}^4\frac{a_h+b_{hj}}{D_{hj}-p_h}.
\]
The first three queries have $b=0$ and
$D=t(1,1,1,1)$, respectively for
$t=1,1001/1000,501/500$. The remaining seven queries are defined by
the following two integer arrays:
\[
 U=\begin{pmatrix}
 3&-5&3\\-2&-3&2\\0&5&1\\-3&-3&4\\-3&-1&5\\4&4&5\\5&0&-5
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 V=\begin{pmatrix}
 -7&-7&-9\\1&0&10\\1&-5&1\\8&-4&6\\2&0&3\\-7&-1&1\\7&-5&-6
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For each row put $u=U_j/1000$, $v=V_j$,
$w=(1+u_1,1+u_2,1+u_3,1)$ and
\[
 \widetilde v=(v_1,v_2,v_3,-\sum_{h=1}^3v_h/(1+u_h)),
 \qquad D_h=1/w_h,\qquad b_h=\widetilde v_h/w_h^2.
\]
Thus $f_j(0,0)=\sum_h\widetilde v_h/w_h=0$.

Write a first-order direction as
\[
 A=(A_1,A_2,A_3,-A_1-A_2-A_3),\qquad
 P=(x_1+z,x_2+z,x_3+z,z).
\]
The first derivatives of the seven extra queries are the linear forms
\[
 Y_j=u\cdot A_{[3]}+v\cdot x+
 z\sum_{h=1}^3\frac{u_hv_h}{1+u_h}.
\]
Define
\[
 Q=A_{[3]}\cdot x=\sum_hA_hP_h,
 \qquad R=\sum_{h=1}^3 A_hx_h^2.
\]
On $Q=0$, the latter also equals $\sum_{h=1}^4A_hP_h^2$.

The finite rational certificate
\texttt{reverse-unbalanced-h4-cubic-certificate.json} contains one
seven-tuple $(A_1,A_2,A_3,x_1,x_2,x_3,z)$ for each of the $128$
prescribed strict signs of $(Y_1,\ldots,Y_7)$, satisfying exactly
$Q=0$ and $R>0$. The checker verifies these equalities and strict
inequalities for every listed tuple. Negating a tuple negates every
$Y_j$ and $R$, and preserves $Q=0$. Consequently every choice of the
seven extra signs and either sign of $R$ has an exact rational witness.
This is the only finite certificate assertion in the proof.

Now prescribe all ten signs. Choose a witness whose extra signs are
the required ones and whose $R$ sign is the required first-query sign.
Construct a polynomial $C(t)$ of degree at most two as follows. Start
with the constant $R$. For each consecutive pair of the three query
times with different prescribed signs, multiply by $1-t/\alpha$, where
$\alpha$ is the midpoint of those two times. Then
\[
 C(t)=R+Mt+Lt^2
\]
has the prescribed signs at all three times. Each root is strictly
between its neighboring query times, so every claimed sign is strict.
Choose a coordinate $h$ with $A_h\ne0$, put $P^2_h=M/A_h$ and all
other coordinates of $P^2$ equal to zero, and put
$A^3=(L/4,L/4,L/4,L/4)$. Take
\[
 a=\delta A+\delta^3 A^3,\qquad
 p=\delta P+\delta^2 P^2.
\]
At a base query with denominator $t$, the expansion is
\[
 \sum_h\frac{a_h}{t-p_h}
 =\frac{\delta}{t}\sum_hA_h
 +\frac{\delta^2}{t^2}\sum_hA_hP_h
 +\frac{\delta^3}{t^3}
 \left(t^2\sum_h A^3_h+t\sum_h A_hP^2_h+
       \sum_h A_hP_h^2\right)+O(\delta^4)
 =\frac{\delta^3}{t^3}C(t)+O(\delta^4).
\]
Every extra query equals $\delta Y_j+O(\delta^2)$. Thus all ten
desired signs hold for sufficiently small positive $\delta$.
All data are rational and there are only $1024$ patterns, so one
positive rational $\delta$ works simultaneously. Clearing the positive
denominators reduces this assertion to finitely many polynomials with
proved nonzero leading coefficients. The checker additionally verifies
all $10240$ strict scores at $\delta=2^{-80}$.

For the full cube, all normalized query denominators lie in
$[200/201,200/199]$. Put $d_0=99/100$, $\rho=1/(10000r)$ and shrink
the common $\delta$ further until $|d_0p_{hi}|<\rho/2$ for all selected
row patterns. Define
\[
 \widehat p_{hi}=\rho+d_0p_{hi},\qquad
 \widehat q_{hj}=1-\rho-d_0D_{hj},
\]
\[
 B_h(z,w)=1-\sum_i\widehat p_{hi}z_i-\sum_j\widehat q_{hj}w_j,
 \qquad
 A_h(z,w)=d_0\left(\sum_i a_{hi}z_i+\sum_jb_{hj}w_j\right).
\]
At $(e_i,e_j)$ these ratios equal the normalized scores exactly.
The slope drops satisfy
\[
 \rho/2<\widehat p_{hi}<3\rho/2,
 \qquad 0<1/199-\rho\leq\widehat q_{hj}<1/67.
\]
Their total is less than $3/20000+10/67<1$. Every slope is strictly
negative, and the all-one vertex, hence the entire Boolean cube, has
positive denominator. This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Mechanism and limitation.}
The two preceding low-order cancellations use the nonlinear cone
$Q=A\cdot x=0$, not a fixed linear subspace of directions. A maximal
isotropic linear slice loses enough variables to erase this cubic gain.
This example shows that such a loss cannot be assumed when auditing
higher-order constructions. It does not show how to share the increasing
number of moment constraints across an unbounded number of base-query
groups. A single group of $D$ proportional queries consumes a degree
$D-1$ polynomial; this alone gives only a constant ratio when $D$ is at
most the number of heads.

The theorem has no initial axis-zero property. Applying the existing
checked reverse-grid axis repair costs $3H+3=15$ heads for this $H=4$
input, before any unrelated gluing. No asymptotic conclusion is asserted.
