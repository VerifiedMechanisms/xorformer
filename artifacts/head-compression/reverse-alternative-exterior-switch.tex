\paragraph{Status and scope.}
This note gives a complete written proof, with an exact symbolic checker,
that varying exterior row poles can change affine orientations of permutation
states even arbitrarily far from every token pole. The paired Cauchy
realization is separately checked in Lean in
\texttt{HeadComplexity/Results/ReverseBalancedRows.lean}.
The orientation calculation in this note is not formalized in Lean.
This does not prove superlinear shattering, a sublinear reverse compiler,
or either requested global asymptotic bound. In particular, independent
copies of the four-token example give only a constant gain per head.

\begin{theorem}[Exterior orientation switch]
Fix $T\geq10$, and define
\[
 A=\frac{5T^2-12T+3}{T^2-5},\qquad
 \tau=\frac{7T-15}{3T-7},\qquad
 (\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3,\xi_4)=(1,2,3,A).
\]
Then $3<A<5$ and $\tau<3$. Use the four permutations, written as
ordered lists of token indices,
\[
 \pi_1=(1,2,3,4),\quad \pi_2=(1,3,2,4),\quad
 \pi_3=(2,1,4,3),\quad \pi_4=(3,2,4,1).
\]
For $t>A$ let $X_k(t)$ be the affine point in $\mathbb R^3$ whose
coordinates are $1/(t-\xi_{\pi_k(h)})$, $h=1,2,3$.
The determinant of the four rows $(1,X_k(t))$ is strictly positive
for $A<t<T$, zero at $t=T$, and strictly negative for $t>T$.
In particular this switch occurs at arbitrarily large $t/\max_h\xi_h$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Direct determinant expansion gives
\[
 \Delta(t)=
 -\frac{(A-5)t^2+12t-5A-3}
 {(A-t)(t-3)^3(t-2)^2(t-1)^2}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The exact numerator factorization after the stated substitution for $A$ is
\[
 (A-5)t^2+12t-5A-3=(A-5)(t-T)(t-\tau).
 \tag{2}
\]
Here
\[
 A-3=\frac{2(T-3)^2}{T^2-5}>0,\qquad
 5-A=\frac{4(3T-7)}{T^2-5}>0,\qquad
 3-\tau=\frac{2(T-3)}{3T-7}>0.
\]
All denominator factors in (1) except $A-t$ are positive when $t>A$.
Equations (1) and (2) therefore give the three asserted signs.
Since $A<5$ whereas $T$ is unbounded, the ratio assertion follows.
\end{proof}

The fourth reciprocal coordinate is unnecessary in the determinant:
its sum with the first three is the same for all permutation states.
Thus these are affine coordinates for the four-dimensional common-pole
readout vectors on their common sum hyperplane. The theorem concerns
orientation of labelled point configurations. It does not assert that
all possible affine cuts of the entire permutation configuration differ
at the two poles, and it does not by itself shatter an extra query.

\begin{proposition}[Compatibility with arbitrary Boolean cube margins]
Fix integers $r\geq2$ and $s\geq1$. The two query poles $T-1,T+1$
from the theorem can occur among $r$ row poles in an exact paired
Cauchy module with $2G=8$ heads, strictly negative slopes in every
coordinate, positive denominators on the whole $(r+s)$-cube, and both
Boolean axis identities. One may take $T$ arbitrarily large.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $T\geq10$ with $T+2>3r+5s$, set $C=T+2$, and choose all
row poles $t_i$ in $[T-1,T+1]$, including both endpoints. Set
$\epsilon_i=C-t_i$. Then $1\leq\epsilon_i\leq3$ and
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^r\epsilon_i+s\max_h\xi_h
       \leq3r+5s<C.
 \tag{3}
\]
For any column states $\pi_j:[4]\to[4]$, arbitrary row coefficients
$a_{ih}$, and arbitrary row biases $b_i$, the checked theorem
\texttt{exists\_permutationCauchy\_reverseAxisData} constructs the
exact grid values
\[
 b_i+\sum_{h=1}^4\frac{a_{ih}}{t_i-\xi_{\pi_j(h)}}
\]
with eight heads and both axes zero. Its assumptions are precisely
positive $\epsilon_i$, positive tokens bounded by $5$, two distinct
tokens, and (3). The construction has denominators
$C-\sum_i\epsilon_i z_i-\sum_j u_{hj}w_j$ with
$u_{hj}\in\{\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3,\xi_4\}$, which proves strict
slopes and whole-cube positivity directly as well.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Remaining amplification question.}
A possible superconstant gain would need overlapping switches or another
mechanism that decodes many independently chosen branch bits with a
sublinear number of token slots. The determinant sign change proves
that the exterior regime does not force a constant orientation in $t$.
It supplies no such amplification. The general reverse compiler and
the global alternatives remain unresolved by this note.
