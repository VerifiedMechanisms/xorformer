\paragraph{Audit of the written geometric transfer.}
The descending order of total center size is essential and is used
correctly. At an earlier protected vertex inside a new center's downset,
the two deletion counts sum to at most two. Either one count is zero,
where the exact axis identity applies, or both are one and the vertex
is in the new cell. The new contribution thus has nonnegative signed
value there. Outside the downset, fixed numerators divided by a growing
positive denominator penalty converge to zero. The finite strict margin
then preserves all previously established signs. Bias is absorbed into
a numerator before applying the penalty. Weak slopes are perturbed only
after the complete finite construction, when intermediate axis zeros
are no longer needed. Complementation sends an everywhere-positive
negative-slope local denominator to a positive constant coefficient and
nonnegative ambient slopes. These steps do not change the head count.
The concrete ordered-center instantiation and final asymptotic
combination remain written proofs, rather than additional Lean theorems.

\paragraph{Five-row theorem and its exact limit.}
The inverse function theorem argument is valid as written. Two distinct
regular preimages of four zero queries permit four independent small
sign perturbations. A fifth query with opposite strict signs at those
preimages adds the fifth bit. The chosen parameter neighborhoods keep
all column denominator coefficients between $\varepsilon/2$ and
$5\varepsilon/2$. The displayed estimate
\[
 1-37\varepsilon-\frac52s\varepsilon>0
\]
proves denominator positivity on the whole $(5+s)$-cube, not just on
the grid. Thus two heads do universally represent every five-row grid
table. This statement is a complete written theorem supported by exact
identities, but has not been formalized in Lean.

Axis subtraction requires solving the row coefficients again. The
source run does this explicitly and obtains six ratios, four having
zero slopes on one ignored block. The finite exceptional set in
$\varepsilon$ is justified by nonzero rational determinants. This
six-ratio exact-axis construction does not beat the linear compiler
at five rows. Repetition of the two-head gadget, even if an appropriate
isolation mechanism were supplied, gives only a fixed row-to-head ratio.
No superconstant rate or global asymptotic improvement follows from it.

\paragraph{Fresh formulation of the amplification gap.}
The two-head construction suggests studying the class of compact-domain
column decoders
\[
 F_i(a,u)=\sum_{h=1}^H\frac{A_{ih}+a_h}{d_{ih}-u_h},
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,r.
\]
The row data are fixed independently of labels, whereas the $2H$ column
parameters vary jointly. A universal strict-sign shattering construction
with $r/H\to\infty$, together with the original whole-cube admissibility
and a constant-factor exact-axis construction, would materially improve
the local compiler. Neither a counting upper bound nor the existence of
many regular sheets proves this: one must give fixed queries that read
all the required independent signs. The five-row result supplies no
recurrence increasing $r/H$. This formulation is recorded as an explicit
open construction problem, not a claimed theorem or completion.
