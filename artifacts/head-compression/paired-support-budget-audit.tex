\paragraph{Scope and verification status.}
This is a complete written counting argument for a restricted proposed
upper-bound construction. It shows that reducing the number of components
in each Erd\H{o}s--Moser bilevel module cannot restore a saving when every
module is compiled separately with exact support, or with the specified
uniformly small error. It is not a lower bound for the unchanged
HeadComplexity model and does not resolve either accepted target.
No result here is Lean formalized and no model definition is changed.

Fix a bipartite table with $r$ rows, $s$ columns and $m=r+s$ vertices.
A signed bilevel support is the restriction of a directed vertex-disjoint
union of complete bipartite graphs to this row-column table. It is encoded
by assigning each vertex either to the isolated class or to the top or
bottom side of one of $k$ components. The direction between top and bottom
specifies the sign on a table entry. Components with no nonzero table
entry may be deleted. We consider decompositions into edge-disjoint such
supports whose signs agree with the desired table; in particular their
union covers every table entry. This is the support-preserving assembly
used by paired orders. Arbitrary real amplitudes inside a component do
not change its signed support.

\begin{theorem}[Small-component tradeoff]
At most $(2k+1)^{mT}$ signed tables can be encoded by a sequence of $T$
bilevel supports having at most $k$ components each. Consequently any such
scheme covering all $2^{rs}$ sign tables requires
\[
 T\geq \frac{rs}{m\log_2(2k+1)}.
\]
In particular $k=O(\log m)$ requires $T=\Omega(m/\log\log m)$ on
balanced tables, rather than $O(m/\log m)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The vertex assignment has $2k+1$ choices at each vertex. Every encoded
support is specified by one of these assignments, with overcounting
allowed. A sequence of $T$ assignments therefore has at most the stated
number of possibilities. Restricting to edge-disjoint sign-consistent
covers only decreases this number. Every valid sequence determines its
final sign table uniquely. Compare with the $2^{rs}$ possible targets.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nonuniform exact-support budget]
Suppose each nonempty support is implemented by a sum of $H_i\geq1$
affine-ratio heads, with denominators nonzero on the grid, having exactly
zero values outside its support and the specified signs on that support.
The number of sign tables obtainable by the above support-preserving
assemblies with total head budget at most $L\geq1$ is at most
\[
 2^{(3m+1)L}.
\]
Thus some balanced tables require $\Omega(m)$ total heads in this
restricted assembly, even when the numbers of components and heads vary
arbitrarily among modules.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exact cleared separation-rank theorem in
\texttt{reverse-joint-exact-support-rank.tex} implies
\[
 k_i\leq 2^{H_i+1}-2.
\]
Indeed choose one supported row-column pair from each component. Cross
pairs between different components have score zero, so the selected
cleared submatrix is diagonal with $k_i$ nonzero diagonal entries.
This argument permits unequal magnitudes and arbitrary signs.
For a fixed $H_i$, all its possible signed supports are consequently
covered by at most
\[
 (2(2^{H_i+1}-2)+1)^m
 \leq 2^{m(H_i+2)}\leq2^{3mH_i}
\]
vertex assignments. For a fixed ordered composition
$H_1+\cdots+H_T=L'\leq L$, the number of support sequences is at most
$2^{3mL'}\leq2^{3mL}$.
There are $2^{L'-1}$ ordered positive compositions of $L'$, and
$\sum_{L'=1}^L2^{L'-1}<2^L$. Summing the bounds proves the assertion.
Whenever $(3m+1)L<rs$, this number is smaller than $2^{rs}$, so some
target table is absent. For $r=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ and
$s=\lceil m/2\rceil$, this gives the stated linear order.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A uniformly accurate support replacement also loses]
Suppose instead that module $i$ approximates its signed unit support
entrywise within $\epsilon_i$, where
\[
 \epsilon_i<\frac1{2k_i},\qquad
 \sum_i\epsilon_i<1.
\]
Assume its denominators are positive on the entire Boolean cube with
weakly nonpositive slopes. The number of sign tables represented by these
support-preserving assemblies with at most $L$ total heads is at most
$2^{(5m+1)L}$. Consequently some balanced tables again require
$\Omega(m)$ total heads within this restricted scheme.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The approximation hypothesis and the strengthened rank calculation in
\texttt{reverse-joint-equality-mask.tex} and
\texttt{reverse-joint-exact-support-rank.tex} give
\[
 k_i\leq4H_i^2+2^{H_i+1}-2.
\]
For $H\geq1$, induction gives $H^2\leq4^{H-1}$, and also
$2^{H+1}\leq2\cdot4^H$. Hence
$4H^2+2^{H+1}-2\leq3\cdot4^H-2<2^{2H+2}-1$.
Hence $2k_i+1<2^{2H_i+3}$, and the count is at most
$2^{m(2H_i+3)}\leq2^{5mH_i}$. Sum over positive head-budget
compositions as before.
Finally, the ideal support sum is exactly the desired $\pm1$ table,
and the total entrywise error is less than one. Its signs therefore
agree with that table. This justifies counting signed supports rather
than unconstrained numerical scores.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Audit of the primary construction.}
In Section 2, Lemmas 3 and 4 of
\href{https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1964-22.pdf}{Erd\H{o}s and Moser's
1964 paper}, the dense phase builds each bilevel graph from components
with one side of order $\sqrt m$ and the other of order
$\log m/(a+1)$, where $a$ indexes the density phase. The proof greedily
packs a number of these components of order $\sqrt m/2^a$.
At bounded $a$ the component count is therefore polynomial in $m$,
not logarithmic. Section 3 uses the resulting large supports to obtain
$O(m/\log m)$ order pairs. The original paper does not provide the
combination of logarithmically many components and $O(m/\log m)$
modules assumed in the small-component proposal; the first theorem
above independently excludes that combination for all sign tables.

\paragraph{What is still open.}
The count does not apply if off-support tails interact to create the
final signs, if overlapping supports are combined with target-dependent
cancellation, or if multiple modules are compiled jointly into heads
whose individual supports no longer follow the decomposition. It also
does not apply to every arbitrary affine-ratio sign representation.
Such a genuinely joint construction remains a possible route to the
requested improvement.
