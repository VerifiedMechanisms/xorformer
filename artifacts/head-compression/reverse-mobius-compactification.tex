\paragraph{Status.}
The identities and geometric budget bounds below are complete elementary
written proofs. The exact transport identity, finite-table extension
criterion, and complete conditional atom assembly are now Lean checked
in \texttt{Results/ReverseMobius.lean}; the optimized box criterion is
not Lean checked. They do not provide a
superconstant saving, nor do they by themselves supply axis-zero
extensions. The distinction between a bound for a whole parameter box
and the actual sum of a finite table's slopes is essential.

\begin{theorem}[Exact change of parameters]
Let $g(t)=\gamma t+\delta$, $\Delta=\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma\ne0$,
and $\phi(t)=(\alpha t+\beta)/g(t)$. Assume $d\ne p$ and
$g(d)g(p)\ne0$. Set
\[
 p'=\phi(p),\quad d'=\phi(d),\quad
 a'=\frac{\Delta a}{g(p)^2},\quad
 b'=\frac{\Delta b}{g(d)^2}.
\]
Then
\[
 \frac{a'+b'}{d'-p'}
 =\frac{a+b}{d-p}+\frac{\gamma a}{g(p)}
                  -\frac{\gamma b}{g(d)}.                 \tag{1}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The determinant identity gives
$d'-p'=\Delta(d-p)/(g(d)g(p))$. The transformed $a$ term is
$a g(d)/(g(p)(d-p))=a/(d-p)+\gamma a/g(p)$.
The transformed $b$ term is
$b g(p)/(g(d)(d-p))=b/(d-p)-\gamma b/g(d)$.
Adding proves (1).
\end{proof}
For a grid score $F_{ij}=\sum_h(a_{hi}+b_{hj})/(d_{hj}-p_{hi})$,
each head may use its own transformation. All discrepancies in (1)
add to one row function plus one column function. Consequently one
additive affine grid correction restores the original scores exactly.
This assertion is about grid values. A constant denominator realizes
the correction with weak slopes. The checked weight-two correction
restores strictly negative slopes without changing any grid value.
The existing axis repair then gives $3H+6$ strictly admissible axis-zero
heads, again preserving every grid value exactly. The theorem
\texttt{exists\_mobius\_reverse\_axis} checks this entire assembly,
conditional on each transformed head satisfying its finite sum budget.

\paragraph{A small exact check.}
For $\phi(t)=t/(2-t)$, $\Delta=2$, $p=1/4$, $d=3/4$, $a=2$, and
$b=-1$, the original score is $2$. The transformed parameters are
$p'=1/7$, $d'=3/5$, $a'=64/49$, and $b'=-32/25$. Their fraction is
$2/35$. The row and column discrepancy in (1) is
$-8/7-4/5=-68/35$, as required. This example checks both the sign of
the column correction and the square in each numerator rescaling.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform box compactification]
Suppose all row parameters belong to $[p_-,p_+]$ and all column
parameters belong to $[d_-,d_+]$, with
$p_-<p_+<d_-<d_+$. Define the invariant
\[
 \chi=\frac{(p_+-p_-)(d_+-d_-)}{(d_--p_+)(d_+-p_-)}>0.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
An increasing M\"obius map, finite on the whole interval, can send
$p_-$ to $0$, $d_+$ to $1$, $p_+$ to $P$, and $d_-$ to $1-Q$
if and only if
\[
 0<P,\quad0<Q,\quad P+Q<1,\qquad
 PQ=\chi(1-P-Q).                                             \tag{3}
\]
For $r,s\geq2$, the existence of such a map with the uniform
full-cube budget $rP+sQ<1$ is equivalent to
\[
 \chi<\frac{1}
 {(\sqrt{r(s-1)}+\sqrt{s(r-1)})^2}.                           \tag{4}
\]
In particular, $\chi<1/(8rs)$ is sufficient, and $\chi<1/(rs)$
is necessary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Differences under a M\"obius map acquire one determinant factor and
one factor $g(x)g(y)$ in their denominators. These factors cancel
from (2), proving invariance. In the normalized coordinates (2)
becomes $PQ/(1-P-Q)$, proving necessity of (3).

For sufficiency put $u=p_+-p_-$ and $L=d_+-p_-$. Given any
$P\in(0,1)$, define
\[
 C=\frac{PL-u}{Lu(1-P)},\qquad
 \phi(t)=\frac{(CL+1)(t-p_-)}{L(C(t-p_-)+1)}.
\]
Since $CL+1=P(L-u)/(u(1-P))>0$, this map is finite and strictly
increasing throughout $[p_-,d_+]$. It has the three specified
endpoint values, and invariance fixes the fourth. Equivalently,
$Q=\chi(1-P)/(P+\chi)$.

For completeness, maximize $PQ/(1-P-Q)$ over the line
$rP+sQ=1$ in the positive quadrant. Substitute
$Q=(1-rP)/s$. The resulting function is
\[
 \frac{P(1-rP)}{s-1+(r-s)P},\qquad 0<P<1/r.
\]
It tends to zero at both endpoints. Differentiation shows its
unique interior maximum is the right side of (4). One can verify
the value without solving for $P$: the equation
$PQ=\chi(1-P-Q)$ and $rP+sQ=1$ has a double positive root exactly
when
\[
 (r-s)^2\chi^2-2(2rs-r-s)\chi+1=0.
\]
Its smaller root is
$[2rs-r-s+2\sqrt{rs(r-1)(s-1)}]^{-1}$, which is (4).
The graph (3) lies strictly below some point of the budget line
exactly when $\chi$ is smaller than that maximum. This proves (4).

For the simpler sufficient bound choose $P=1/(2r)$. Then
$Q=\chi(1-P)/(P+\chi)<2r\chi<1/(4s)$, so the budget is less than
$3/4$. Conversely, $rP+sQ<1$ implies $PQ<1/(4rs)$ by AM--GM and
$P+Q<1/2$ because $r,s\geq2$. Thus
$\chi=PQ/(1-P-Q)<1/(2rs)<1/(rs)$, which proves the stated weaker
necessary bound as well.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Relation to original denominators.}
For the normalized parameters define
\[
 B(z,w)=1-\sum_{i=1}^r p'_i z_i
          -\sum_{j=1}^s(1-d'_j)w_j.
\]
Its value at $(e_i,e_j)$ is $d'_j-p'_i$. The box budget ensures
positivity on the full cube. A strict budget leaves room to replace
zero slopes by strictly negative slopes while preserving all grid
values, by the checked weight-two correction. The actual finite-table budget is
$\sum_i p'_i+\sum_j(1-d'_j)<1$; it can be much smaller than the
uniform box bound $rP+sQ<1$. Thus failure of (4) is not a barrier to
all table-specific compactifications. Conversely, compactifying a
fixed finite inventory once does not authorize arbitrarily many
copies of its largest row slope. A growing-table compiler still
requires quantitative control as $r,s$ grow.

\paragraph{Exact extension criterion and verification.}
Without endpoint normalization, let $\ell=\min_i p_i$ and
$u=\max_j d_j$. A reverse affine extension with precisely the grid
denominators $d_j-p_i$ exists if and only if
\[
 u-\ell-\sum_i(p_i-\ell)-\sum_j(u-d_j)>0.
\]
Sufficiency uses the natural coefficients $\ell-p_i$ and $d_j-u$.
For necessity, every other extension's row coefficients differ by a
common nonpositive offset from the natural row coefficients, and the
column coefficients do likewise. At the all-ones vertex this changes
the natural slack by $(r-1)$ times the row offset plus $(s-1)$ times
the column offset, which is nonpositive. This argument is checked by
\texttt{separated\_reverse\_extension\_iff}. The exact identity is
\texttt{mobius\_separated\_ratio}. None of these declarations assumes
or establishes a universal sublinear compiler.
