\section{Target and status}

The proposed larger local domain is a full $k$-bit cube times $s$ one-hot
columns. The sufficient decoder would have directions
$d_h\in\mathbb R_{>0}^k$ fixed for all columns and all labelings, and for
every Boolean table would choose column-dependent $a_{hj},b_{hj},\tau_j$
so that
\[
 F_j(x)=\tau_j+\sum_{h=1}^H\frac{a_{hj}}{b_{hj}+d_h\cdot x}
\]
has the prescribed strict signs on the entire row cube. For the reverse
column extension, the offsets must lie in a common interval of relative
width $O(1/s)$. Directions may depend on $k,s$, but cannot depend on an
individual column's labels.

A bound $H(k)=O(2^k/\sqrt{k})$ with this compact-offset property would be
sufficient for the requested exponent one half under the one-star
transfer: choose $k$ proportional to the ambient dimension. The stronger
$H(k)=O(2^k/k)$ would give the corresponding quadratic denominator. Neither
large-dimensional decoder is established here.

The proposition below is a written mathematical proof, not a Lean
formalization. Its fixed rational instance is independently checked by
exact rational arithmetic in the accompanying finite certificates. The
computational scripts do not change the original model or assert a
universal asymptotic theorem.

\section{Every two-bit table with two reciprocal terms}

\begin{proposition}
For every $\eta>0$, there are two fixed strictly positive directions
$d_1,d_2\in\mathbb R_{>0}^2$ such that every map
$f:\{0,1\}^2\longrightarrow\{-1,1\}$ has a strict sign representation
\[
 f(x)\left(\tau+\frac{a_1}{b_1+d_1\cdot x}
                   +\frac{a_2}{b_2+d_2\cdot x}\right)>0
\]
with both offsets in $(1,1+\eta)$. Only two offset pairs are needed,
independently of $f$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $B=1+\eta/3$, $\delta=\eta/3$, and choose $\epsilon>0$ so small that
\[
 \epsilon\bigl(B^2+B+\delta(B-1)\bigr)<2\delta.
\]
Take $d_1=(1,1)$ and $d_2=(1,1+\epsilon)$. Use either $(b,c)=(B,B)$ or
$(b,c)=(B+\delta,B)$ as the two offsets. All denominators are positive
on the cube, and both offset choices lie in the required interval.

Order the four inputs as $00,01,10,11$ and form the four by three
feature matrix with rows
\[
 v_{xy}=\left(1,\frac1{b+x+y},
                  \frac1{c+x+(1+\epsilon)y}\right).
\]
Let $C_i=(-1)^i\det V_{\widehat i}$ be its signed maximal minors, starting
with $i=0$. Direct expansion gives
\[
 C_0=\frac{\epsilon}{(b+1)(b+2)(c+1)(c+\epsilon+1)},\qquad
 C_3=\frac{\epsilon}{b(b+1)(c+1)(c+\epsilon+1)},
\]
\[
 C_1=-\frac{bc\epsilon-b\epsilon-2b+2c\epsilon+2c}
 {bc(b+1)(b+2)(c+1)(c+\epsilon+2)},
\]
\[
 C_2=-\frac{bc\epsilon+b\epsilon^2+3b\epsilon+2b-2c}
 {bc(b+1)(b+2)(c+\epsilon+1)(c+\epsilon+2)}.
\]
All denominators displayed here are positive. Since $C_0>0$, the matrix
has rank three, and its column space is precisely
$\{z\in\mathbb R^4:C\cdot z=0\}$. A strict orthant with signs $f_i$
intersects this hyperplane if and only if the four nonzero numbers
$C_i f_i$ do not all have the same sign. For sufficiency, choose positive
magnitudes for the coordinates so that the positive and negative terms
in $C\cdot z$ have equal total magnitude. Necessity is immediate.

At $(b,c)=(B,B)$, the two middle numerators are respectively
$B(B+1)\epsilon$ and $B\epsilon(B+\epsilon+3)$, so
\[
 \operatorname{sgn} C=(+,-,-,+).
\]
At $(b,c)=(B+\delta,B)$, the numerator of $-C_1$ is
$\epsilon(B^2+B+\delta(B-1))-2\delta<0$, whereas the numerator of
$-C_2$ is positive. Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{sgn} C=(+,+,-,+).
\]
Each feature matrix misses only its own two opposite orthants, and these
two excluded pairs are distinct. Their union consequently meets every
strict orthant of $\mathbb R^4$. Membership in a feature column space
supplies the required $(\tau,a_1,a_2)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite computations and their limits}

\texttt{LargeDomainFixedDirections.py} clears the positive denominators
and asks Z3 for real solutions. Rational solutions are then re-evaluated
independently with Python's exact rational arithmetic. For
$d_1=(1,1)$, $d_2=(1,1001/1000)$, and offsets in $(1,9/8)$, all eight
labelings up to complementation passed this rational recheck. Their
witnesses are in \texttt{LargeDomainTwoBitCertificates.jsonl}. Negating
the bias and residues realizes the complementary table.

\texttt{LargeDomainFixedDirectionCoverage.py} samples offsets while
holding a family of positive directions fixed. It enumerates candidate
chambers of the feature arrangement by maximal intersections, using
floating-point singular value decompositions. Reported patterns are
re-evaluated at explicit floating-point coefficients, but are not exact
certificates. In one fixed near-uniform direction family, the observed
numbers of sign complement classes were:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|r|r|r}
 k&H&\text{offset samples}&\text{observed classes}&\text{all classes}\\
 3&3&1000&105&128\\
 3&4&1000&125&128\\
 4&4&100&5522&32768\\
 4&5&200&10619&32768
\end{array}
\]
These missing sampled patterns are not impossibility results. Near
singular offset regimes may be missed by random sampling, and large
condition numbers limit floating-point evidence. Separate exact-real
solver timeouts also establish no lower bound.

The next algebraic route starts at equal row directions. With $H\geq
k+1$, a nonzero reciprocal combination can have numerator divisible by
$\prod_{r=0}^k(w-r)$, where $w=\sum_i x_i$, and hence vanish on the whole
cube. Perturbing the directions then produces polynomial jets on each
Hamming layer. The first jet is affine on each layer and cannot represent
arbitrary central-layer labels. The unresolved step is independent
sign control across layers using higher jets and the nonlinear offset
parameters, at $H=O(2^k/\sqrt{k})$ or better.
