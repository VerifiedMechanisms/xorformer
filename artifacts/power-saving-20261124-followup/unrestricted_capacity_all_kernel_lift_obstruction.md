# Constant overhead fails for all-kernel moment lifting

## Status and scope

This is a written proof, not a Lean theorem. It uses the written Cayley–Bacharach curve-cover corollary in [the base-locus note](cayley_bacharach_base_locus.md#a-bounded-degree-curve-cover-at-linear-length). The arithmetic behind that note's Hilbert-window estimate is kernel checked, but its geometric inputs are not.

The conclusion concerns preservation of every projected signed measure, equivalently preservation against every scalar kernel. It does **not** rule out preserving a fixed reciprocal score, preserving only a truth table, or a dichotomy allowing configurations with few distinct observations. Indeed the examples have only four Boolean queries and are already a class with low capacity. A fixed-score interpolation construction below shows why this distinction is essential.

## Statement

For every fixed real $C\geq1$, there exist an integer $D$, an integer $H=3D+1$, strictly positive direction vectors $w_1,\ldots,w_H\in\mathbb R^3$, and nonzero real residues $c_1,\ldots,c_H$ with the following properties. Write

$$ \ell_0(u,v,w)=u,\qquad \ell_x(u,v,w)=u+v,\qquad \ell_y(u,v,w)=u+w,\qquad \ell_s(u,v,w)=u+v+w. $$

Every Boolean projection moment through order $D$ vanishes:

$$ \sum_{i=1}^H c_i\ell_j(w_i)^k=0\qquad(j\in\lbrace0,x,y,s\rbrace,\ 0\leq k\leq D). $$

There is no signed measure $\nu$ on at most $CH$ points of $\mathbb R^3$ which simultaneously satisfies both conditions:

1. For all four projections, the pushforward signed measure equals that of $\mu=\sum_i c_i\delta_{w_i}$.

2. The measure annihilates every ordinary polynomial in three variables of total degree at most $D$.

The auxiliary support is allowed arbitrary real intercepts and slopes, arbitrary signed residues, and data-dependent locations. Thus the nonexistence also holds under the stricter requirement that every auxiliary direction be strictly positive.

## Generic Boolean moment circuits

First work before translation, with points $p_i=(0,a_i,b_i)$. Choose the $2H$ real numbers $a_i,b_i$ algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$, all in a bounded positive interval. For $D\geq1$, the polynomial space

$$ E_D=\mathrm{span}\lbrace1,a,b,a^k,b^k,(a+b)^k:2\leq k\leq D\rbrace $$

has dimension $3D$. Distinct homogeneous degrees are independent. In every degree at least two, the three indicated homogeneous polynomials are independent, because $(a+b)^k$ has a mixed term.

The evaluation matrix of a basis of $E_D$ at $H=3D+1$ such points has every maximal minor nonzero. For each choice of $3D$ point positions, the determinant is a nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients: linearly independent polynomial functions admit a tuple with invertible evaluation matrix, by successively extending the span of evaluation functionals. Algebraic independence avoids its zero set.

Consequently its kernel is one-dimensional and contains a vector $c$ with every coordinate nonzero. This vector annihilates the three projected moments through degree $D$. It also satisfies $\sum_i c_i=0$, so the fourth projection, constantly zero, has zero pushforward measure. All atoms within each of the three nonconstant original projected measures are distinct.

Translate every point by the same rational vector $(1,1,1)$. The points become strictly positive. Ordinary and projected moment annihilation through degree $D$ are translation invariant, by the binomial theorem. Equality of projected signed measures is also preserved by translation. A proposed auxiliary lift can therefore be translated back, and it suffices to prove nonexistence for the points $p_i$.

## Curve support forces a transcendence bound

We prove a general obstruction. Suppose an auxiliary signed measure has the same four projected measures as the generic cloud above and is supported on an algebraic curve in $\mathbb C^3$. Suppose this curve has $r$ irreducible components and is defined over a field $K$ of transcendence degree at most $\kappa$ over $\mathbb Q$. Components may be defined over the algebraic closure of $K$. Then

$$ H\leq\kappa+8r. $$

Repeated auxiliary support points are combined and points with zero final residue are discarded.

### Lemma 1. Nonconstant projection fibers

For each of the four projections, record its constant value on every curve component on which it is constant. Let $B$ be the set of real values among these bad values, and put $b=|B|\leq4r$. Every value in $B$ is algebraic over $K$.

Make a graph on the auxiliary support by connecting points that share any projection value outside $B$. Every connected component of this graph contributes at most one transcendence parameter over $K$. In fact, a nonbad projection has finite fibers on the whole curve. Starting from any point of a graph component, a neighboring point is algebraic over the field generated by that point and $K$. Induct along a path. The first point itself lies on a curve, so its coordinates have transcendence degree at most one over $K$.

### Lemma 2. An additive incidence constraint

For each original index $i$, assign its three values $a_i,b_i,a_i+b_i$ to graph components $C_x(i),C_y(i),C_s(i)$. A bad value is assigned to one additional dummy vertex $\bot$. A nonbad value has nonzero total projected residue $c_i$, and all auxiliary points with that value lie in a single graph component, so its assignment exists and is unique.

Construct an incidence graph on these vertices, with two directed edges for each index:

$$ C_s(i)\longrightarrow C_x(i),\qquad C_s(i)\longrightarrow C_y(i). $$

Loops are permitted. Its real incidence matrix has rank at most $b$.

**Proof.** Let $V$ be the finite-dimensional rational vector space spanned by every real projection value occurring in the two measures together with all values in $B$. Choose a rational basis and write $v(t)$ for the rational coordinate vector of $t\in V$. The map $v$ is additive. We use coordinate vectors because an ordinary scalar first-moment equation would not justify the rank bound.

The $2H$ vectors $v(a_i),v(b_i)$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb R$. They are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$ by algebraic independence, and their coordinate matrix is rational, so extending the scalar field preserves rank.

At each auxiliary point $q$, additivity gives

$$ v(\ell_x(q))+v(\ell_y(q))-v(\ell_s(q))-v(\ell_0(q))=0. $$

Multiply by the residue of $q$ and sum over one auxiliary graph component $A$. For each nonbad fiber, use equality of projected measures to replace its total residue by the corresponding original residue. The original $\ell_0$ measure is zero. All remaining terms are real linear combinations of the vectors $v(t)$ for $t\in B$. Therefore

$$ \sum_i c_i\bigl(\mathbf1_{C_x(i)=A}-\mathbf1_{C_s(i)=A}\bigr)v(a_i)+\sum_i c_i\bigl(\mathbf1_{C_y(i)=A}-\mathbf1_{C_s(i)=A}\bigr)v(b_i)\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb R}\lbrace v(t):t\in B\rbrace. $$

Because the vectors $v(a_i),v(b_i)$ are independent, the space of these coefficient rows has dimension at most $b$. Add the dummy row as the negative sum of all other rows; this does not increase rank. The resulting matrix is the incidence matrix with each of its two columns for index $i$ multiplied by $c_i$. All $c_i$ are nonzero, so this diagonal column scaling preserves rank. $\blacksquare$

### Lemma 3. Counting relevant components

A graph incidence matrix has rank equal to its number of vertices minus its number of connected components. Hence at most $2b$ vertices of the incidence graph belong to nontrivial connected components: a connected component with $v\geq2$ vertices contributes rank $v-1\geq v/2$.

Every remaining nondummy vertex used by an original projection is isolated in this incidence graph. It then contains all three assignments of any original index using it. There are at most $H$ such vertices. Thus all the original numbers $a_i,b_i$ can be recovered from bad values, which are algebraic over $K$, and at most $H+2b$ auxiliary graph components.

Lemma 1 now gives

$$ 2H=\mathrm{trdeg}_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q(a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_H,b_H)\leq\kappa+H+2b. $$

Therefore $H\leq\kappa+2b\leq\kappa+8r$, as claimed.

## Ordinary moments force a bounded curve

Assume a lift with $L\leq CH$ nonzero support atoms exists. Annihilation of ordinary polynomials through degree $D$, with all residues nonzero, implies that its reduced support $\Gamma$ satisfies $\mathrm{CB}(D)$: a polynomial separating one support point would give a nonzero signed sum.

For $D\geq1$, we have $H=3D+1\leq4D$, hence $L\leq4CD$. Set

$$ s=\lceil16C\rceil+2,\qquad m=\binom{s+3}{3}, $$

and take $D\geq3(s+1)$. The curve-cover corollary in the [base-locus note](cayley_bacharach_base_locus.md#a-bounded-degree-curve-cover-at-linear-length) places $\Gamma$ on the union of the one-dimensional components of its degree $s$ base locus, with total curve degree at most $s^2$.

There are $m$ monomials of degree at most $s$ in three variables. Choose a basis of the space of equations of degree at most $s$ vanishing on $\Gamma$. It has at most $m$ elements, with at most $m$ coefficients each. The field $K$ generated by all these coefficients has transcendence degree at most $m^2$. The base locus is defined over $K$, its irreducible components are defined over its algebraic closure, and its curve components number at most $s^2$.

The preceding obstruction therefore requires

$$ 3D+1=H\leq m^2+8s^2. $$

For fixed $C$, the right side is constant. Choosing $D$ larger gives the required contradiction. $\blacksquare$

## Why fixed-score access remains possible

The four-measure requirement cannot be weakened silently to equality of one reciprocal score. In fact, for any $M$ distinct positive query values $t_1,\ldots,t_M$ and arbitrary desired real scores $y_1,\ldots,y_M$, there exist residues on $D+M+1$ distinct positive scalar poles $a_j$ such that

$$ \sum_j c_j a_j^k=0\quad(0\leq k\leq D),\qquad \sum_j\frac{c_j}{t_i+a_j}=y_i\quad(1\leq i\leq M). $$

To verify invertibility of this square Cauchy–Vandermonde system, suppose a linear combination of its rows vanishes at all poles. It is a rational function $P(a)+\sum_i\lambda_i/(t_i+a)$, with $\deg P\leq D$. Multiplication by $\prod_i(t_i+a)$ gives a polynomial of degree at most $D+M$, with $D+M+1$ distinct zeros. It is identically zero. Evaluating at $a=-t_i$ gives $\lambda_i=0$ for every $i$, and then $P=0$.

Choose an affine form with positive coefficients having distinct values on the Boolean cube, for example binary-weight slopes. The corresponding reciprocal heads have positive denominators and are admissible. Their denominator vectors lie on one affine line. The scalar moment identities imply annihilation of all ordinary direction polynomials through degree $D$ on that line.

For the present two-input example, $M=4$ and this construction uses only $D+5$ heads. Thus a constant-overhead fixed-score lift exists for these examples. For the unrestricted critical-width problem, the generic cost $D+2^n+1$ is too large. A useful access theorem must exploit the number and structure of observations needed by the target representation, or preserve only its signs. The all-kernel obstruction proves neither impossibility.
