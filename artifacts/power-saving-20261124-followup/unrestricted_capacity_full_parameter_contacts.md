# Full-parameter unit contact systems

## The one-head distinction

The counterexamples in `unrestricted_capacity_unimodular_basis.md` fix only enough unit contacts to determine the affine numerator after the denominator has been chosen. They do not contradict uniqueness of a nonsingular contact system in all parameters.

For one head, global uniqueness follows from an elementary linearization. Absorb the score bias into the affine numerator and fix the nonzero denominator constant. Write the remaining parameters as a vector $\theta$, with numerator $A\theta$ and denominator $c+B\theta$. For any prescribed contact values $f$, including unit labels, the contact equations are equivalent to

$$ \frac{(A\theta)_x}{c_x+(B\theta)_x}=f_x \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad ((A-\mathrm{diag}(f)B)\theta)_x=f_xc_x. $$

Every denominator is assumed nonzero on the contact rows. Thus all numerator and denominator parameters satisfy one affine linear system. If its coefficient map is injective, it has at most one solution, even across distinct connected components of the denominator domain.

At a contact solution, the quotient-rule differential satisfies

$$ D S_\theta(v)_x=\frac{((A-\mathrm{diag}(f)B)v)_x}{c_x+(B\theta)_x}. $$

The denominator factors only rescale rows by nonzero numbers. Therefore an injective full contact differential implies global uniqueness. In particular, a nonsingular square full-parameter unit interpolation system for one head cannot have two admissible roots.

This statement is kernel-checked in `HeadComplexity/Research/OneHeadFullContact.lean`. The declarations are `HeadComplexity.OneHeadFullContact.contact_iff`, `unique_of_injective_contactMatrix`, `differential_eq_contactMatrix`, and `unique_of_injective_differential`. The function named `differential` is the explicit quotient-rule expression; the theorem checks its algebraic identity and injectivity consequence, without adding a separate analytic differentiability theorem. All transitive axiom outputs contain only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

## A universal one-head unit deformation

There is an important limitation to the preceding uniqueness theorem. At a nonconstant interior one-head score, an injective full unit-contact differential cannot occur. Define

$$ T_t(s)=\frac{s+t}{1+ts},\qquad T_t\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)=\frac{A+tB}{B+tA}. $$

This transformation fixes both unit values. More precisely, for $f^2=1$,

$$ fT_t(s)-1=\frac{(fs-1)(1-ft)}{1+ts}. $$

For sufficiently small $|t|$, every denominator on the finite Boolean cube stays positive, $1-ft$ is positive, and strict coherence of the denominator slopes is preserved. Thus $A+tB$ and $B+tA$ give another admissible original one-head score with exactly the same unit contacts and with the full signed unit inequalities preserved. It preserves the entire truth table.

For normalized denominator constant one, let $a_0=A(0)$. Divide both new affine forms by $1+ta_0$. The resulting tangent parameter direction is

$$ \dot A=B-a_0A,\qquad \dot B=A-a_0B,\qquad \dot S=1-S^2. $$

The denominator tangent has zero constant coefficient. The direction is nonzero for a nonconstant score, and its score differential vanishes at every unit contact. This gives a direct kernel vector for every full unit-contact matrix at such a representation. It is consistent with the earlier uniqueness result: the injectivity hypothesis is not available at these interior unit solutions.

The exact transformation, unit residual identity, preservation of signed unit gaps, and change at a nonunit value are kernel-checked as `HeadComplexity.OneHeadFullContact.Moebius.transform_ratio`, `fixes_unit`, `unit_residual`, `preserves_unit_gap`, and `changes_nonunit`. The small-parameter admissibility and normalized tangent consequences above are written finite-dimensional arguments. No quantitative relative-margin improvement is claimed.

For a scalar rational score of one statistic with distinct simple real poles, small Möbius transformations also preserve the number of real poles by continuity of simple roots, and partial fractions give another score with the same head count. This observation requires that the entire numerator is a polynomial of that single statistic. Shared-pencil denominators with arbitrary affine numerators need not satisfy it.

For several genuinely multivariate heads, the transformed denominator is $Q+tP$, where $S=P/Q$ and $Q$ is the product of the affine denominators. It generally does not factor into the same number of affine factors. The cross terms in the desired tangent $1-S^2$ expose the same obstruction. Formal polynomial partial fractions would require products of head numerators to belong to ideals generated by pairs of denominator forms. This global rational-function observation alone would not rule out Boolean-cube tangent identities. The subsequent exact certificate in `unrestricted_capacity_pencil_moebius.md` does rule out the desired tangent at a particular admissible two-head score on six bits, even though its denominators share one scalar pencil. Its arbitrary affine numerators are essential to the distinction from the univariate scalar argument.

## The remaining multivariate question

For several heads, clearing the contact equations introduces products of their different affine denominators. The preceding linearization no longer applies. A possible route to the unrestricted lower bound is to select a globally feasible full-parameter contact or critical-point certificate and bound its admissible branches after quotienting head permutations. The required bound is substantially stronger than the generic polynomial-degree estimate. No such selection or branch bound has been proved here.

The hypotheses matter. A regular solution of prescribed contact equations need not satisfy the unit inequalities on all other Boolean rows. Two parameter roots with the same full truth table do not increase the truth-table count. A pair related only by head permutation also does not supply two distinct branches after the natural quotient.

## Numerical exploration

`probe_full_unit_branches.py` explores normalized original scores with coherent positive or negative denominator slopes. The first run used five Boolean variables and three heads, fixed bias one, the origin as an automatic positive unit contact, and thirty of the thirty-one remaining rows as additional unit contacts. It performed 17,422 nonlinear least-squares starts on 968 randomly generated contact-label targets in 600 seconds. No solution passed both the residual threshold and the numerical nonsingularity threshold. The result is recorded in `full-unit-branches-n5-h3.json`.

This is negative numerical evidence only. It proves neither nonexistence nor uniqueness. The contact set has thirty-one of the thirty-two cube rows, much denser than the prospective asymptotic certificates. The first implementation also encountered floating-point cancellation near negative-orientation denominator boundaries; the current script evaluates those denominators by a positive sum to avoid that cancellation. Subsequent probes allow free bias and fewer contact rows.

A separate script, `probe_grid_unit_branches.py`, restricts each of two heads to depend on the two block counts of a Boolean input. This reduces the invariant parameter system to eight variables. Entire permutation orbits of the count contacts can then be tested for full Jacobian rank in the unrestricted parameters. Checking the score on the complete count grid checks all Boolean rows of that symmetric construction. The first block-size-four run completed 40,659 starts on 4,995 sampled contact-label targets in 600 seconds, with no accepted numerically nonsingular roots. This is again only negative numerical evidence, recorded in `grid-unit-branches-block4-h2.json`.

A free-bias eight-variable, two-head run performed 13,131 starts on 1,564 sampled targets in 600 seconds, with no accepted regular roots. Its report is `full-unit-branches-n8-h2.json`. This is again a search result, not a nonexistence theorem.

A more informative local probe starts at the independently certified regular eighteen-variable, three-head unit-contact root from `origin/ar/7485202da367`. The source is `artifacts/beyond-counting-bound/unit-minimum-certificate/data.json`, SHA-256 `46c83825266d3e7971d89882a42ef3e04747d10858f07ce860cbafd85c682fc5`. The script `probe_certified_unit_branches.py` preserves the original coherent negative denominator orientation and tests the same 108 nonorigin unit contacts. Two preliminary 600-second runs, with perturbations bounded below by 0.01, did not converge to regular roots. This was a solver-basin issue: after admitting perturbations as small as $10^{-6}$ and rescaling the numerator coordinates, 788 of 1,808 starts converged to regular roots during 300 seconds. Every accepted root agreed with the baseline on the contact rows and 256 additional sampled rows within the deduplication tolerance. No distinct branch was found. The three reports are `certified-unit-branches-n18-h3.json`, `certified-unit-branches-n18-h3-scaled.json`, and `certified-unit-branches-n18-h3-local.json`.

The baseline numerical maximum contact residual was approximately $7.95\times10^{-10}$ and the smallest singular value was approximately $0.0234$. The source branch supplies an independent exact interval certificate, but these new searches use floating-point arithmetic only. They do not certify global uniqueness or a branch-count bound. The objective audited in `unrestricted_capacity_full_cube_norm.md` is the actual supremum-norm budget; the inherited local minimum concerns a different squared coefficient norm.

## Nonuniform thresholds already have regular ambiguity

Common unit contacts should be distinguished from generic row-dependent thresholds. The existing certificate in `boolean_coordinate_fiber_obstruction.md` and `boolean_coordinate_fiber_certificate.json`, commit `c8467153`, gives two admissible two-head scores on six bits that agree on twenty-five selected Boolean rows and have nonsingular full twenty-five-parameter evaluation Jacobians at both solutions. Their full truth tables differ. All sixty-four scores are nonzero, with minimum absolute value $1/60$.

For each selected row, set its threshold to the absolute common sample value. For every other row, choose a positive threshold smaller than the absolute values of both scores. Both roots then satisfy their own full signed threshold inequalities, with identical selected contact labels and different full truth tables. This is a direct deduction from the existing exact certificate, not a new numerical finding. It refutes uniqueness for globally feasible full-parameter certificates with arbitrary positive row thresholds. It does not refute a sufficiently small quantitative branch count.
