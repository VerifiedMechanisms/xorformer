# A small determining subscheme need not exist

## Exact obstruction

The reduced Cayley–Bacharach capacity argument chooses a small subset whose degree $s$ evaluations determine the full degree $s$ vanishing space. Replacing that subset by a subscheme of length equal to the degree $s$ Hilbert function is false, even for a real smoothable curvilinear scheme with a nondegenerate ordinary-moment functional.

Fix integers $D\geq s\geq1$. Put $M=\binom{s+2}{2}$, choose $m\geq M$, and put $L=m(D+1)+1$. Consider

$$ A=\mathbb R[t]/(t^L),\qquad x=t,\qquad y=t^m. $$

This defines a length $L$ subscheme $Z\subset\mathbb A^2$. Let $\Lambda:A\to\mathbb R$ extract the coefficient of $t^{L-1}$. It is nondegenerate as a multiplication functional: for every nonzero element with first nonzero term $c t^j$, multiplication by $t^{L-1-j}$ gives nonzero $\Lambda$-value.

Every monomial $x^a y^b$ with $a+b\leq D$ maps to $t^{a+mb}$, whose exponent is at most $mD\lt L-1$. Therefore

$$ \Lambda(P)=0\qquad\text{for every polynomial }P\text{ of total degree at most }D. $$

On the other hand, the degree $s$ monomials have pairwise distinct images. Indeed $a+mb=a'+mb'$ and $0\leq a,a'\leq s\lt m$ force $a=a'$ and $b=b'$. None vanishes modulo $t^L$. Thus

$$ h_Z(s)=\binom{s+2}{2}=M. $$

Every ideal in $A$ is generated by a power of $t$. The unique quotient of length $M$ is $A/(t^M)$. Since $m\geq M$, it kills $y=t^m$, although $y\neq0$ in $A$. Consequently every subscheme $Z_0\subset Z$ of length $M$ has

$$ y\in I(Z_0)_s\setminus I(Z)_s, $$

where degree at most $s$ is meant in affine coordinates. In particular, no length $h_Z(s)$ subscheme determines the degree $s$ vanishing space of $Z$.

## Smoothability and positive pole coordinates

The embedding is the plane curve $y=x^m$ together with $x^L=0$. Replacing $x^L$ by $\prod_{j=1}^L(x-\varepsilon j)$ gives $L$ distinct real points for every nonzero real $\varepsilon$, and a flat family because the defining polynomial is monic of fixed degree $L$. Thus the scheme is real-split smoothable.

Translation to pole coordinates $(1+t,1+t^m)$, with any other pole coordinates fixed at one, puts all sufficiently small positive real smoothing points in the strictly positive coherent denominator region. Translation does not change the asserted total-degree moment annihilation. This example is therefore compatible with interior admissible pole geometry. It is not merely a complex or boundary example.

For $D\asymp\exp(\sqrt n)$ and $s\asymp D/n^3$, choose $m$ of order $H/D$ at $H\asymp2^n/n^{3/2}$. Then $m\geq M$ for every sufficiently large $n$, while $L=m(D+1)+1$ is of order $H$. The obstruction persists at the length scale relevant to the benchmark.

## Scope

This does not refute choosing a basis of evaluation and derivative functionals with their own jet parameters. In particular, for a scheme supported on ordinary first-order tangent vectors, each selected functional needs only a pole and one direction. Such data may still determine the low-degree vanishing space cheaply.

It also does not refute the already-counted curvilinear cluster class: the scheme here is curvilinear. It rules out exactly the proposed replacement of a reduced determining point subset by a determining subscheme of equal Hilbert-function length. The argument is written and elementary; no Lean declaration is claimed.
