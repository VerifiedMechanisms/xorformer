# Superadditive weights for arbitrary downset jets

## Status and scope

This note proves a new finite comparison-class count in writing. Its only sign-count input is the kernel-checked theorem `HeadComplexity.weightedStrictPolynomialSignPatterns_card_le` in [WeightedPolynomialSignCount.lean](../../formalization/HeadComplexity/Polynomial/WeightedPolynomialSignCount.lean). The strong product estimate and the downset-jet specialization below are not yet Lean declarations. The grading construction was proposed by the root agent and independently checked by the margin-repair agent.

An independent explicit superadditive grading is kernel checked in [DownsetJetGrading.lean](../../formalization/HeadComplexity/Research/DownsetJetGrading.lean). Its declarations `grading_pos`, `grading_le_half`, and `grading_superadditive` use

$$ \widetilde\omega(\alpha)=\frac{|\alpha|}{2h(\alpha)},\qquad h(\alpha)=\max_{\substack{\gamma\in T\cr\gamma\geq\alpha}}|\gamma|. $$

At zero the quotient is zero. The maximum extension degree is antitone, which immediately proves superadditivity. This formal construction establishes existence of a suitable grading but does not formally establish the stronger primitive-ray product bound below.

The comparison class allows arbitrary real coefficients and arbitrary signed cancellation. Every base denominator is positive on the full Boolean cube. No claim places all original xorformer truth tables in this class. In particular, a finite local algebra need not be a quotient by a monomial ideal, and replacing it by its initial monomial ideal need not preserve the truth table.

## The grading lemma

Let $r\geq1$, and let $T\subseteq\mathbb N^r$ be a finite nonempty downset for coordinatewise order. Write $q=|T|$, so $0\in T$. There is a rational function $\omega:T\to\mathbb Q$ satisfying

$$ \omega(0)=0,\qquad 0\lt\omega(\alpha)\leq\frac12\quad(\alpha\neq0),\qquad \omega(\alpha+\beta)\geq\omega(\alpha)+\omega(\beta)\quad(\alpha+\beta\in T), $$

and

$$ \prod_{\alpha\in T\setminus\lbrace0\rbrace}\omega(\alpha)(1-\omega(\alpha))\geq(16r)^{-(q-1)}. $$

### Construction and superadditivity

For nonzero $\alpha\in T$, define the positive integer

$$ M_\alpha=\max\lbrace k\geq1:k\alpha\in T\rbrace,\qquad \eta_\alpha=\frac1{2r(M_\alpha+1)}. $$

Let $\omega(\alpha)$ be the maximum of $\sum_j\eta_{\beta_j}$ over all ordered partitions $\alpha=\sum_j\beta_j$ into nonzero vectors. Each part is coordinatewise at most $\alpha$, hence belongs to $T$. The partition length is at most $|\alpha|=\sum_i\alpha_i$, and there are finitely many such partitions. Thus the maximum exists and is rational. Set $\omega(0)=0$.

The singleton partition gives $\omega(\alpha)\geq\eta_\alpha\gt0$. Concatenating maximizing partitions proves superadditivity whenever their sum belongs to $T$.

Fix a partition of nonzero $\alpha\in T$. For every nonzero part $\beta\leq\alpha$, coordinatewise downward closure gives

$$ M_\beta\geq\left\lfloor\min_{i:\beta_i\gt0}\frac{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}\right\rfloor,\qquad \frac1{M_\beta+1}\leq\max_{i:\beta_i\gt0}\frac{\beta_i}{\alpha_i}\leq\sum_{i:\alpha_i\gt0}\frac{\beta_i}{\alpha_i}. $$

All denominators in the last sum are positive. Summing over the partition yields

$$ \sum_j\eta_{\beta_j}\leq\frac1{2r}\sum_{i:\alpha_i\gt0}\frac{\sum_j(\beta_j)_i}{\alpha_i}=\frac{|\mathrm{supp}(\alpha)|}{2r}\leq\frac12. $$

Taking the maximum proves the claimed upper bound on $\omega$.

### Product bound along primitive rays

Partition $T\setminus\lbrace0\rbrace$ by primitive integer rays. On a ray generated by a vector $v$ whose coordinates have greatest common divisor one, downward closure gives exactly the points $v,2v,\ldots,Lv$ for some $L\geq1$. For $1\leq j\leq L$, one has $M_{jv}=\lfloor L/j\rfloor$. Therefore

$$ \prod_{j=1}^L(M_{jv}+1)\leq\prod_{j=1}^L\frac{L+j}{j}=\binom{2L}{L}\leq4^L. $$

Multiplication over the rays gives $\prod_{\alpha\neq0}(M_\alpha+1)\leq4^{q-1}$. Using $\omega(\alpha)\geq\eta_\alpha$ and $1-\omega(\alpha)\geq1/2$ now gives

$$ \prod_{\alpha\neq0}\omega(\alpha)(1-\omega(\alpha))\geq\prod_{\alpha\neq0}\frac1{4r(M_\alpha+1)}\geq(16r)^{-(q-1)}. $$

The empty product covers $q=1$. $\blacksquare$

## Reciprocal jets over downsets

Put $N=2^n$ and $d=n+1$. Fix downsets $T_1,\ldots,T_G\subseteq\mathbb N^r$, with positive sizes $q_g=|T_g|$ and total size $K=\sum_gq_g$. At cluster $g$, choose arbitrary real affine forms $B_{g,\alpha}$ and $L_{g,\alpha}$ for $\alpha\in T_g$, requiring only $B_{g,0}(x)\gt0$ at every Boolean vertex.

Define reciprocal coefficients by formal multiplication modulo the monomial ideal complementary to the downset:

$$ R_{g,0}=B_{g,0}^{-1},\qquad R_{g,\alpha}=-B_{g,0}^{-1}\sum_{\substack{0\neq\beta\leq\alpha}}B_{g,\beta}R_{g,\alpha-\beta}. $$

All indices in this formula lie in $T_g$ by downward closure. Consider every strict score of the form

$$ S(x)=c+\sum_{g=1}^G\sum_{\alpha\in T_g}L_{g,\alpha}(x)R_{g,\alpha}(x),\qquad S(x)\neq0\text{ on the full cube}. $$

There are $p=1+2dK$ real parameters. If $p\leq N$, the number of strict truth tables in this class is at most

$$ \left(\frac{8eN}{p}\right)^p K^{dG}(16r)^{d(K-G)}\leq\left(\frac{32e\sqrt r N}{p}\right)^p K^{dG}. $$

### Positive clearing and weighted degrees

Let $m_g=1+\max_{\alpha\in T_g}|\alpha|$. A path from $0$ to a maximal-degree index supplies $m_g$ distinct points of $T_g$, so $m_g\leq q_g$. Set $M=\sum_gm_g\leq K$ and multiply the score by the positive quantity $Q=\prod_gB_{g,0}^{m_g}$.

Every monomial contributing to $B_{g,0}^{m_g}R_{g,\alpha}$ has the form

$$ (-1)^\ell B_{g,0}^{m_g-1-\ell}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}B_{g,\beta_j},\qquad \beta_j\neq0,\qquad\sum_j\beta_j=\alpha. $$

The exponent is nonnegative because $\ell\leq|\alpha|\leq m_g-1$. Choose the preceding grading $\omega_g$ on each downset. Give every affine coefficient of the indicated form the following rational weight:

$$ w(B_{g,0})=\frac1K,\qquad w(B_{g,\alpha})=\omega_g(\alpha)+\frac1K\quad(\alpha\neq0),\qquad w(L_{g,\alpha})=1-\omega_g(\alpha),\qquad w(c)=1. $$

All weights are positive. Superadditivity gives $\sum_j\omega_g(\beta_j)\leq\omega_g(\alpha)$. Hence the corresponding term of the globally cleared score has weight at most

$$ 1-\omega_g(\alpha)+\sum_j\omega_g(\beta_j)+\frac{M-1}{K}\leq2. $$

The bias term has weight $1+M/K\leq2$. Evaluation at a Boolean vertex preserves these bounds. Multiply every rational weight and the degree bound by a common positive integer to apply the kernel-checked integer-weight sign theorem.

The product of all parameter weights is at least

$$ \prod_{i=1}^p w_i\geq K^{-dG}(16r)^{-d(K-G)}. $$

Indeed, the zero index contributes $1/K$ per affine coefficient position, and each nonzero index contributes at least $\omega_g(\alpha)(1-\omega_g(\alpha))$. The weighted sign theorem with weighted degree two proves the first displayed count. The second follows from $d(K-G)\leq(p-1)/2$.

## Union over downsets and the target widths

There are at most $(4r)^{q-1}$ downsets of size $q$ in $\mathbb N^r$. To see this, give every nonzero index its parent by decrementing its first nonzero coordinate. The resulting rooted tree spans the downset, and its edges carry one of $r$ coordinate labels. Sorting children by coordinate gives an ordered rooted tree, whose traversal and edge labels reconstruct the embedded downset. There are at most $4^{q-1}$ ordered rooted trees with $q$ vertices and $r^{q-1}$ edge labelings.

Allow arbitrary ordered lists of downsets with total size at most $K_{\max}$, in a fixed ambient dimension $1\leq r\leq n+1$, with every size at least $q_{\min}$. The number of such charts, including the empty list, is at most $2(8r)^{K_{\max}}$. Padding with unused coordinates includes every smaller ambient dimension.

For every sufficiently large $n$, either of the following two assumptions makes the union of all the strict truth tables in these charts have size at most $2^{N/2}$:

1. $K_{\max}\leq N/(1000n\sqrt n)$ and $q_{\min}\geq\sqrt n$.

2. $K_{\max}\leq N\log n/(1000n^2)$ and $q_{\min}\geq\log n$.

Here and below logarithms are natural. The constants charge the complete total size $K$, including every cluster, and permit no free width overhead.

**Estimate.** In either case, $p\leq5nK$ and eventually $p/N\leq1/(200\sqrt n)$. Since $r\leq n+1$ and the function $u\log(A/u)$ is increasing on the relevant range, the logarithm of the first factor is at most

$$ \frac{N}{200\sqrt n}\log(6400e\sqrt2 n)=o(N). $$

The base penalty satisfies $G\leq K/q_{\min}$ and

$$ dG\log K\leq\frac{2nK}{q_{\min}}n\log2\leq\frac{N\log2}{500}. $$

Finally the chart entropy is at most $\log2+K_{\max}\log(8(n+1))=o(N)$ in both cases. The total logarithm is thus at most $N\log2/500+o(N)$, which is strictly less than $N\log2/2$ for every sufficiently large $n$. Constants and the empty chart add only two tables and are absorbed by the same eventual strict inequality. $\blacksquare$

## The remaining access problem

This removes the need for every favorable cluster to be a one-parameter jet. Arbitrary monomial downsets with many tangent directions have the same useful entropy bound, up to a factor polynomial in the ambient jet dimension.

It does not establish that a general collision of original heads is represented by a downset jet with comparable total size. A general finite local algebra has nonmonomial multiplication relations. Its inverse-system polynomial can have many monomials even when its algebra has small length. Passing to a monomial initial ideal changes this functional and may change every label. Such a degeneration needs an independent truth-table preservation argument.

There is a second, independent access requirement. Even a complete count of all favorable collision limits would not by itself show that every original-width truth table lacking a good-margin representation admits such a limit. The quantifier remains over every representation at the permitted comparison width. Neither a small margin in one representation nor an exact zero relation among some heads supplies that conclusion.
