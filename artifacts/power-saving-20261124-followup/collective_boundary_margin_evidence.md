# Collective denominator boundary moves with full-cube margin control

## Status

This is finite numerical exploration with exact rational verification of successful witnesses. It proves neither global optimality nor a universal repair theorem. The full-cube margin convention, original admissibility, and failed-search quantifiers are retained throughout.

The experiment complements the exact obstruction in `frozen_head_parity_margin.md`: it moves all denominator coefficients simultaneously and refits every numerator and the bias. The numerical objective is the minimum signed score subject to the bias plus the sum of full-cube head supremum norms being at most one.

## Method

For fixed coherent denominator coefficients, the objective is a linear program. Its variables are arbitrary affine numerator coefficients, the bias, individual head norm bounds, and a common signed gap. Every Boolean input enters both the gap and norm inequalities. The code then uses the dual envelope derivative to optimize all denominator coefficients jointly on a product of simplices.

Both orientations are allowed separately for each head. Boundary slopes are permitted to vanish in this exploratory comparison class. A strictly positive full-cube gap at such a point survives adding sufficiently small positive coefficients to all slopes, which restores genuine original admissibility. The constant coefficient is constrained to stay positive.

The search uses SLSQP, has no global convergence guarantee, and may stop at a local nonsmooth optimum. A returned zero margin is an unresolved search, not an infeasibility certificate. The denominator support is not fixed across all targets.

## Results

The inputs are the previously recorded joint-margin targets. The dimension-five set has 40 targets originally generated by two admissible heads. The dimension-six set has 24 random Boolean targets for which the previous search found four-head original representations.

| Search | Targets | Strict witnesses | Minimum exact certified margin |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Dimension five, two heads, free boundary search | 40 | 40 | 0.173133 |
| Dimension six, four heads, free boundary search | 24 | 24 | 0.008072 |
| Dimension five, two heads, two slopes forced zero in each head | 40 | 40 | 0.157026 |
| Dimension six, four heads, three slopes forced zero in each head | 24 | 22 | 0.000404 |

The forced-face searches use twelve restarts and at most 150 optimizer iterations per restart. The free searches use three restarts and at most 100 iterations. For the free search, the reported numerical margin improves over the previous witness in 29 of the 40 dimension-five cases and 20 of the 24 dimension-six cases. At least one slope in every head vanishes in the best returned free-search witness for 23 and 8 cases, respectively.

The forced dimension-six searches for targets with indices 7 and 21 did not find positive gaps. Nothing here proves that those targets lack such representations. A separate numerical linear-program check found candidate degree-three polynomial separators for both targets; that auxiliary check was not converted into an exact certificate.

## Exact verification

`check_collective_boundary_exact.py` rounds every successful coefficient to a rational with denominator $10^{10}$. It checks all cube inputs twice: first with the exact zero slopes, then after adding $10^{-10}$ to every slope. Both checks retain strictly positive denominators and the entire truth table. The latter check has all slopes strictly positive in the head's chosen orientation, so the positive-affine-ratio constructor and global reflection give genuine original heads.

The checker centers each head by its exact full-cube midrange and moves the removed constants to the bias. It computes the exact signed gap and amplitude using rational arithmetic and certifies the lower margins in the table. In total, 126 successful certificates pass both checks. These are finite Python rational computations, not Lean kernel declarations.

The original floating-point optimization can slightly overestimate the margin after rational rounding and admissibility perturbation. The table uses the smaller exact rational certificates, rounded downward to millionths.

## What the evidence does not establish

A repair in which every denominator depends on very few coordinates is not a viable universal conclusion. If every denominator depends on at most $q$ coordinates, its reciprocal has a Boolean multilinear representation of degree at most $q$. Multiplying by an affine numerator gives degree at most $q+1$, and summing any number of heads preserves that degree bound. Thus this entire comparison class has threshold degree at most $q+1$.

Parity on $n$ coordinates has threshold degree $n$, but has an original representation with polynomially many heads and can be padded to either benchmark width. Hence a claim forcing all replacement denominators to depend on $O(\log n)$ coordinates fails even at exponentially large padded widths. A useful boundary-access theorem would need a separately controlled dense-direction remainder or a different comparison geometry.

The simultaneous experiments provide positive evidence that changing denominators jointly can improve conditioning and reach many boundary faces. They do not bound the number of truth tables that resist such moves, and they do not supply either requested unrestricted lower bound.
